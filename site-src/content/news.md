<div class="stamp stamp--no">SBND detector paper</div>
<p>The SBND Collaboration has posted a paper describing the as-built detector and its first year of beam operation. SBND is a 112 tonne active-mass liquid argon time projection chamber in the Booster Neutrino Beam at Fermilab and part of the Short-Baseline Neutrino Program. It began operation in 2024, and the authors report all detector systems at or above design requirements. The electron lifetime exceeded 30 ms, ten times the design requirement, for more than 95% of Run 1.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">PandaX-4T supernova background</div>
<p>PandaX-4T reports the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background through coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. The analysis uses a total exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs. No significant event excess over the expected background was observed. The collaboration sets an upper limit of (2.27 - 2.43) × 10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 per flavour at 90% confidence level on the ν_x flux, for neutrino energies above 19.3 MeV.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">Combined atmospheric fit</div>
<p>A new preprint combines the latest publicly available atmospheric data from Super-Kamiokande (stages before the gadolinium phase), IceCube-DeepCore and the six-detection-unit configuration of KM3NeT/ORCA. The fit uses all event samples at once, treats shared systematic uncertainties as fully correlated and models detector-response uncertainties separately for each experiment. The authors present the result as evidence for normal neutrino ordering.</p>
<div class="cites"><span class="cite">Evidence for Normal Neutrino Ordering with Combined Atmospheric Neutrino Experiments — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12208">arXiv</a></span></div>

<p>A single-author preprint states that the JUNO Collaboration has reported the most precise measurements to date of both Δm²_21 and Δm²_31. This is the author's own analysis of JUNO's reported values, not a JUNO result.</p>
