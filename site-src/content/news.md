<div class="stamp stamp--no">Super-Kamiokande reactor antineutrinos</div>
<p>Super-Kamiokande reported the first observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors, using SK-Gd data with a total exposure of 411.52 live days. The prompt energy spectra and monthly event rates are consistent with the oscillated reactor antineutrino prediction after accounting for accidental, geoneutrino and spallation backgrounds. The no-reactor hypothesis is disfavoured, with a signal significance of approximately 6σ.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">PandaX-4T supernova background</div>
<p>PandaX-4T presented the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background using coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. With a total exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs, it observed no significant event excess over the expected background. It set an upper limit of (2.27–2.43)×10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 per flavour at 90% confidence level on the ν_x flux, for incident energies above 19.3 MeV.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">MicroBooNE cross sections</div>
<p>MicroBooNE reported the first double-differential cross-section measurements of muon-neutrino charged-current interactions on argon with no mesons and one or more protons in the final state. The measurements use the full Booster Neutrino Beam dataset, corresponding to 1.30×10^21 protons on target. They are given as a function of proton multiplicity and kinematics, up to and including the fourth-leading proton.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">Baikal-GVD point sources</div>
<p>Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. A search for point-like astrophysical sources used a five-year track-like event dataset collected in 2019–2024. No significant source was found, so upper limits were computed for a selection of candidate objects and compared with published IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT results.</p>
