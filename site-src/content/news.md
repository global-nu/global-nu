<div class="stamp stamp--no">XENONnT Solar Neutrinos</div>
<p>The XENONnT dark-matter experiment reported the first measurement of low-energy solar neutrinos via elastic neutrino-electron scattering at the keV scale, using 2.46 tonne-year exposure from its first two science runs. The analysis rejected the background-only hypothesis at 5.0 sigma and measured a solar pp neutrino flux of (10.2 ± 2.0) × 10^10 cm^-2 s^-1, reaching the lowest energy threshold achieved for any neutrino detection to date.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">Super-Kamiokande Proton Decay</div>
<p>Using the full 401 kiloton-year pure-water-phase exposure, Super-Kamiokande searched for proton decay into a charged antilepton plus an invisible massless particle and found no significant signal beyond the expected atmospheric neutrino background. The collaboration set 90% confidence lower limits on the partial proton lifetime of 1.72×10^33 years for the positron channel and 0.61×10^33 years for the muon channel, improving on previous limits by factors of about 2 and 1.5 respectively.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">FASER and SND@LHC</div>
<p>Proton-proton collisions at the LHC produce a large flux of high-energy, far-forward neutrinos reaching TeV-scale energies, opening an experimental window beyond the reach of traditional accelerator-based neutrino experiments. The FASER and SND@LHC experiments have reported the first direct observations of these collider neutrinos.</p>
<div class="cites"><span class="cite">Neutrino Experiments at the LHC and the HL-LHC — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00842">arXiv</a></span></div>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">ARA Diffuse Flux Search</div>
<p>The Askaryan Radio Array at the South Pole has carried out its first array-wide diffuse flux search for ultra-high-energy neutrinos, using data from its five autonomous stations collected between January 2013 and December 2023. The analysis incorporates improved detector characterisation and simulation, including data-driven noise and electronics models and revised neutrino interaction modelling.</p>

<p>IceCube has searched for high-energy neutrino emission coincident with 89 tidal disruption events selected from optical/UV and X-ray observations. The analysis used time-dependent likelihood methods to look for emission from individual sources as well as from the population as a whole.</p>
