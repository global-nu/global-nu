<div class="stamp stamp--no">XENONnT solar neutrinos</div>
<p>XENONnT reported the first detection of solar pp neutrinos via elastic neutrino-electron scattering in a multi-tonne liquid xenon dark matter detector, using an exposure of 2.46 tonne-years and reaching sensitivity down to 17 keV recoil energy, the lowest energy threshold achieved for any neutrino measurement. The collaboration rejected the background-only hypothesis at 5.0 sigma and measured a pp neutrino flux of (10.2 ± 2.0) × 10^10 cm^-2 s^-1. A subsequent comment from outside authors flagged what they describe as an important omission in the collaboration's summary plot.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">Super-Kamiokande proton decay</div>
<p>Using the full pure-water phase of Super-Kamiokande, a 401 kiloton-year exposure, a search for proton decay via p to l+ plus an invisible massless particle found no significant signal above the expected atmospheric-neutrino background. The analysis set 90% confidence-level lower limits on the partial proton lifetime of 1.72×10^33 years for the positron channel and 0.61×10^33 years for the muon channel, improving on previous limits by factors of 2 and 1.5 respectively.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">IceCube extra dimensions</div>
<p>IceCube placed new constraints on low-scale-gravity scenarios that address the hierarchy problem, using 10.7 years of upward-going muon-neutrino data spanning 0.5 to 100 TeV. Such scenarios predict characteristic spectral distortions from matter effects as neutrinos cross the Earth; none were observed, letting the collaboration constrain the compactification scale of large extra dimension models.</p>

<p>KM3NeT/ARCA carried out a stacking search for high-energy neutrino emission from three candidate source populations: ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and two blazar subclasses (high-synchrotron-peaked BL Lacs and extreme blazars).
