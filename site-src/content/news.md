<div class="stamp stamp--no">JUNO oscillation fit</div>
<p>The collaboration reports an improved measurement of oscillation parameters from 207.2 live days of data, comprising 8,294 inverse beta-decay candidates at an average reactor baseline of 52.5 km. The precision on the atmospheric splitting Δm²31 is approximately 1%, described as world-leading. The fit gives Δm²31 = +2.509 (+0.027/−0.025) ×10⁻³ eV² for normal ordering and −2.482 (+0.025/−0.026) ×10⁻³ eV² for inverted ordering, with the Daya Bay constraint used in the fit, and the paper presents this as an indication on the mass ordering.</p>

<p>The collaboration presents the first double-differential cross-section measurements of muon-neutrino charged-current interactions on argon with no mesons and one or more protons. <div class="stamp stamp--no">MINERvA superscaling</div>
<p>The collaboration reports the first measurement of the superscaling variable ψ′ built from visible final-state kinematics. It uses charged-current quasielastic-like muon-neutrino interactions on carbon in the medium-energy NuMI beam. The variable is used as an externally calibrated quantity whose peak position is sensitive to the invisible nuclear missing energy.</p>

<div class="section-head"><h2>Theory highlights</h2><p>recently published, with the links each record carries</p></div>
<ul class="list list--news">
<li><p>This paper presents what it calls the first combined oscillation analysis of recent atmospheric datasets from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA, together with Daya Bay reactor data. A single physics model describes all datasets with no significant parameter tensions, and the fit prefers normal over inverted ordering at 3σ. It matters because such combinations had long been considered infeasible outside the collaborations.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09714">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3166344">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/v4hl-pbpn">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The authors combine current neutrinoless double-beta decay results with nuclear matrix elements from ab initio calculations based on chiral effective field theory, in a Bayesian framework. They conclude that, in contrast to nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate that the current generation of experiments has likely not yet reached the required sensitivities. This bears directly on how far present searches can be read as probes of Majorana neutrino mass.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09288">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3166340">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/rsw5-57fz">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The paper calculates two-neutrino double-beta decay rates without decoupling nuclear structure from the lepton kinematics. It compares the resulting rates and electron spectra with closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion approximations for ⁸²Se and ⁸⁸Xe-type cases, specifically ⁸²Se and ¹³⁶Xe. This is relevant to anyone modelling the two-neutrino spectrum as a background or a benchmark in double-beta searches.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31107">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3174998">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-026-16356-x">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The authors ask whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected as nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals. They vary neutron-star masses, include failed supernovae that form black holes, and consider the role of the nuclear equation of state. They find that sensitivity is enhanced for models with larger neutrino emission.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26826">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3184513">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/gys2-q6bk">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The paper studies thermal leptogenesis in a model with a scalar quadruplet and two vectorlike fermion triplets. Neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop, and sub-eV masses are accommodated even for a new-physics scale of O(TeV). The authors argue this makes the model a target for experimental searches and examine leptogenesis in a setting that differs from canonical seesaw leptogenesis.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01890">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3186500">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/lj7k-5c1q">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The paper considers magnetorotational supernova neutrino spectra, including oscillation effects in the outer layers of the star. It finds that the hardness of the electron antineutrino spectrum increases significantly. The enhancement is most pronounced for the inverted mass ordering, which the authors say favours ground-based detection.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3207686">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1134/S1063779626701182">DOI</a></span></span></li>
</ul>
