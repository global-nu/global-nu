<div class="stamp stamp--no">IceCube extra dimensions search</div>
<p>IceCube has been examining its data for possible signatures of extra spatial dimensions, one candidate explanation for the hierarchy problem — the puzzle of why the weak force is so many orders of magnitude stronger than gravity. <div class="stamp stamp--no">NOvA sterile search</div>
<p>NOvA has carried out its first sterile-neutrino search to combine neutrino- and antineutrino-mode beam data, fitting its near and far detectors simultaneously within a three-active-plus-one-sterile model, with exposures of 26.61×10^20 and 12.50×10^20 protons on target respectively. The analysis finds no evidence for sterile neutrinos and excludes parameter space previously allowed by other experiments, including most of the region allowed by IceCube.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">NOvA neutron modelling</div>
<p>Using its high-statistics near-detector antineutrino sample, NOvA has examined how well simulations reproduce the low-energy neutron candidates that complicate energy reconstruction in neutrino interactions. The study reports an excess of simulated neutron candidates with low energy depositions relative to data, pointing to model dependence in neutron-related systematic uncertainties.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">KM3NeT source stacking</div>
<p>KM3NeT/ARCA has carried out a stacking search for high-energy astrophysical neutrinos from two candidate source populations, ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and blazars, using 336 days of data from an array of 19-21 detection units and a sample of 1544 track-like neutrino candidates. The ultra-luminous infrared galaxy sample comprised 75 sources, modelled with conventional power-law spectra.</p>

<li><p>TAMBO, a mountain-based, deep-valley neutrino observatory, is presented as a way to help complete the still-incomplete map of the high-energy neutrino sky after a decade of progress in neutrino astronomy. The authors frame the instrument as a possible route to clues of new physics as well.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3185371">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-02916-4">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>Combining likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio in the in-medium similarity renormalization group approach, the authors derive global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, their ab initio results indicate that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass region of interest.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09288">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3166340">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/rsw5-57fz">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The paper assesses whether nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals — paleodetectors — could register neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae, accounting for the diversity of outcomes from neutron-star-forming collapses to failed supernovae that form black holes. The analytical and numerical results agree, giving a quick way to bound plausible neutrino output from blazar sources.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">On the Maximum Neutrino Flux of Blazars in the One-zone Leptohadronic Model — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24107">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3159328">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ae734a">DOI</a></span></span></li>
