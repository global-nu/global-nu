<div class="stamp stamp--no">NOvA sterile search</div>
<p>NOvA has completed its first search for oscillations to a sterile neutrino that combines both neutrino- and antineutrino-mode beam data, fitting the Near and Far Detectors simultaneously with exposures of 26.61x10^20 and 12.50x10^20 protons on target in the two modes. The collaboration found no evidence for sterile neutrinos and excluded regions of parameter space allowed by previous experiments, including most of the region reported by IceCube.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">IceCube and the KM3NeT event</div>
<p>IceCube searched fifteen years of its data for high-energy neutrino emission from the direction of the roughly 200 PeV event recorded by the KM3NeT ARCA detector in February 2023, the highest-energy neutrino yet reported. Testing steady, flaring and unconstrained-time emission hypotheses, the collaboration found no evidence of emission from that direction and set upper limits accordingly.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">PandaX-4T quadruple beta decay</div>
<p>Using commissioning and first science-run data corresponding to a 136Xe exposure of 148.4 kg-yr, PandaX-4T searched for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay, a process whose observation without neutrinoless double beta decay would point to Dirac rather than Majorana neutrinos. No excess over background was seen, and the collaboration set a lower limit on the half-life of 6.01x10^24 years at 90% confidence, the most stringent constraint on this decay in xenon to date.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">NOvA detector systematics</div>
<p>NOvA has reported results from its dedicated Test Beam experiment, which used a 30-ton segmented liquid-scintillator detector functionally identical to the Near and Far Detectors to characterise detector response with tagged particles from proton-copper collisions at the Fermilab Test Beam Facility between 2019 and 2022. A companion study of neutron detector response in NOvA's high-statistics antineutrino near-detector sample found an excess of simulated low-energy neutron candidates relative to data, a source of model-dependent systematic uncertainty in energy reconstruction.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">JUNO information limits</div>
<p>A new study builds an 'information ladder' for JUNO, using quantum and classical Fisher information to track how precisely reactor antineutrino oscillation parameters can be recovered as information passes from the neutrino state through to the events actually observed in the detector. The authors find that the resulting loss of precision is strongly dependent on which oscillation parameter is being measured.</p>

<li><p>Eller presents the first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1,536 bins with 91 parameters. The unified model describes all datasets with no significant tensions and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3-sigma significance, a combination long considered infeasible outside the individual collaborations.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09714">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3166344">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/v4hl-pbpn">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>Shickele, Jokiniemi, Belley and collaborators combine likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed from ab initio nuclear theory to derive global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. Unlike phenomenological nuclear models, their ab initio results indicate that current-generation 0-nu-beta-beta experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass regime of interest.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09288">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3166340">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/rsw5-57fz">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>Argueelles, Bazo, Bricenio and collaborators describe TAMBO, a proposed mountain-based observatory that would detect tau neutrinos via air showers in a deep valley, aimed at mapping the high-energy neutrino sky in an energy range that has so far been hard to access and potentially revealing signs of new physics.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3185371">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-02916-4">DOI</a></span></span></li>
