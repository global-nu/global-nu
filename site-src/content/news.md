<div class="stamp stamp--no">IceCube extra dimensions</div>
<p>A dedicated analysis of 10.7 years of upward-going muon neutrino data, covering energies from 0.5 to 100 TeV, was used to constrain low-scale gravity scenarios put forward to address the hierarchy problem. The predicted spectral distortions from matter effects as neutrinos cross the Earth were used to place constraints on large extra dimension models. A companion IceCube blog post explains the motivation behind this line of search.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">XENONnT solar neutrinos</div>
<p>Using data from its first two science runs, corresponding to an exposure of 2.46 tonne-years, XENONnT reported the first measurement of solar neutrinos through elastic neutrino-electron scattering, with sensitivity down to 17 keV, the lowest energy threshold achieved for any neutrino detection to date. The background-only hypothesis was rejected at 5.0σ, and the collaboration measured a pp solar neutrino flux of (10.2 ± 2.0) × 10^10 cm⁻² s⁻¹.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">IceCube tidal disruption search</div>
<p>IceCube searched for high-energy neutrino emission coincident with 89 tidal disruption events selected from optical/UV and X-ray observations, using time-dependent likelihood methods to probe both individual sources and a stacked population. Tidal disruption events are theorized to produce neutrinos through photohadronic interactions between accelerated protons and photons in the accretion disc and outflows.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">Forward LHC neutrinos</div>
<p>A review describes the large flux of far-forward neutrinos, reaching TeV energies, produced in proton-proton collisions at the LHC near the beam axis. The new FASER and SND@LHC experiments have reported the first direct observations of these collider neutrinos, opening an experimental window beyond the reach of traditional accelerator-based neutrino experiments.</p>
<div class="cites"><span class="cite">Neutrino Experiments at the LHC and the HL-LHC — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00842">arXiv</a></span></div>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">Super-Kamiokande proton decay</div>
<p>Using the full 401 kiloton-year exposure of its pure-water phase, Super-Kamiokande searched for proton decay into a charged antilepton plus an invisible, massless particle. No significant signal was found above the expected atmospheric neutrino background, and the collaboration set new lower limits on the proton partial lifetime, improving on previous limits by factors of 2 and 1.5 for the electron and muon channels respectively.</p>
