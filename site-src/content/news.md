<div class="stamp stamp--no">JUNO oscillation update</div>
<p>JUNO reported an improved measurement of the oscillation parameters using 207.2 live days of data, with 8,294 inverse beta-decay candidates at an average reactor baseline of 52.5 km. The precision on the atmospheric splitting Δm²31 is approximately 1%, described as world-leading. The fit gives Δm²31 = +2.509 (+0.027/−0.025) ×10⁻³ eV² for normal ordering and −2.482 (+0.025/−0.026) ×10⁻³ eV² for inverted ordering, using the Daya Bay constraint, and the paper presents this as an indication of the mass ordering.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">MicroBooNE proton cross sections</div>
<p>MicroBooNE presented the first double-differential cross-section measurements for muon-neutrino charged-current interactions on argon with no mesons and one or more protons in the final state. The analysis uses the full Booster Neutrino Beam dataset, corresponding to 1.30×10²¹ protons on target. Cross sections are given as functions of proton multiplicity and proton kinematics, up to the fourth-leading proton.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">MINERvA superscaling</div>
<p>MINERvA reported the first measurement of the superscaling variable in neutrino–nucleus scattering, using charged-current quasielastic-like muon-neutrino interactions on carbon in the medium-energy NuMI beam. The variable is reconstructed from visible final-state kinematics. It is used as an externally calibrated quantity whose peak position is sensitive to the invisible nuclear missing energy.</p>

<div class="stamp stamp--no">Baikal-GVD point sources</div>
<p>Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. <ul class="list list--news">
<li><p>This paper presents a combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino datasets from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA, together with Daya Bay reactor data. It fits 839 048 events in 1536 bins with 91 parameters and finds no significant parameter tensions. The combination prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ, and it shows that such a combination can be done outside the experimental collaborations.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09714">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3166344">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/v4hl-pbpn">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The authors combine results from neutrinoless double-beta decay searches with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory. They derive global Majorana-mass limits in a Bayesian framework. Unlike nuclear-model calculations, the ab initio results indicate that the current generation of experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the neutrino mass range of interest, which bears on how discovery reach is quoted.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09288">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3166340">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/rsw5-57fz">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The paper calculates two-neutrino double-beta decay rates without decoupling nuclear structure from the lepton kinematics, which earlier closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion treatments relied on. It reports deviations in the rates and electron spectra for ⁸²Se and ⁸⁶Xe-adjacent case ¹³⁶Xe relative to those methods. The 2νββ spectrum is the main background in neutrinoless searches, so a more realistic description matters to those experiments.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31107">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3174998">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-026-16356-x">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>The study asks whether nuclear-recoil tracks in ancient minerals could record neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae. It varies neutron-star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the role of the nuclear equation of state. The sensitivity is quantified as enhanced for models with larger neutrino emission, which links a geological detection idea to supernova physics.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26826">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3184513">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/gys2-q6bk">DOI</a></span></span></li>
<li><p>This paper considers magnetorotational supernovae and how neutrino spectra depend on the mass ordering. It finds that oscillations in the outer stellar layers harden the electron-antineutrino spectrum significantly. The enhancement is most pronounced for the inverted ordering and is described as favourable for ground-based detection, so a future Galactic burst could carry ordering information.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3207686">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1134/S1063779626701182">DOI</a></span></span></li>

This yields transition dipole couplings of order 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁸ GeV⁻¹, parametrically larger than in minimal Dirac or Majorana models. They carry out an event-level simulation of production and decay, relevant to anyone looking for radiative signatures of heavy neutral leptons.</p><span class="cites"><span class="cite">Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20987">arXiv</a> · <a href="https://inspirehep.net/literature/3170873">INSPIRE</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP09(2026)234">DOI</a></span></span></li>
