<div class="prose" style="margin-top:1.2rem">
<p>Both files hold exactly the same rows and the same columns — CSV for a
spreadsheet or a quick <code>pandas.read_csv</code>, JSON for a script. The
URLs are stable: a regenerated release adds or corrects rows in place, it does
not move the file. Regenerated by <code>tools/make_history_data.py</code> from
<code>site-src/data/history.yaml</code>, the same source the parameter-history
page itself is drawn from — the two can never disagree, because nothing is
retyped between them.</p>
</div>

<div class="section-head">
  <h2>The two value columns</h2>
  <p>what the paper printed, and our arithmetic — never merged into one</p>
</div>

<div class="prose">
<p>Every row carries the value <strong>twice</strong>, under names that cannot
be confused:</p>

<ul>
<li><code>value_as_published</code> — exactly what the paper printed, in the
paper's own convention and normalisation (see <code>convention</code> and
<code>unit</code>, below).</li>
<li><code>value_our_convention</code> — the same quantity converted to this
group's convention: δm² = m₂² − m₁² &gt; 0 and Δm² = m₃² − (m₁² + m₂²)/2.</li>
</ul>

<p>Only the <code>Dm2</code> parameter is ever converted. NuFit reports Δm²₃ℓ
(Δm²₃₁ &gt; 0 for normal ordering, Δm²₃₂ &lt; 0 for inverted) and Valencia
reports |Δm²₃₁| for <em>both</em> orderings — neither is our Δm², and the sign
of the correction between the two is not even the same for the two groups
(worked through on the <a href="history.html#compare">parameter-history
page</a>). The conversion is performed by a single function,
<code>to_our_Dm2(rel, ordering, value)</code> in
<code>tools/make_history.py</code>, and nowhere else — read it there rather
than re-deriving the identity from this page.</p>

<p>For every other parameter (<code>dm2</code>, <code>sin2_th12</code>,
<code>sin2_th13</code>, <code>sin2_th23</code>, <code>delta_pi</code>) — and
for Bari's own <code>Dm2</code>, which already <em>is</em> our convention —
the two columns hold the identical number. That repetition is deliberate, not
a column to optimise away: it means a downloader gets one column,
<code>value_our_convention</code>, that is directly comparable across all six
parameters and all three groups, without first having to know which
parameter, in which release, needed the conversion.</p>
</div>

<div class="section-head">
  <h2>Fields</h2>
  <p>13 columns, one row per (group, year, parameter, ordering) point</p>
</div>

<div class="table-scroll">
<table class="data">
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Field</th><th scope="col">Meaning</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><code>group</code></td><td><code>bari</code>, <code>nufit</code> or <code>valencia</code></td></tr>
<tr><td><code>year</code></td><td>Publication year of the release, as used on the parameter-history page</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>arxiv</code></td><td>arXiv identifier of the source paper</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>journal</code></td><td>Full journal citation</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>table</code></td><td>The table or equation, inside that paper, the value is transcribed from and checked against</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>parameter</code></td><td>One of <code>dm2</code> (δm²), <code>Dm2</code> (Δm²), <code>sin2_th12</code>, <code>sin2_th13</code>, <code>sin2_th23</code>, <code>delta_pi</code> (δ/π)</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>ordering</code></td><td><code>no</code> (normal), <code>io</code> (inverted) or <code>any</code> (the paper quotes one value for both, or does not split by ordering)</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>convention</code></td><td>The release's own stated mass-splitting convention, verbatim from <code>history.yaml</code></td></tr>
<tr><td><code>kind</code></td><td><code>measurement</code> (a best fit) or <code>limit</code> (a bound with no measured central value)</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>value_as_published</code></td><td>The number as printed in the paper — see "The two value columns", above</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>value_our_convention</code></td><td>The same quantity in this group's convention — see "The two value columns", above</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>unit</code></td><td>The normalisation the value is written in, e.g. <code>1e-2</code> means the printed digits are hundredths</td></tr>
<tr><td><code>level</code></td><td>For a <code>limit</code> row only: the confidence level the bound holds at (<code>3sigma</code>, <code>2sigma</code>, <code>90%CL</code> or <code>95%CL</code>); empty for a <code>measurement</code> row</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<div class="prose" style="margin-top:1.2rem">
<p class="small muted">A best fit's 3σ (or 1σ, where that is all the paper
gives) range is not in this export: the register publishes the headline value
per point, best fit or bound, the same one plotted on the parameter-history
page. The ranges remain readable there, and in <code>history.yaml</code>
itself, alongside the source each number is checked against.</p>
</div>
