Experiments and results

DUNE develops AI triggers

Researchers are preparing automated, AI-based systems to sort through the large volumes of data the detector will produce, aiming to identify neutrino interactions, flag signs of a stellar explosion, and spot problems inside the detector itself. The effort builds on machine-learning work developed at Fermilab over previous decades.

DUNE detector stress test

A prototype DUNE detector module was subjected to an extreme high-voltage stress trial, reaching 300,000 volts, ahead of its eventual installation deep underground. The test is part of ongoing efforts to validate the detector design before it is installed underground.

300,000 volts: US team puts world’s largest neutrino detector through extreme stress test — Read it
IceCube probes Earth's interior

Researchers are using atmospheric neutrinos, produced when cosmic rays strike the atmosphere, to investigate the structure of the Earth's interior, complementing traditional seismic and gravitational measurements. Many questions about the Earth's deep structure remain unanswered, and neutrinos offer an independent probe.

Probing the Earth’s interior using atmospheric neutrinos — Read it
IceCube's WavePID tool

A new tool called WavePID identifies neutrino interactions from the shape of the light pattern they produce in the ice, working at the nanosecond level. That light is emitted by secondary particles created when a neutrino interacts with a molecule of ice, and its detailed timing structure carries information the tool can now extract.

WavePID: A tool that identifies neutrinos at the nanosecond level — Read it
NOvA hydrogen cross section

Using its near detector in the NuMI beam, the NOvA collaboration measured the cross section for muon antineutrino charged-current quasi-elastic scattering on hydrogen, exploiting the detector's hydrogen-rich composition to isolate the process. Backgrounds from interactions on heavier nuclei were constrained with dedicated control samples, yielding the highest-statistics sample of such antineutrino-hydrogen interactions measured to date.

IceCube Upgrade calibrations

During a cold week at the South Pole, IceCube's winterover crew carried out routine detector maintenance alongside ongoing calibrations for the IceCube Upgrade. Such weekly on-site work continues as the experiment prepares for the Upgrade.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • TAMBO, a mountain-based observatory designed to detect neutrinos via air showers, aims to help fill in the map of the high-energy neutrino sky, where existing detectors have struggled to build a complete picture. The paper, published in Nature Astronomy, describes the observatory's approach and its potential to turn up signs of new physics alongside its astrophysical goals.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining results from several neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed from ab initio, chiral-effective-field-theory-based nuclear theory, the authors derive global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. Unlike phenomenological nuclear models, their ab initio calculations indicate that the current generation of 0νββ experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivities needed to probe the mass scale in question.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using the GENIE event generator, the authors benchmark established and newer theoretical and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon scattering against recent MicroBooNE measurements of charged-current interactions. They find a clear separation between competing models in some cases, giving neutrino experiments a firmer basis for choosing between them as simulations are refined for upcoming argon-based detectors.

    Benchmarking state-of-the-art theory and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors examine how well NOvA and DUNE, used together, could constrain a scenario in which the heaviest neutrino mass state decays invisibly into a sterile state, under the assumption of the normal mass hierarchy. They find that the assumed true value of the mixing angle θ23 affects the resulting constraint on the decay parameter, with a true value in the higher octant improving it.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper presents a model in which a secluded dark-matter sector, built around an extra U(1) gauge symmetry with a Dirac dark-matter particle and three heavy neutrinos, generates both dark matter and active neutrino masses through shared Yukawa structures. The same loop-level mechanism that produces neutrino masses also fixes the mixing between a new singlet scalar and the Higgs, linking two otherwise separate puzzles within a single framework.

    Neutrino mass and secluded dark matter puzzles solved together — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Revisiting the proposal that winds driven by active galactic nucleus accretion disks contribute to the diffuse cosmic neutrino and gamma-ray backgrounds, the authors build a lepto-hadronic wind model calibrated against radio and gamma-ray data from nearby Seyfert galaxies. The work addresses how much of the observed backgrounds such winds could plausibly explain, a question previously complicated by poorly constrained wind energetics and ambient densities.
