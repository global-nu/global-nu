Experiments and results

Halzen and IceCube

On 6 October the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison, principal investigator of IceCube. The citation reads "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin". Physics World has since published a podcast on why neutrinos keep attracting Nobel prizes, with the neutrino physicist Kirsty Duffy as guest.

IceCube decoherence search

A new IceCube analysis searches for quantum-gravity-induced decoherence of neutrino flavour oscillations. It uses 368,071 atmospheric neutrinos between 0.5 and 100 TeV, an order of magnitude higher in energy than any previous decoherence search. The collaboration found no evidence for decoherence and sets what it describes as the strongest constraints to date.

Planck-Scale Constraints on Quantum Gravity-Induced Neutrino Decoherence with IceCube — arXiv
Combined atmospheric fit

A joint analysis combines public atmospheric data from Super-Kamiokande (all stages before the gadolinium phase), IceCube-DeepCore and the six-detection-unit configuration of KM3NeT/ORCA. All event samples are fitted simultaneously, with shared systematic uncertainties treated as fully correlated and detector-response uncertainties modelled independently per experiment. The paper is titled as evidence for normal neutrino ordering and also quotes constraints on the atmospheric oscillation parameters.

Evidence for Normal Neutrino Ordering with Combined Atmospheric Neutrino Experiments — arXiv
IceCube infrared flares

IceCube has posted a search for high-energy neutrinos from infrared flares. It is motivated by the neutrino emission seen from the blazar TXS 0506+056 and the Seyfert galaxy NGC 1068, and by external studies that proposed candidate sources such as the tidal disruption event AT2019dsg and the active galactic nucleus AT2019fdr. The abstract excerpt available here does not state the outcome of the search.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. A search for point-like astrophysical sources in its 2019–2024 track-like dataset found no significant source, so upper limits are computed for selected candidate objects. These are compared with published results from IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT.

T2K SuperFGD identification

SuperFGD is a highly granular scintillator target at the heart of the upgraded ND280 near detector of T2K. A published paper proposes a deep neural network with long short-term memory and a permutation-invariant feature encoder to distinguish electrons, photons, muons, pions and protons in it. The stated motivation is that precise particle identification is needed to reconstruct neutrino interactions and reduce systematic uncertainties in T2K's oscillation measurements.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The paper computes the neutrino emissivity of Δ-direct-Urca processes in neutron-star cores, where Δ isobars may accompany nucleons and hyperons. The contribution of these processes to neutrino emission had not been explicitly determined, and it bears on the thermal evolution and cooling of neutron stars, in which neutrino emission is the dominant early heat loss.

    Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors calculate two-neutrino double-beta decay rates with the full interdependence of nuclear structure and lepton kinematics, without the usual decoupling approximations. They present deviations of the decay rates and electron spectra from the closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion methods for 82Se and 136Xe. This matters for anyone interpreting double-beta spectra and the backgrounds to searches for the neutrinoless mode.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This work studies scalar-mediated neutrino self-interactions acting on the diffuse supernova neutrino background as it propagates through the cosmic neutrino background. It uses a full three-flavour framework that keeps the complete PMNS structure and considers four flavour-diagonal coupling structures. It is relevant to what a future detection of this background could say about non-standard neutrino interactions.

    Diffuse Supernova Neutrinos with Secret Neutrino Interactions — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper builds a gauged U(1) Lμ−Lτ model with an ultralight vector boson that kinetically mixes with the photon. This produces an effective matter potential in the Sun that modifies MSW flavour conversion, which is then confronted with solar measurements from Borexino, SNO and Super-Kamiokande. It also discusses prospects for JUNO and XLZD.

    Constraining ultralight vector bosons via solar neutrino oscillations: The gauged L μ - L … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors ask whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could leave nuclear-recoil tracks detectable in ancient minerals (paleodetectors). They vary neutron-star masses, include failed supernovae that form black holes, and consider the nuclear equation of state. They quantify how sensitivity increases for models with larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper examines neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu–Nandi–Tavartkiladze model, which has a scalar quadruplet and two vectorlike fermion triplets. Neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop, allowing sub-eV masses with new physics at the TeV scale. The authors explore how thermal leptogenesis works in this setting, which differs from canonical seesaw-based leptogenesis.

    Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
