Experiments and results

IceCube DeepCore

Four years ago the collaboration reported evidence for high-energy neutrinos from NGC 1068, an active galactic nucleus whose X-ray emission traces the environment around its central supermassive black hole. IceCube DeepCore data are now used in a new search for steady-state neutrino emission, building on that earlier evidence.

Search for steady-state neutrino emission using IceCube DeepCore — Read it
IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube has continued its search for the sources of the astrophysical neutrino flux first observed in 2013, having so far identified two galaxies as neutrino sources and detected neutrinos from the Milky Way. A new analysis searches for neutrino emission associated with tidal disruption events, transient phenomena that occur on timescales of weeks.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
IceCube Upgrade

IceCube has published projected sensitivities for the IceCube Upgrade, the planned enhancement of the DeepCore region. The study builds on the collaboration's evidence that active galaxies are likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration and sources of high-energy neutrinos.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
IceCube operations renewed

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, covering 2026 to 2031 with $53 million in funding. The five-year agreement secures continued operation and maintenance of the South Pole detector.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
DUNE

DUNE researchers are developing AI-based automated triggers to sort the large data volumes the experiment will produce, aimed at identifying neutrino interactions, spotting signs of a stellar explosion, and flagging detector problems as the collaboration prepares for data analysis deep underground. In parallel, Rice University's DUNE-TECH programme is introducing undergraduates to the science behind the experiment.

US lab to hunt most elusive ‘ghost particles’ deep underground with automated triggers — Read itParticles of Confederation — Read it
Lake Vermilion detector

A novel underwater neutrino detection experiment led by Fermilab, the Lake Vermilion neutrino detector, is due to begin testing this autumn in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
PandaX-4T quadruple beta decay

Using commissioning and first-science-run data from the PandaX-4T detector, an exposure of 148.4 kg·yr of xenon-136, the collaboration searched for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay and found no significant excess over background. The result sets a lower limit on the process's half-life of 6.01×10^24 years at 90% confidence, the most stringent constraint on this decay in xenon to date.

Search for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of ¹³⁶Xe in PandaX-4T detector — arXiv
NEXT-100 low pressure run

The NEXT-100 high-pressure xenon time projection chamber has completed its first physics run at the Laboratorio Subterráneo de Canfranc, operating at around 4 bar. This work reports the first validation of the detector's topological discrimination performance at low pressure, a capability central to the experiment's neutrinoless double beta decay programme.

Demonstrating topological identification capabilities of the NEXT experiment at low … — arXiv
NOvA Test Beam

NOvA deployed a dedicated Test Beam experiment at the Fermilab Test Beam Facility, running from April 2019 to July 2022, using a 30-ton segmented liquid scintillator detector functionally identical to the NOvA near and far detectors. The measurements characterise systematic uncertainties in detector response and calibration that underpin NOvA's long-baseline oscillation measurements of mixing parameters, mass ordering and CP violation.

The NOvA Test Beam Experiment — arXiv
JUNO information study

A new study quantifies how information about reactor antineutrino oscillation parameters flows from the neutrino state through to the events JUNO will observe, using quantum and classical Fisher information. The authors construct an information ladder for the JUNO configuration and find that the loss of precision at each stage depends strongly on which oscillation parameter is being measured.

Quantifying Information Hierarchy for Neutrino Oscillation Parameters at JUNO — arXiv

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • This paper performs a combined oscillation fit using recent atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, a combination long considered infeasible outside the individual collaborations. Fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters, the unified model describes all four datasets with no significant tensions and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • By combining likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed in an ab initio many-body approach using chiral effective field theory forces, this paper derives global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to results based on other nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass region implied by oscillation data.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper introduces TAMBO, the Tau Air-shower Mountain-Based Observatory, a proposed deep-valley detector aimed at mapping the high-energy neutrino sky, an area where the field has struggled to make progress despite a decade of neutrino astronomy. The authors present the observatory concept as a route to surveying neutrino sources while probing new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, and surveys its possible astrophysical and cosmological interpretations, including active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos. Despite extensive follow-up observations, the review notes that no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart to the event has been found.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper assesses the prospects for detecting neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae through the nuclear recoil tracks they leave in ancient minerals, so-called paleodetectors. The authors account for the diversity of core-collapse outcomes, including variations in neutron star mass and failed supernovae that form black holes, and show that sensitivity depends on the nuclear equation of state and is enhanced for more compact remnants.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using a full many-body calculation of neutrino helicity flips in dense neutrino environments, the authors show that spin-flip probabilities can exceed by orders of magnitude those predicted by standard mean-field treatments. The enhancement arises from many-body momentum exchange, an effect missed by calculations that consider only forward scattering, with implications for astrophysical settings where neutrino density is high.

    Neutrino helicity oscillations in astrophysical environments: A many-body approach — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
