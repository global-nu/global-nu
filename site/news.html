Experiments and results

DUNE cryostat construction

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to build the DUNE cryostat, nearly two million pounds of steel, filling a south cavern measuring 500 feet long, 65 feet wide and about seven storeys tall.

DUNE collaboration meeting

Researchers gathered at Unicamp for the DUNE Collaboration Meeting to review progress on the Fermilab-led experiment. DUNE was separately named among the twelve most ambitious international science projects, cited as an example of science that transcends borders.

Rice University's DUNE-TECH programme introduces undergraduates to the science underpinning DUNE, described as one of the nation's most ambitious physics experiments.

IceCube: KM3NeT event follow-up

IceCube is searching for the source of the highest-energy neutrino candidate ever detected, a 220 PeV event reported by the KM3NeT ARCA telescope. As a neutrino telescope with different sky coverage, IceCube's search complements KM3NeT's own effort to pin down the event's origin.

IceCube DeepCore steady-state search

IceCube's DeepCore infill array is being used to search for steady-state neutrino emission, building on the collaboration's earlier evidence for high-energy neutrinos from NGC 1068, an active galactic nucleus with a central supermassive black hole.

Search for steady-state neutrino emission using IceCube DeepCore — Read it
IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube continues to search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena on timescales of weeks, as part of its long-running effort to identify sources of the astrophysical neutrino flux first observed in 2013.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
IceCube extra-dimension search

IceCube data are being used to search for signatures of extra dimensions, motivated by the hierarchy problem — the unexplained disparity in strength between the weak force and gravity.

IceCube Upgrade deployment

During the austral summer of 2025/26, five new strings equipped with new photosensor designs were deployed as a dense infill in the middle of the existing IceCube detector, part of the IceCube Upgrade.

KM3NeT's ARCA site completed a sea campaign that advanced construction of the detector, including an enlargement of the submarine infrastructure, described by the collaboration as a significant step forward.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • A combined oscillation analysis unites recent atmospheric neutrino datasets from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters. The analysis finds no significant tension between the datasets and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance, a combination previously thought infeasible outside the individual experimental collaborations.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed via ab initio chiral effective field theory, this study derives global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. Unlike conventional nuclear models, the ab initio results suggest current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity required to probe the mass regime of interest.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, and surveys interpretations including active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos. Despite extensive follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been identified.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • TAMBO, a mountain-based neutrino observatory sited in a deep valley, is presented as a project to help map the high-energy neutrino sky, a goal that has remained only partially achieved a decade into the field of neutrino astronomy. The authors note its potential to also turn up clues of new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • A full many-body calculation of neutrino helicity oscillations — the mixing between left- and right-handed states enabled by a nonzero neutrino rest mass — finds spin-flip probabilities that can exceed mean-field estimates by orders of magnitude in dense neutrino environments. The enhancement traces to many-body momentum exchange, an effect missed by calculations that keep only forward-scattering processes.

    Neutrino helicity oscillations in astrophysical environments: A many-body approach — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This study assesses how neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected as nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals, so-called paleodetectors. It accounts for diverse collapse outcomes, including variation in neutron star mass and failed supernovae forming black holes, and the nuclear equation of state, finding sensitivity enhanced for more compact remnants.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
