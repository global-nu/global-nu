Nobel Prize for IceCube

The Royal Swedish Academy of Science announced on 6 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen, IceCube principal investigator, for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. Wisconsin Public Radio has since interviewed him about the prize and the importance of neutrino research. The KM3NeT Collaboration sent its congratulations, and the whole South Pole station gathered at the IceCube Lab for a celebratory photograph.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal, now 70% complete and taking data in partial configurations since 2016. A search for point-like astrophysical sources in a five-year track-like dataset from 2019-2024 found no significant source, so upper limits were computed for a selection of candidates. These are compared with published results from IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT.

IceCube infrared flares

A new IceCube paper searches for high-energy neutrinos from infrared flares. It is motivated by neutrino emission already seen from the blazar TXS 0506+056 and the Seyfert galaxy NGC 1068, which points to accretion onto supermassive black holes as a candidate origin of the diffuse flux. The record gives no result for the search.

T2K near-detector upgrade

The leading systematic uncertainties in T2K's oscillation analysis come from modelling neutrino-nucleus interactions, and are largely constrained by measurements at the ND280 near detector. The ND280 upgrade was completed in 2024. One new paper presents a selection of muon-neutrino charged-current events with single positively charged pion production in the new modules, and another proposes a neural network to identify particles in the SuperFGD.

Gallium anomaly and the Sun

A preprint reconsiders the Gallium Anomaly by using the Sun as a neutrino source calibrated through neutrino-electron elastic scattering in terrestrial detectors. It finds the measured gallium capture rate of 66.1±3.1 SNU from SAGE and Gallex/GNO in good agreement with the total measured solar source strength of 67.5 (+1.4, -1.1) SNU, and concludes the result is not anomalous.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper examines the contribution of Δ-direct-Urca processes to neutrino emissivity in neutron stars that may contain Δ isobars. These processes add to the nucleonic and hyperonic Urca channels that are regarded as dominant cooling mechanisms, so the result bears on neutron-star thermal evolution.

Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full lepton-energy dependence

The authors compute two-neutrino double-beta decay rates without decoupling nuclear structure from the lepton kinematics, and compare with the closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion approximations for 82Se and 136Xe. Anyone using these rates or the electron spectra in double-beta searches should note that deviations from the traditional methods are reported.

Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses

The authors ask whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected through nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals. They include variations in neutron-star mass, failed supernovae that form black holes, and the nuclear equation of state, and quantify how sensitivity grows for models with larger neutrino emission.

Diffuse Supernova Neutrinos with Secret Neutrino Interactions

The paper examines how scalar-mediated neutrino self-interactions, acting during propagation through the cosmic neutrino background, would leave spectral imprints on the diffuse supernova neutrino background. It uses a full three-flavour framework with the complete PMNS structure, which matters for next-generation detectors expected to observe this flux.

Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass

The authors study neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, where masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model accommodates sub-eV neutrino masses with new physics at the TeV scale, and the paper tests whether thermal leptogenesis is viable there, which differs from canonical seesaw leptogenesis.
