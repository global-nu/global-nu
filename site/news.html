Experiments and results

Lake Vermilion Detector

A new underwater neutrino detector led by Fermilab is being deployed in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, Minnesota, continuing the state's history of neutrino research. Testing is due to begin this autumn.

IceCube Upgrade Sensitivities

The IceCube Collaboration has published projected sensitivities for the IceCube Upgrade, the densely instrumented multi-PMT extension installed within the existing DeepCore infill array in 2025/2026. The projections cover neutrino transient searches, steady point-source emission such as from NGC 1068, and diffuse emission, with commissioning expected to be complete by the end of 2026.

IceCube Operations Renewal

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers the period 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
DUNE AI Triggers

DUNE researchers are preparing for the experiment's data-analysis phase by developing AI systems intended to sort large volumes of data, identify neutrino interactions, spot signatures of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector. The work draws on machine-learning experience built up at Fermilab.

XENONnT Solar Neutrinos

The XENONnT dark-matter experiment reported the first measurement of low-energy solar neutrinos via elastic neutrino-electron scattering at the keV scale, using 2.46 tonne-year exposure from its first two science runs. The analysis rejected the background-only hypothesis at 5.0 sigma and measured a solar pp neutrino flux of (10.2 ± 2.0) × 10^10 cm^-2 s^-1, reaching the lowest energy threshold achieved for any neutrino detection to date.

Super-Kamiokande Proton Decay

Using the full 401 kiloton-year pure-water-phase exposure, Super-Kamiokande searched for proton decay into a charged antilepton plus an invisible massless particle and found no significant signal beyond the expected atmospheric neutrino background. The collaboration set 90% confidence lower limits on the partial proton lifetime of 1.72×10^33 years for the positron channel and 0.61×10^33 years for the muon channel, improving on previous limits by factors of about 2 and 1.5 respectively.

NUCLEUS CEvNS Detectors

The NUCLEUS experiment, which aims to study coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering from reactor antineutrinos at the Chooz nuclear power plant, has developed and commissioned the cryogenic target-detector module for its Technical Run at the Technical University of Munich. The module comprises four gram-scale CaWO4 detectors with a total target mass of 6.96 g.

FASER and SND@LHC

Proton-proton collisions at the LHC produce a large flux of high-energy, far-forward neutrinos reaching TeV-scale energies, opening an experimental window beyond the reach of traditional accelerator-based neutrino experiments. The FASER and SND@LHC experiments have reported the first direct observations of these collider neutrinos.

Neutrino Experiments at the LHC and the HL-LHC — arXiv
ARA Diffuse Flux Search

The Askaryan Radio Array at the South Pole has carried out its first array-wide diffuse flux search for ultra-high-energy neutrinos, using data from its five autonomous stations collected between January 2013 and December 2023. The analysis incorporates improved detector characterisation and simulation, including data-driven noise and electronics models and revised neutrino interaction modelling.

The First Array-Wide Diffuse Flux Search for UHE Neutrinos with the Askaryan Radio Array — arXiv
IceCube Tidal Disruption Search

IceCube has searched for high-energy neutrino emission coincident with 89 tidal disruption events selected from optical/UV and X-ray observations. The analysis used time-dependent likelihood methods to look for emission from individual sources as well as from the population as a whole.

Search for Neutrinos from Tidal Disruption Events with IceCube — arXiv

In contrast to conventional nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the relevant mass scale.

Because the predicted background level depends sensitively on poorly constrained wind energetics and ambient densities, the work helps assess how much of the diffuse neutrino flux such winds could plausibly account for.
