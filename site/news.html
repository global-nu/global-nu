Prizes for IceCube and MicroBooNE

Panofsky Prize for co-leadership of MicroBooNE at Fermilab, which advanced next-generation liquid-argon time projection chamber technology for neutrino detection.

DUNE construction

Fermilab, CERN and SURF crews have started building the first of two large cryostats, insulated containers designed to hold liquid argon at very low temperature for DUNE. Fermilab News also reports a construction milestone for the project, with Colorado State University and the University of Texas at Arlington among the institutions contributing to detector development and components such as the field cage.

SBND cross sections

SBND reported measurements of the total and single-differential inclusive electron-neutrino charged-current cross sections on argon. The sample is 95.2% electron neutrinos and 4.8% electron antineutrinos, and the authors describe it as the highest-statistics measurement of this channel on argon to date and the first using the Booster Neutrino Beam. Such measurements matter for electron-neutrino appearance searches in both long- and short-baseline experiments.

JUNO ultrapure water

JUNO needs ultrapure water with a radium concentration below 50 μBq/m3 because the water is in direct contact with the liquid scintillator during detector filling. A new paper describes a measurement system able to detect radium in water down to 3.9 μBq/m3 and an optimised 100 t/h production process that combines selective ion-exchange resin with membrane separation.

Geoneutrinos and decay

A new analysis uses geoneutrinos, which have energies of a few MeV and cross up to the Earth's diameter, to test neutrino decay for the first time. The signal at Borexino, KamLAND, SNO+ and JUNO was computed for visible decay and compared with published data. The author finds no evidence of decay that holds across Earth models.

ProtoDUNE muon seasonality

The DUNE Collaboration measured seasonal variation of the cosmic-ray muon rate with the ProtoDUNE single-phase detector at the CERN Neutrino Platform, using data from December 2018 to July 2020. The modulation has a significance of 4σ against no seasonal variation, with a maximum in winter, consistent with other surface experiments with a low muon-energy threshold.

SHiP two-baseline sensitivity

The SHiP Collaboration shows that its baseline detector configuration forms a two-baseline experiment for oscillations induced by a sterile neutrino. Neutrino interactions are recorded in the scattering and neutrino detector and, about 70 m further downstream, in the calorimeter system. The study uses a Geant4 simulation of Genie-generated interactions to derive the energy response and the separation of electron-neutrino events from neutral-current pi-zero background.

A probabilistic surrogate of the MINERvA detector response was trained on an open simulation dataset. It maps generator-level final-state particles and interaction geometry to reconstructed observables, which the authors say previously required computationally intensive methods inaccessible outside the original collaborations.

Movable reactor at JUNO

A phenomenology study considers a movable reactor antineutrino source placed within about 3 km of the JUNO detector, with the aim of improving on Daya Bay's precision on theta-13. It considers a microreactor of order 20 MW thermal and argues that JUNO's large fiducial mass would give a statistically significant event count over a few years.
