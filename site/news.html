Experiments and results

JUNO oscillation update

The JUNO Collaboration reports an improved measurement of oscillation parameters from 207.2 live days, using 8,294 inverse beta-decay candidates at an average reactor baseline of 52.5 km. It quotes a precision of approximately 1% on Δm²31, described as world-leading. The values are +2.509 (+0.027, −0.025) ×10⁻³ eV² for normal ordering and −2.482 (+0.025, −0.026) ×10⁻³ eV² for inverted ordering, with the Daya Bay constraint used in the fit. The paper's title reports an indication of the mass ordering; the excerpt available here does not give its significance.

DUNE cryostat steel

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to construct the cryostat. This amounts to nearly 2 million pounds of steel, which completely fills the south cavern. Separately, the collaboration recently met at Unicamp, where progress on the project was discussed.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. A point-source search on a 5-year track-like dataset from 2019-2024 found no significant neutrino source. Upper limits are given for a selection of candidate objects and compared with published IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT results.

MINERvA superscaling

The MINERvA Collaboration reports the first measurement of the superscaling variable ψ′ in neutrino–nucleus scattering. It uses charged-current quasielastic-like νμ–carbon interactions in the medium-energy NuMI beam, with ψ′ reconstructed from visible final-state kinematics. The variable is used as an externally calibrated quantity whose peak position is sensitive to the invisible nuclear missing energy.

T2K near detector

The ND280 upgrade was completed in 2024 and has improved the detector's tracking capabilities. Two new papers address the upgraded detector. One proposes a neural network to identify electrons, photons, muons, pions and protons in SuperFGD. The other presents an event selection for muon-neutrino charged-current interactions with single positively charged pion production, aimed at better constraining interaction models.

EXO-200 public data

The EXO-200 collaboration has released a subset of calibration data for AI and machine-learning applications. It comprises approximately one million events from ²²⁸Th calibration campaigns between 26 February and 2 March 2018, near the end of Phase-II operations. Each event includes raw waveforms from the U-wire, V-wire and APD channels together with reconstructed quantities, distributed in HDF5 format.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • This paper presents what it calls the first combined oscillation analysis of recent atmospheric datasets from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA, together with Daya Bay reactor data. A single physics model describes all datasets with no significant parameter tensions, and the fit prefers normal over inverted ordering at 3σ. It matters because such combinations were long considered infeasible outside the collaborations.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors combine current neutrinoless double-beta decay results with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory, within a Bayesian framework. In contrast to nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate that the current generation of experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivities required for the mass limits at stake. This bears on how the reach of running searches is interpreted.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies invisible decay of ν3 into a sterile state under normal ordering, and the ability of DUNE and NOvA to constrain the decay parameter. The true value of θ23 affects the constraint significantly, and a higher-octant value improves it.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors consider neutrinos from a protoneutron star, including nuclear scattering through the neutral Gamow-Teller current and oscillations in the outer stellar layers. They find the electron antineutrino spectrum hardens significantly, and that this is most pronounced for the inverted mass ordering. That makes the spectrum relevant to ordering-sensitive supernova neutrino detection.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper investigates whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could leave nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals, known as paleodetectors. It varies neutron star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the nuclear equation of state. Sensitivity is found to be enhanced for models with larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors study composite neutrino models in which heavy neutrinos are bound states of a near-conformal strongly coupled sector, mixing with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. They obtain transition dipole couplings of about 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁸ GeV⁻¹, parametrically larger than loop-level predictions in minimal Dirac or Majorana models. An event-level simulation of the production-and-decay signature is carried out.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
