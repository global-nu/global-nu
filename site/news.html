Experiments and results

Nobel Prize for IceCube

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced on 6 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison. The citation reads "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin". IceCube says he proposed and proved that ice could be used as a detection medium. CERN notes that IceCube found high-energy neutrinos coming from far beyond our Solar System, and says the work is paving the way for a new generation of observatories.

IceCube has published a search for the source of the event that the KM3NeT Collaboration recently reported as the highest-energy neutrino candidate ever discovered, at 220 PeV. The record gives the motivation for the search and does not state its outcome here.

IceCube and HAWC

IceCube and HAWC data are being used together to investigate the centre of our galaxy. The region is of interest for how cosmic rays are accelerated to extremely high energies, and it contains dense gas and potential accelerators, including a supermassive black hole. The particles of particular interest are those accelerated to PeV energies.

Super-Kamiokande reactor antineutrinos

Super-Kamiokande reported the first observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors, using SK-Gd data with a total exposure of 411.52 live days. The prompt energy spectra and monthly event rates are consistent with the oscillated reactor antineutrino prediction after accounting for accidental, geoneutrino and spallation backgrounds. The no-reactor hypothesis is disfavoured, with a signal significance of approximately 6σ.

DUNE construction

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to build the cryostat for DUNE. The steel fills the south cavern, which is 500 feet long and 65 feet wide. Separately, researchers gathered at Unicamp for a DUNE Collaboration Meeting to discuss progress of the project, which Fermilab leads.

PandaX-4T supernova background

PandaX-4T presented the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background using coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. With a total exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs, it observed no significant event excess over the expected background. It set an upper limit of (2.27–2.43)×10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 per flavour at 90% confidence level on the ν_x flux, for incident energies above 19.3 MeV.

Search for Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background of All Flavors with PandaX-4T — arXiv
MicroBooNE cross sections

MicroBooNE reported the first double-differential cross-section measurements of muon-neutrino charged-current interactions on argon with no mesons and one or more protons in the final state. The measurements use the full Booster Neutrino Beam dataset, corresponding to 1.30×10^21 protons on target. They are given as a function of proton multiplicity and kinematics, up to and including the fourth-leading proton.

JUNO mass splittings

A preprint by I. Alikhanov states that the JUNO Collaboration has reported the most precise measurements to date of both Δm²_21 and Δm²_31. The author tests an empirical relation between the two splittings, proposed earlier on empirical grounds. He obtains 1.4143 (+0.0036, −0.0038) against a predicted √2, and calls this excellent agreement. The relation is the preprint's own claim and not a JUNO result.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. A search for point-like astrophysical sources used a five-year track-like event dataset collected in 2019–2024. No significant source was found, so upper limits were computed for a selection of candidate objects and compared with published IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT results.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The paper computes two-neutrino double-beta decay rates with a unified formula that keeps the full interdependence between nuclear structure and lepton kinematics, instead of the usual decoupling approximations. It presents deviations in decay rates and electron spectra from the closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion approaches for 82Se and 136Xe. This matters to anyone interpreting double-beta-decay spectra, since the two-neutrino mode is the background to the neutrinoless search.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper considers magnetic and oscillation effects on neutrinos emitted from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. It finds that oscillations in the outer layers of the star harden the electron-antineutrino spectrum significantly. The enhancement is particularly pronounced for the inverted mass ordering, which the authors describe as favourable for ground-based detection of supernova neutrinos.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper investigates whether nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals, known as paleodetectors, could register neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae. It varies neutron-star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the nuclear equation of state. The sensitivity enhancement is quantified for models with larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This experimental review covers high-energy neutrino astronomy. It describes the road to the present instruments, the current status of the field and future developments. It is a useful single entry point for a physicist outside the subfield, and it dates the detection of the diffuse extraterrestrial flux to barely a dozen years ago.

    Neutrino Astronomy at High Energies-An Experimental Review — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The review summarises the main properties of the ultra-high-energy event KM3-230213A seen by KM3NeT and discusses its interpretation in different astrophysical and cosmological scenarios. These include active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos, with attention to multimessenger constraints. It reports that, despite extensive follow-up observations, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been found.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, which has a scalar quadruplet and a pair of vectorlike fermion triplets. Neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop, so sub-eV masses are possible with the new-physics scale at the TeV level. The authors explore leptogenesis in a setting they describe as distinct from canonical seesaw-based leptogenesis.
