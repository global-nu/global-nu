In summary. This week's neutrino news is dominated by null results that sharpen the field's exclusion limits: NOvA finds no evidence for sterile neutrinos, IceCube sees no emission from the direction of KM3NeT's record ultra-high-energy event, and PandaX-4T tightens constraints on neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of 136Xe. On the infrastructure side, underwater detector testing is set to begin this autumn in Minnesota's lakes and DUNE continues developing automated data-analysis tools, while published theory work spans a combined atmospheric-neutrino oscillation fit, ab initio limits on the Majorana neutrino mass, and new detection concepts such as the TAMBO observatory. Experiments and results

NOvA sterile search

NOvA has completed its first search for oscillations to a sterile neutrino that combines both neutrino- and antineutrino-mode beam data, fitting the Near and Far Detectors simultaneously with exposures of 26.61x10^20 and 12.50x10^20 protons on target in the two modes. The collaboration found no evidence for sterile neutrinos and excluded regions of parameter space allowed by previous experiments, including most of the region reported by IceCube.

IceCube and the KM3NeT event

IceCube searched fifteen years of its data for high-energy neutrino emission from the direction of the roughly 200 PeV event recorded by the KM3NeT ARCA detector in February 2023, the highest-energy neutrino yet reported. Testing steady, flaring and unconstrained-time emission hypotheses, the collaboration found no evidence of emission from that direction and set upper limits accordingly.

IceCube neutrino point-source searches in the direction of the KM3NeT ultra-high-energy … — arXiv
PandaX-4T quadruple beta decay

Using commissioning and first science-run data corresponding to a 136Xe exposure of 148.4 kg-yr, PandaX-4T searched for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay, a process whose observation without neutrinoless double beta decay would point to Dirac rather than Majorana neutrinos. No excess over background was seen, and the collaboration set a lower limit on the half-life of 6.01x10^24 years at 90% confidence, the most stringent constraint on this decay in xenon to date.

Search for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of ¹³⁶Xe in PandaX-4T detector — arXiv
IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

IceCube has published projected sensitivities for the IceCube Upgrade, the planned addition of densely instrumented strings to the existing array, framed within the collaboration's ongoing effort to identify the sources of the high-energy astrophysical neutrinos it has observed since 2013.

IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube has carried out a search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena on timescales of weeks to months, as part of its continuing effort to pin down sources of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos beyond the two galaxies and the Milky Way already identified.

Lake Vermilion detector

Fermilab is leading a new underwater neutrino detection effort spanning Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion in Minnesota, with testing due to begin this autumn, continuing the state's history of neutrino research.

DUNE automated triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI-based automated trigger systems ahead of data-taking, intended to sort through large volumes of detector data, identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and flag problems inside the detector.

IceCube operations renewed

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin-Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a five-year, $53 million agreement covering 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
NOvA detector systematics

NOvA has reported results from its dedicated Test Beam experiment, which used a 30-ton segmented liquid-scintillator detector functionally identical to the Near and Far Detectors to characterise detector response with tagged particles from proton-copper collisions at the Fermilab Test Beam Facility between 2019 and 2022. A companion study of neutron detector response in NOvA's high-statistics antineutrino near-detector sample found an excess of simulated low-energy neutron candidates relative to data, a source of model-dependent systematic uncertainty in energy reconstruction.

JUNO information limits

A new study builds an 'information ladder' for JUNO, using quantum and classical Fisher information to track how precisely reactor antineutrino oscillation parameters can be recovered as information passes from the neutrino state through to the events actually observed in the detector. The authors find that the resulting loss of precision is strongly dependent on which oscillation parameter is being measured.

Quantifying Information Hierarchy for Neutrino Oscillation Parameters at JUNO — arXiv

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • Eller presents the first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1,536 bins with 91 parameters. The unified model describes all datasets with no significant tensions and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3-sigma significance, a combination long considered infeasible outside the individual collaborations.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Shickele, Jokiniemi, Belley and collaborators combine likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed from ab initio nuclear theory to derive global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. Unlike phenomenological nuclear models, their ab initio results indicate that current-generation 0-nu-beta-beta experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass regime of interest.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Argueelles, Bazo, Bricenio and collaborators describe TAMBO, a proposed mountain-based observatory that would detect tau neutrinos via air showers in a deep valley, aimed at mapping the high-energy neutrino sky in an energy range that has so far been hard to access and potentially revealing signs of new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Yamasaki and Nakazato examine whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected today as nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals, accounting for the diversity of collapse outcomes including neutron star mass variation and failed supernovae that form black holes. They find sensitivity is enhanced for more compact remnants and larger neutrino emission, with the nuclear equation of state, through its effect on neutron star radii and maximum masses, also playing a role.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Ergashov, Narzilloev, Hussain and collaborators study how dark matter coexisting with a phantom field near a black hole affects two-flavour neutrino oscillations. They find the oscillation phase for radially moving neutrinos behaves as in flat spacetime, while neutrinos on deflected trajectories pick up additional phase shifts that produce a degeneracy between the lens mass and the dark matter parameters in the transition probability.

    Probing dark matter and phantom field effects on neutrino oscillations around black holes — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Xu, Froustey, Fuller and collaborators carry out a full many-body calculation of neutrino helicity (spin-flip) oscillations in dense neutrino environments relevant to settings such as supernovae. They find that many-body momentum exchange can enhance spin-flip probabilities by orders of magnitude beyond mean-field predictions, an effect missed by calculations that consider only forward scattering.

    Neutrino helicity oscillations in astrophysical environments: A many-body approach — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
