In summary. This week's neutrino news centres on DUNE, which is stress-testing prototype detector hardware and building AI-based triggers ahead of installation, alongside new analyses that use Super-Kamiokande's diffuse supernova neutrino background indication and the ultra-high-energy KM3NeT event to probe astrophysical and particle-physics questions. On the theory side, several papers extend neutrino mass and flavour model-building, from axion-lepton couplings to global limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay. Experiments and results

Super-Kamiokande DSNB constraint

A new analysis takes as its starting point Super-Kamiokande's reported indication of the diffuse supernova neutrino background, with a statistical significance of 2.6σ. Using long-term neutrino-radiation hydrodynamic simulations, the authors examine how axion cooling would alter the predicted DSNB flux and derive an upper limit on the axion-proton coupling constant, comparable to the existing bound from the SN 1987A neutrino burst. Unlike the SN 1987A bound, this constraint does not rely on the properties of a single observed supernova.

KM3NeT event origin

A new paper investigates whether the ultra-high-energy neutrino event KM3-230213A could be a cosmogenic neutrino, produced by ultra-high-energy cosmic rays accelerated in the lobes of FRII radio galaxies. The authors model the expected cosmic-ray, neutrino and photon fluxes using a luminosity-dependent density evolution of radio galaxies, deriving the FRII contribution to the ultra-high-energy cosmic-ray population self-consistently from the observed radio luminosity function.

DUNE AI triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI systems to sort through the large data volumes the experiment will produce, aiming to identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector. The effort draws on decades of machine-learning work at Fermilab and is intended to help automate data analysis triggers.

DUNE detector stress test

A DUNE prototype detector was subjected to an extreme 300,000-volt stress trial ahead of its installation deep underground in the United States, testing the limits of the detector design.

300,000 volts: US team puts world’s largest neutrino detector through extreme stress test — Read it
DUNE/T2HK CP sensitivity

A theoretical study examines the sensitivity of the DUNE and T2HK long-baseline experiments to time-reversal violation, searching for an L-dependence of the muon-to-electron transition probability at fixed neutrino energy rather than exchanging initial and final flavours. The authors find that DUNE and T2HK together could establish an L-odd component in the oscillation probability at up to around 4σ significance, with DUNE's second oscillation maximum playing a key role.

Probing T and CP Violation at DUNE and T2HK — arXiv
IceCube Earth interior

Atmospheric neutrinos detected by IceCube, produced in cosmic-ray-induced air showers, are being used to probe the properties of the Earth's interior, complementing indirect methods such as seismic and gravitational measurements.

Probing the Earth’s interior using atmospheric neutrinos — Read it
IceCube WavePID tool

IceCube has introduced WavePID, a tool that identifies neutrino interactions from the shape of the light pattern produced when secondary particles from a neutrino interaction emit blue light in the ice, working at the nanosecond level.

WavePID: A tool that identifies neutrinos at the nanosecond level — Read it
JUNO calibration background

A study of JUNO's calibration system quantifies its contribution to the detector's radioactive background, requiring extensive material screening since several calibration components sit close to the liquid-scintillator target. JUNO's reactor antineutrino analysis requires the total singles background rate from radioactivity to stay below 10 Hz in the 0.7-12 MeV range within the fiducial volume.

The Radioactive Background of the JUNO Calibration System — arXiv
NINJA emulsion detector

The NINJA collaboration reports charged-particle multiplicities and kinematic distributions of muons, protons and charged pions from charged-current muon-neutrino interactions on water, recorded with an emulsion detector exposed to the J-PARC neutrino beam. The analysis, based on an exposure of 4.63×10^20 protons on target, selected 82 events from a 3.9 kg fiducial water volume and compared the reconstructed distributions with Monte Carlo predictions.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • A minimal flavoured axion model combines the KSVZ framework with a type-I seesaw, treating the Peccei-Quinn symmetry as a flavour symmetry with flavour-dependent charge assignments. The resulting zero texture in the inverse Majorana neutrino mass matrix, together with the observed size of θ13, predicts an inverted neutrino mass ordering with a specific ratio range for m1/m3, linking axion-lepton couplings directly to the mass-ordering question DUNE and JUNO aim to resolve.

    Neutrino mass hierarchy and axion–lepton couplings in a minimal flavored νKSVZ model — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper studies how invisible decay of the heaviest neutrino mass state into a sterile state would show up at NOνA and DUNE, under the assumption that ν3 is unstable in the normal mass hierarchy. It finds that the true value of θ23 significantly affects the achievable constraint on the decay parameter, with the higher octant giving a notably better bound when NOνA and DUNE are combined.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This work derives global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass by combining likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the relevant mass range, a sobering benchmark for the field.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper on the TAMBO mountain-based observatory concept describes the project's goal of mapping the high-energy neutrino sky more fully than existing facilities have managed, aiming also to search for signs of new physics along the way.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that produces a rank-two Dirac neutrino mass matrix, enforcing one exactly massless neutrino while remaining consistent with oscillation data and cosmological constraints. Anomaly cancellation forces a specific dark-sector particle content, giving a predictive and testable origin for the Dirac neutrino mass pattern.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper presents a secluded dark-matter framework built on an extra U(1) gauge symmetry in which the portal between a singlet scalar and the Standard Model Higgs arises only at one loop, through the same Yukawa couplings that generate active neutrino masses. This ties the size of the Higgs-portal mixing angle directly to the heavy-neutrino mass spectrum, linking two otherwise separate puzzles.

    Neutrino mass and secluded dark matter puzzles solved together — INSPIRE · DOI
