This week's neutrino news centres on facility milestones and design studies rather than discoveries: IceCube's five-year operations renewal and Upgrade sensitivity projections, a KM3NeT point-source stacking search, a new underwater detector test beginning in Minnesota, and progress on DUNE's automated triggers, the proposed NEON telescope and the LHC's forward-neutrino programme. Experiments and results

KM3NeT point-source search

A stacking analysis of 336 days of KM3NeT/ARCA data, collected with 19-21 detection units, searched for high-energy neutrino emission from 75 ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and two blazar populations. The sample yielded 1544 track-like neutrino candidates, assessed against power-law spectral models for the candidate sources.

NEON deep-sea telescope

The proposed Neutrino Observatory in the Nanhai (NEON), a deep-sea telescope planned for the South China Sea, now has its first comprehensive reconstruction framework for shower-like events. The framework combines a physics-driven maximum-likelihood method with a graph neural network, addressing challenges from seawater optical attenuation, irregular detector geometry and potassium-40 background, ahead of energy measurements and multi-messenger work.

IceCube and extra dimensions

IceCube data are being used to search for signatures of extra spatial dimensions, one candidate explanation for the hierarchy problem: the vast disparity between the strength of the weak force and gravity.

Lake Vermilion detector

A Fermilab-led underwater neutrino detector will begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
Forward neutrinos at the LHC

A review of the LHC's far-forward neutrino programme describes how proton-proton collisions produce a large flux of TeV-scale neutrinos concentrated near the beam axis, with electron and tau neutrinos at the highest energies dominated by heavy-flavour decays. The FASER and SND@LHC experiments have reported the first direct observations of these collider neutrinos, opening an energy window beyond traditional accelerator-based neutrino experiments, with further prospects at the HL-LHC.

Neutrino Experiments at the LHC and the HL-LHC — arXiv

IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

IceCube has already found evidence that active galaxies are among the sources of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration. A new study examines the projected sensitivities of the planned IceCube Upgrade for continuing this search for neutrino sources.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
IceCube funding renewal

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to manage and operate the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers continued operation and maintenance of the observatory for 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
DUNE automated triggers

DUNE researchers are preparing for data analysis by developing AI systems that can sort through large volumes of data, identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion and detect problems inside the detector.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • Combining results from current neutrinoless double-beta decay searches with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory, this study derives global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. Unlike results based on conventional nuclear models, the ab initio matrix elements suggest that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass scale of interest, a sobering benchmark for the field.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using the DUNE and NOvA long-baseline setups, this study constrains a scenario in which the heaviest neutrino mass state decays invisibly into a sterile state under the normal mass ordering. The authors find that the assumed true value of the mixing angle θ23 strongly affects how tightly the decay parameter can be constrained, with a value in the higher octant improving the bound.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper uses the GENIE event generator to benchmark competing theoretical and empirical models of pionless charged-current neutrino-argon scattering against recent MicroBooNE measurements. It finds strong separation between some competing models, giving upcoming argon-based experiments a clearer basis for choosing interaction models in their simulations.

    Benchmarking state-of-the-art theory and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that forces a rank-two Dirac neutrino mass matrix, leaving one neutrino exactly massless while remaining consistent with oscillation data and cosmological constraints. Anomaly cancellation fixes the required dark-sector field content, giving an economical origin for both the Dirac nature of neutrino masses and the existence of a massless state.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Working in modified teleparallel gravity, this paper shows how spacetime torsion alters both vacuum neutrino oscillations and the matter-enhanced MSW resonance, and uses solar neutrino data to place new bounds on the torsion-neutrino coupling. The authors suggest the resulting modification to the survival probability could account for observational discrepancies without invoking new particle physics.

    Testing modified teleparallel gravity by neutrino oscillations — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This model generates neutrino mass radiatively while allowing a Dirac dark matter candidate to semi-annihilate into an anti-dark matter particle plus a neutrino, producing boosted dark matter in the present universe. For dark matter masses of 20-50 MeV with a tuned resonance, the authors find the boosted component could be probed by DARWIN, alongside a monochromatic neutrino signal.

    A radiative neutrino mass model with semi-annihilating boosted dark matter — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
