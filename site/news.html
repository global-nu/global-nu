Experiments and results

DUNE cryostats

Crews from Fermilab, CERN and SURF have started building the first of two cryostats, the insulated containers that will hold liquid argon for the experiment. Together the cryostats will house approximately 17,000 tons of liquid argon. At present the crews are assembling large steel beams that form the outer structure of the detector modules, nearly a mile underground in South Dakota.

DUNE collaboration and near detector

Black Hills State University has become an institutional member of the collaboration, which now counts more than 1,500 collaborators from 228 institutions in 38 countries and CERN. Separately, a simulation-based study by the collaboration examines sensitivity of the near detector to 3+1 sterile-neutrino oscillations. It uses three event samples: highly elastic muon-neutrino charged-current, highly elastic electron-neutrino charged-current, and neutrino-electron elastic scattering.

IceCube Galactic plane

A new Letter reports the first measurement of how the high-energy neutrino flux varies along the Galactic plane, using 10 years of neutrino-induced shower data. The flux normalisation and spectral index are fitted in several regions independently of model expectations. The spectral indices are 2.76 (+0.18, −0.15) in the inner region and 3.13 (+0.57, −0.37) in the outer region. Separately, more than 230 participants attended the collaboration's fall meeting in Madison.

Askaryan Radio Array

The collaboration reports the first array-wide search for diffuse ultrahigh-energy neutrinos, using data from all five stations at the South Pole. It found no events on an expected background of 0.13 ± 0.01 (stat.) +0.05/−0.06 (syst.). The resulting upper limit is the most stringent from 50 EeV to 1000 EeV and gives the first constraints on the flux reported by KM3NeT. A companion paper sets out the analysis methodology for the 10.6-year dataset, which is three times larger than in previous ARA searches.

A new paper describes searches for joint observations of high-energy neutrinos and gravitational waves from the same sources, using KM3NeT and ANTARES data taken during the O3 observing run. The motivation is that sources of gravitational waves may also produce high-energy neutrinos. The record does not state the outcome of the search.

DEAP-3600 mergers

DEAP-3600 searched its data for neutrinos coincident with gravitational waves from compact mergers seen by LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA. The detector recorded data during seven mergers involving neutron stars between 2016 and 2020. It finds no statistically significant evidence of neutrinos from any of them. The detector is sensitive to electron neutrinos through charged-current absorption on 40Ar, as recently observed.

ESSnuSB solar neutrinos

A sensitivity study of the ESSnuSB large underground water-Cherenkov detector addresses two solar-neutrino signatures: the MSW spectral upturn in the 8B electron-neutrino survival probability, and a first direct observation of the rare hep flux. The analysis includes energy-resolution and flux-normalisation systematics, and models backgrounds from cosmic-ray muon spallation and 222Rn. It uses a shape-driven strategy with floating normalisation, so discovery power rests on spectral distortion rather than on the absolute rate.

CUPID light detectors

CUPID is a planned tonne-scale cryogenic search for neutrinoless double beta decay of 100Mo in Li2MoO4 crystals, to be located at Gran Sasso. A new paper reports preliminary measurements with the Vertical Slice Test Tower prototype, built to test detector response and the mechanical and thermal stability of the apparatus. A key objective was to demonstrate the improved performance of germanium-based cryogenic light detectors.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper computes the neutrino emissivity of Δ-direct-Urca processes in neutron-star matter, a contribution the authors say had not been explicitly studied despite recent proposals that Δ isobars exist in dense cores. It matters to neutrino physicists because Urca processes are dominant neutrino-cooling mechanisms in neutron-star thermal evolution.

Toward measuring the low-energy cosmic-ray spectrum with next-generation neutrino detectors

The authors ask whether kiloton-scale detectors such as Hyper-Kamiokande can determine the primary cosmic-ray spectrum from the atmospheric neutrino flux. They find that future detectors can distinguish between cosmic-ray models with high statistical significance, even allowing for uncertainties in cross sections and hadronic interactions. The appeal is full-sky coverage and long-term monitoring, which satellite and balloon experiments lack.

Quasisteady flavor configuration of multienergy neutrino ensembles

The paper presents semianalytic approximations for the outcome of slow and fast flavour conversion in quasihomogeneous neutrino ensembles with periodic boundary conditions. These are meant as subgrid schemes for supernova simulations that cannot include neutrino quantum kinetics. The ansatz agrees well with multiangle and multienergy solutions of the kinetic equations, independently of the mass ordering.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem: any completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is exactly blind to a Majorana phase in open two-flavour oscillations. A rate anisotropy between the transverse decoherence rates can supply an environmental phase reference. This sets the conditions under which decoherence could in principle reveal a Majorana phase.

Constraining ultralight vector bosons via solar neutrino oscillations: The gauged L μ - L τ model and prospects for JUNO and XLZD

The authors extend the Standard Model with a gauged Lμ−Lτ symmetry whose ultralight vector boson, through kinetic mixing with the photon, generates an effective matter potential in the Sun and modifies MSW oscillations. They use recent solar neutrino measurements from Borexino, SNO and Super-Kamiokande, and discuss prospects for JUNO and XLZD. Solar oscillations are thereby used as a probe of ultralight mediators.

Exploring anomaly-free dark photon models through solar neutrino-electron scattering in ton-scale dark matter detectors

The paper derives exclusion limits on anomaly-free U(1)X dark photon models with kinetic and mass mixing, using low-energy solar neutrino-electron scattering data from PandaX-4T and XENONnT. The limits across six benchmark models correlate with the leptonic charge assignments. Model-independent limits are also given for the pure kinetic and pure mass mixing cases, showing that ton-scale dark matter detectors can constrain neutrino-sector mediators.
