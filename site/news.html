Experiments and results

DUNE progress and outreach

Fermilab-led DUNE was included among the twelve most ambitious international science projects, cited as an example of how modern science crosses borders and drives new technology. Separately, DUNE researchers are developing AI-based automated triggers to sift through the experiment's data volumes, identify neutrino interactions, flag possible signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector. Rice University's DUNE-TECH program is introducing undergraduates to the science behind the experiment.

Lake Vermilion detector

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion is set to begin this autumn, led by Fermilab. The novel underwater detection experiment continues Minnesota's long-running tradition of neutrino research.

IceCube DeepCore search

Four years after IceCube reported evidence for high-energy neutrinos from NGC 1068, an active galactic nucleus with a supermassive black hole, the collaboration is using DeepCore to search for steady-state neutrino emission from such sources. Because X-rays from around the black hole can escape its dense environment, they offer complementary information about its properties.

Search for steady-state neutrino emission using IceCube DeepCore — Read it
IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube continues its search for the sources of the high-energy astrophysical neutrinos first observed in 2013, having so far identified two galaxies and the Milky Way as neutrino sources. The collaboration is now searching for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena occurring on timescales of weeks.

IceCube extra dimensions

IceCube is being used to search for extra dimensions as a possible resolution of the hierarchy problem, the unexplained disparity between the strength of the weak force and gravity.

IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

The collaboration has projected the sensitivities expected from the IceCube Upgrade, building on evidence that active galaxies are among the sources of high-energy neutrinos and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration.

IceCube NSF renewal

The U.S. National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a five-year, $53 million contract covering 2026-2031.

T2K ND280 upgrade

The T2K collaboration has reported on the commissioning and performance of the new Time-of-Flight detector built for the ND280 upgrade, aimed at reducing systematic uncertainties in oscillation measurements and improving sensitivity to the CP-violating phase. The detector surrounds the SuperFGD and the two high-angle time-projection chambers and provides timing and directional information for particle identification and background rejection.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • A new analysis presents the first combined oscillation fit to atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters. The combination, previously thought infeasible outside the experimental collaborations themselves, finds no significant tension between the datasets under a single physics model and prefers the normal mass ordering at 3σ significance.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining current neutrinoless double-beta decay results with ab initio nuclear-theory matrix elements computed via the in-medium similarity renormalization group, this work derives global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to phenomenological nuclear models, the ab initio calculations indicate that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass scale implied by oscillation data.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper describes TAMBO, a proposed mountain-based neutrino observatory intended to help map the high-energy neutrino sky, a goal that has proven difficult for the field over the past decade despite the growth of neutrino astronomy.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, and discusses possible interpretations in terms of active galactic nuclei, transient sources, and cosmogenic neutrinos. Despite extensive multimessenger follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been identified.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using a full many-body calculation rather than the usual mean-field treatment, this work shows that neutrino helicity (spin-flip) conversion in dense neutrino environments can be enhanced by orders of magnitude beyond mean-field predictions, an effect tied to many-body momentum exchange that forward-only calculations miss.

    Neutrino helicity oscillations in astrophysical environments: A many-body approach — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
