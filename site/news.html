This week's neutrino news is dominated by infrastructure and programme milestones rather than new measurements: IceCube's operations funding has been renewed through 2026-2031, Fermilab is preparing a new underwater detector for the Great Lakes, and DUNE is developing automated tools for its future data analysis. On the theory side, a combined atmospheric-oscillation analysis reports a preference for the normal mass ordering, alongside new papers on double-beta-decay mass limits, a proposed high-energy neutrino observatory, supernova paleodetectors, invisible neutrino decay, and models for the origin of neutrino mass. Experiments and results

IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube continues its search for astrophysical neutrino sources that vary on timescales of weeks, including tidal disruption events. The observatory has previously identified two galaxies as neutrino sources and detected neutrinos from the Milky Way, and this effort extends that hunt to disruption events specifically.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
IceCube extra-dimensions search

IceCube is being used to search for evidence of extra spatial dimensions, motivated by the long-standing hierarchy problem, the vast disparity between the strength of the weak force and gravity. The analysis draws on IceCube's sensitivity to probe scenarios that could help explain this discrepancy.

Searching for extra dimensions with IceCube — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

The IceCube Collaboration has released projected sensitivities for the IceCube Upgrade, building on evidence that active galaxies are among the sources of high-energy neutrinos and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration. The Upgrade is intended to extend this line of source-hunting.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
IceCube operations renewed

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin-Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
Lake Vermilion detector

Fermilab is leading a new underwater neutrino detection experiment in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research. Testing is set to begin this autumn.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
DUNE automated triggers

DUNE researchers are preparing for the experiment's future data analysis by developing AI-based automated trigger systems. These are intended to sort through large data volumes, identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector.

US lab to hunt most elusive ‘ghost particles’ deep underground with automated triggers — Read it

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • Eller presents the first combined oscillation analysis using atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters. Such cross-collaboration combinations have long been considered infeasible, and the fit shows no significant tensions between datasets while preferring the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3 sigma.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Shickele, Jokiniemi, Belley and collaborators combine results from multiple neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed via ab initio chiral effective field theory to derive global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, their ab initio treatment indicates the current generation of 0νββ experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass region of interest.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Argüelles, Bazo, Briceño and collaborators describe TAMBO, a proposed mountain-based, deep-valley neutrino observatory intended to help complete the still-incomplete map of the high-energy neutrino sky. They also discuss the observatory's potential to reveal clues of new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Yamasaki and Nakazato assess the prospects of detecting neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae through nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals, so-called paleodetectors. They account for the diversity of collapse outcomes, including variations in neutron star mass and failed supernovae that form black holes, and the nuclear equation of state, finding enhanced sensitivity for the more compact remnants and larger neutrino emission associated with them.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Dev, Gehrlein, Sengupta and collaborators propose a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that enforces one neutrino to be exactly massless within a rank-two Dirac neutrino mass matrix, while remaining consistent with oscillation data and cosmological constraints. Anomaly cancellation together with a discrete symmetry fixes the minimal dark-sector completion needed to realise the scenario.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Dey and Dutta study how the DUNE and NOvA experiments, individually and in combination, could constrain invisible decay of the neutrino mass state ν3 into a sterile state under the normal mass hierarchy. They find that the true value of θ23 strongly affects the achievable constraint on the decay parameter, with the higher octant improving the bound.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI
