DUNE construction

Crews from Fermilab, CERN and SURF have started building the first of two cryostats, the insulated containers that will hold liquid argon for the experiment. The cryostats will house approximately 17,000 tons of liquid argon. Work nearly a mile underground in South Dakota currently consists of assembling large steel beams to form the outer structure of the detector modules.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper computes neutrino emissivity from Δ-direct-Urca processes in neutron stars, where Δ isobars may appear in the dense core alongside nucleons and hyperons. It addresses a contribution to neutron-star cooling that, according to the abstract, had not been explicitly evaluated. This matters for anyone using neutrino emission as an input to thermal evolution.

Toward measuring the low-energy cosmic-ray spectrum with next-generation neutrino detectors

The authors study whether kiloton-scale detectors such as Hyper-Kamiokande could constrain the primary cosmic-ray spectrum through the atmospheric neutrino flux. They report that future detectors can distinguish between cosmic-ray models with high statistical significance, even allowing for uncertainties in cross sections and hadronic interactions. It recasts atmospheric neutrinos as a probe of cosmic rays, not only as a background.

Neutrino production at the extreme vicinity of the supermassive black hole in NGC 1068

Motivated by updated IceCube data for NGC 1068, the paper infers the conditions for neutrino production near the supermassive black hole using hadronic channels and accompanying cascade gamma rays. It explores the parameter space with Latin hypercube sampling, constrained by multimessenger observations. It is relevant to understanding where the first identified extragalactic neutrino source produces its neutrinos.

Exploring anomaly-free dark photon models through solar neutrino-electron scattering in ton-scale dark matter detectors

The paper analyses solar neutrino–electron scattering data from PandaX-4T and XENONnT to constrain anomaly-free dark photon models with kinetic and mass mixing. It derives exclusion limits for six benchmark models and finds that their stringency correlates with the leptonic charge assignments. Dark matter detectors thus serve as neutrino-physics probes of new gauge bosons.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The authors prove a no-go theorem: open two-flavour neutrino oscillations with evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane cannot reveal a Majorana phase. They also show how a rate anisotropy can supply an environmental phase reference. This clarifies when decoherence could in principle make the Majorana phase observable in oscillations.

Quasisteady flavor configuration of multienergy neutrino ensembles

The paper presents semianalytic approximations for the quasisteady outcome of slow and fast flavour conversion in neutrino-dense environments, which hydrodynamic simulations cannot treat with full quantum kinetics. The ansatz is reported to agree well with multiangle and multienergy solutions of the kinetic equations, independent of mass ordering. It offers a subgrid prescription for core-collapse supernova modelling.
