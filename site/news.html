Experiments and results

Halzen Nobel Prize

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced on 6 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison. The IceCube collaboration describes him as its principal investigator and quotes the citation as being "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin". Fermilab News and Physics World both reported the award, the latter describing it as recognition for work detecting cosmic neutrinos.

IceCube has posted a summary of a search for the source of the highest-energy neutrino candidate yet detected, which the KM3NeT Collaboration reported at 220 PeV. Both IceCube and KM3NeT ARCA are neutrino telescopes that search for the sources of astrophysical neutrinos. The excerpt of the post does not state the outcome of the search.

Galactic centre with HAWC

IceCube and HAWC data have been used together to study the centre of the Galaxy, according to a new IceCube post. The region holds dense gas and potential accelerators, including a supermassive black hole, and is of interest for particles accelerated to PeV energies. It offers a view of how cosmic rays reach such energies. The excerpt does not give the result.

DUNE cryostat steel

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to build the cryostat for the Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment. That is nearly 2 million pounds of steel, which completely fills the south cavern. Separately, researchers from around the world met at Unicamp for a DUNE Collaboration Meeting to discuss progress of the project, which Fermilab leads.

Supernova background limit

PandaX-4T reported what it calls the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background using coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. Using an exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs, the collaboration observed no significant excess over the expected background. It set a 90% confidence-level upper limit of (2.27–2.43)×10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 per flavour on the ν_x flux for neutrino energies above 19.3 MeV.

Search for Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background of All Flavors with PandaX-4T — arXiv
JUNO splittings relation

A preprint by I. Alikhanov takes the JUNO Collaboration's reported measurements of Δm²21 and Δm²31, which it describes as the most precise to date, and tests an empirically proposed relation between the two splittings. The author reports that the data give 1.4143 (+0.0036, −0.0038) for the relevant combination, against a predicted value of √2, at a relative precision of 0.27%. This is the author's own analysis of the JUNO results, not a JUNO publication.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The paper calculates two-neutrino double-beta decay rates without the usual approximations that decouple nuclear structure from the kinematics of the emitted leptons. It presents deviations of decay rates and electron spectra from closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion treatments for 82Se and 136Xe. Anyone interpreting double-beta decay spectra or modelling the two-neutrino background to neutrinoless searches has reason to look at it.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors ask whether paleodetectors, which record nuclear-recoil tracks in ancient minerals, could detect neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae. They vary neutron-star masses, include failed supernovae that form black holes, and consider the role of the nuclear equation of state. They quantify the gain in sensitivity for models with larger neutrino emission, which bears on how far such minerals could probe past collapses.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper considers magnetic and oscillation effects on neutrinos emitted from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. It finds that oscillations in the outer layers of the star harden the electron-antineutrino spectrum significantly. The effect is most pronounced for the inverted mass ordering and favours ground-based detection, so a future Galactic supernova signal could carry information on the ordering.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors build composite neutrino models in which heavy neutrinos arise as bound states of a near-conformal strongly coupled sector and mix with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. They find transition dipole couplings of order 10^-6 to 10^-8 GeV^-1, parametrically larger than the loop-level predictions of minimal Dirac or Majorana models. This makes electromagnetic signatures of heavy neutrinos a possible test of such models, and the paper includes an event-level simulation of the production-and-decay signal.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, where masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model accommodates sub-eV neutrino masses even with new physics at the TeV scale. The authors examine whether thermal leptogenesis works in this setting, which differs from canonical seesaw leptogenesis.

    Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors investigate non-standard neutrino–two-photon interactions at a future high-energy muon collider, using dimension-7 effective operators and the process μ−γ → μ−νν̄ with a Weizsäcker–Williams photon. The signal and background are simulated with FeynRules, MadGraph5_aMC@NLO and PYTHIA 8. It shows how a collider could probe neutrino electromagnetic properties, a channel complementary to low-energy experiments.

    Investigation of neutrino electromagnetic properties at muon antimuon collider — INSPIRE · DOI
