Experiments and results

IceCube DeepCore

Analysis of 11.1 years of DeepCore data delivered a competitive sub-TeV time-integrated neutrino search, covering active galactic nuclei with high intrinsic X-ray flux (including NGC 1068), Galactic objects identified by Fermi-LAT with a pion-decay-like spectral shape, and an all-sky point-source scan. The DeepCore subarray extends IceCube's sensitivity down to sub-TeV energies, which is especially useful for sources with soft spectra. Four years ago IceCube reported evidence for high-energy neutrinos from NGC 1068, an active galactic nucleus with a central supermassive black hole.

IceCube toward KM3-230213A

IceCube searched 15 years of its own data for high-energy neutrino emission from the sky location of the roughly 200 PeV event that KM3NeT's ARCA detector recorded in February 2023, while ARCA was still under construction. Testing steady, flaring, and arbitrary-time hypotheses, the collaboration found no evidence of emission from that direction and set upper limits instead.

IceCube operations renewed

The US National Science Foundation renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to manage and operate the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers continued operation and maintenance for 2026-2031.

DUNE data triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI-based automated trigger systems to help sort through the large volumes of data the experiment will collect deep underground. The systems are intended to identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector.

Lake Vermilion detector

A Fermilab-led underwater neutrino detection experiment is set to begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

NOvA Test Beam

NOvA operated a dedicated Test Beam experiment at the Fermilab Test Beam Facility from April 2019 to July 2022, using a 30-ton segmented liquid scintillator detector functionally identical to the NOvA Near and Far Detectors. The programme analysed tagged particles from proton-copper collisions to sharpen understanding of the systematic uncertainties in detector response and calibration that underpin NOvA's oscillation, mass-ordering and CP-violation measurements.

The NOvA Test Beam Experiment — arXiv
PandaX-4T beta decay limit

Using 148.4 kg·yr of xenon-136 exposure collected during commissioning and the first science runs, PandaX-4T searched for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of Xe-136, a process whose observation, in the absence of neutrinoless double beta decay, would point to Dirac neutrinos. No excess over background was seen, and the collaboration set a lower limit on the half-life of 6.01×10^24 years at 90% confidence, the most stringent constraint on this process in xenon to date.

Search for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of ¹³⁶Xe in PandaX-4T detector — arXiv
NEXT-100 topology

The NEXT-100 high-pressure xenon time-projection chamber completed its first physics run at the Laboratorio Subterráneo de Canfranc, operating at roughly 4 bar. Researchers report the first validation of the detector's topological event-discrimination performance and the first topological analysis at low pressure, a capability central to NEXT's neutrinoless double beta decay programme.

JUNO information limits

A new study applies quantum and classical Fisher information to quantify how precisely JUNO can determine oscillation parameters from reactor antineutrino data, tracking how information carried by the neutrino state is lost as it moves through detection and analysis to the observed events. The authors establish an information ladder for JUNO and show that this loss of precision depends strongly on which oscillation parameter is being estimated.

Quantifying Information Hierarchy for Neutrino Oscillation Parameters at JUNO — arXiv
IceCube tidal disruption

IceCube continues to search for neutrino emission tied to transient astrophysical events on week-to-month timescales, including tidal disruption events, building on its identification of two galaxies as steady neutrino sources and its detection of neutrinos from the Milky Way.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • Eller presents the first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters. The unified model describes all four datasets without significant tensions between them and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance, a result of direct interest to anyone tracking the mass-ordering question.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper introduces TAMBO, a proposed mountain-based neutrino observatory designed to help fill the gap in mapping the high-energy neutrino sky. The valley-based detector concept targets tau neutrino air showers and is presented as a route to both new source identification and new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Shickele and collaborators combine likelihoods from current neutrinoless double-beta decay searches with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio, within the in-medium similarity renormalization group approach, to set global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. Unlike more phenomenological nuclear models, their ab initio calculation indicates that the current generation of 0νββ experiments likely has not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the target mass range.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Distefano reviews the properties and interpretation of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV. The review surveys astrophysical and cosmological explanations, including active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic origin, and notes that no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been found despite extensive follow-up.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Dey and Dutta study how well combined NOvA and DUNE data could constrain a scenario in which the neutrino mass state ν3 decays invisibly into a sterile state, under the normal mass ordering. They find that the true value of θ23 strongly affects the achievable constraint on the decay parameter, with the higher octant giving a notably tighter bound.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Yamasaki and Nakazato assess whether nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals could record neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae, accounting for the range of possible neutron star masses, failed supernovae that form black holes instead, and the nuclear equation of state. They show that more compact remnants and more energetic core collapses leave a stronger imprint, making such paleodetectors a potential archive of the Galaxy's supernova history.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
