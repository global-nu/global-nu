Nobel Prize: Halzen

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences has awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison. The IceCube post identifies him as the observatory's principal investigator and gives the citation as being for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. Physics World describes the award as being for his work detecting cosmic neutrinos.

DUNE cryostat steel

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to construct the DUNE cryostat. That is nearly 2 million pounds of steel, which completely fills the south cavern. Separately, Fermilab News reports that researchers gathered at Unicamp for a DUNE Collaboration Meeting to discuss the progress of the project.

PandaX-4T supernova background

PandaX-4T, a liquid xenon detector at the China Jinping Underground Laboratory, made the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background that uses coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. With a total exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs, it observed no significant event excess over the expected background. It set an upper limit of (2.27 – 2.43) × 10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 per flavour at 90% confidence level on the nu_x flux, for incident energies above 19.3 MeV.

JUNO mass-splitting relation

A single-author preprint by I. Alikhanov takes the most precise measurements to date of both mass-squared differences, which it says the JUNO Collaboration has now reported. It tests an empirical relation between the splittings that was predicted earlier. The author reports that the relation agrees excellently with the data, giving 1.4143 (+0.0036, -0.0038), a relative precision of 0.27%. This is the author's analysis, not a JUNO result.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. A search for astrophysical point-like sources in a 5-year track-like dataset from 2019-2024 found no significant source. The authors compute upper limits for a selection of candidate objects and compare them with published results from IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT.

IceCube and HAWC

IceCube and HAWC data are used together to investigate the centre of the Galaxy. The region contains a large amount of dense gas and potential accelerators, including a supermassive black hole, and offers a window into how cosmic rays are accelerated to extremely high energies. Particles accelerated to PeV energies are of particular interest.

T2K near detector upgrade

The ND280 upgrade was completed in 2024 and has significantly enhanced the near detector's tracking capabilities. A new selection targets muon-neutrino charged-current interactions with single positively charged pion production in the new modules, to constrain interaction models. A separate paper proposes a deep neural network to distinguish electrons, photons, muons, pions and protons in SuperFGD, the highly granular scintillator target of the upgraded detector.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The authors calculate two-neutrino double-beta decay rates without decoupling nuclear structure from the emitted lepton kinematics, treating the full lepton-energy dependence in a unified formula. They compare decay rates and electron spectra with closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion approximations for 82Se and 136Xe. The comparison bears on how realistically the two-neutrino mode is described, which matters for interpreting double-beta decay searches.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper investigates whether nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals (paleodetectors) could record neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae. It varies neutron star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the nuclear equation of state. The sensitivity is enhanced for models with larger neutrino emission, which gives a geological route to supernova neutrinos.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper considers magnetic and oscillation effects on neutrinos emitted from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. It finds that oscillations in the outer layers of the star significantly harden the electron antineutrino spectrum. The enhancement is particularly pronounced for the inverted mass ordering, so the spectrum could in principle carry information on the ordering.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors study thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, in which neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model gives sub-eV neutrino masses even for a new-physics scale of order TeV. The paper asks whether leptogenesis works in this setting, where it differs from canonical seesaw-based leptogenesis.

    Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies composite neutrino models in which heavy neutrinos are bound states of a near-conformal strongly coupled sector, mixing with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. Matching onto the low-energy theory gives transition dipole operators with couplings of 10^-6 to 10^-8 GeV^-1, which the authors describe as parametrically larger than loop-level predictions of minimal Dirac or Majorana models. They carry out an event-level simulation of the production-and-decay signature.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors build a time-dependent, multienergy framework for neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae. It includes MeV neutrinos from thermonuclear runaway and GeV neutrinos from hadronic interactions in nova-driven shocks. They use RS Oph as a benchmark and model the shock interacting with both the red giant wind and a dense accretion disk around the white dwarf.
