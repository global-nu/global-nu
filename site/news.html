Experiments and results

IceCube extra dimensions search

IceCube has been examining its data for possible signatures of extra spatial dimensions, one candidate explanation for the hierarchy problem — the puzzle of why the weak force is so many orders of magnitude stronger than gravity. The collaboration frames the discrepancy as leaving major gaps in current theoretical understanding of particle physics.

Searching for extra dimensions with IceCube — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

Since the 2013 discovery of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, IceCube has built evidence that active galaxies are among their sources and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration. A new study projects the sensitivity of the planned IceCube Upgrade for continuing this search for neutrino sources.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
IceCube operations funding

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to manage and operate IceCube, covering the period 2026-2031 with $53 million over five years. The agreement funds continued operation and maintenance of the observatory.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
DUNE AI triggering

DUNE researchers are developing AI systems to prepare for data analysis, aiming to sort through large volumes of data, identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector. The effort draws on decades of machine-learning work at Fermilab and is described as part of integrating artificial intelligence into the experiment's operations.

Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detector, led by Fermilab, is set to begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn. The project continues Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
NOvA sterile search

NOvA has carried out its first sterile-neutrino search to combine neutrino- and antineutrino-mode beam data, fitting its near and far detectors simultaneously within a three-active-plus-one-sterile model, with exposures of 26.61×10^20 and 12.50×10^20 protons on target respectively. The analysis finds no evidence for sterile neutrinos and excludes parameter space previously allowed by other experiments, including most of the region allowed by IceCube.

NOvA neutron modelling

Using its high-statistics near-detector antineutrino sample, NOvA has examined how well simulations reproduce the low-energy neutron candidates that complicate energy reconstruction in neutrino interactions. The study reports an excess of simulated neutron candidates with low energy depositions relative to data, pointing to model dependence in neutron-related systematic uncertainties.

Neutron detector response modeling in NOvA — arXiv
KM3NeT source stacking

KM3NeT/ARCA has carried out a stacking search for high-energy astrophysical neutrinos from two candidate source populations, ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and blazars, using 336 days of data from an array of 19-21 detection units and a sample of 1544 track-like neutrino candidates. The ultra-luminous infrared galaxy sample comprised 75 sources, modelled with conventional power-law spectra.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • TAMBO, a mountain-based, deep-valley neutrino observatory, is presented as a way to help complete the still-incomplete map of the high-energy neutrino sky after a decade of progress in neutrino astronomy. The authors frame the instrument as a possible route to clues of new physics as well.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio in the in-medium similarity renormalization group approach, the authors derive global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, their ab initio results indicate that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass region of interest.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper assesses whether nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals — paleodetectors — could register neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae, accounting for the diversity of outcomes from neutron-star-forming collapses to failed supernovae that form black holes. Sensitivity is found to depend on the nuclear equation of state, through its effect on neutron star radii and maximum mass, and is enhanced for more compact remnants and larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors revisit accretion-disk winds from active galactic nuclei as a possible contributor to the cosmic neutrino and gamma-ray backgrounds, building a lepto-hadronic wind model calibrated against radio and GeV gamma-ray data from nearby Seyfert galaxies detected by Fermi-LAT. Cosmic rays accelerated at the wind's forward and reverse shocks are shown to produce synchrotron, external-Compton and hadronic emission feeding into the predicted background levels.

    Revisiting Disk Winds in Active Galactic Nuclei as an Origin of Cosmic Gamma-Ray and … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Facing the tension between one-zone leptohadronic blazar jet models and strict X-ray constraints, the authors derive an analytical expression for the maximum neutrino flux a blazar can produce as a function of its observed X-ray flux, and test it against full numerical modelling for a sample of neutrino candidate blazars. The analytical and numerical results agree, giving a quick way to bound plausible neutrino output from blazar sources.

    On the Maximum Neutrino Flux of Blazars in the One-zone Leptohadronic Model — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using the GENIE event generator, the authors benchmark a range of nucleon form factors, quasielastic cross-section calculations and nuclear models for pionless charged-current neutrino-argon scattering against recent MicroBooNE measurements. Some model combinations separate clearly from the data, giving argon-based experiments clearer grounds for choosing among competing interaction models.

    Benchmarking state-of-the-art theory and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
