Experiments and results

DeepCore steady-state search

A new time-integrated analysis used 11.1 years of IceCube-DeepCore data, which extends the detector's reach down to sub-TeV energies, to search for steady neutrino emission. The study covered active galaxies with high intrinsic X-ray flux (including NGC 1068, previously identified as a source of high-energy neutrinos), Galactic objects with gamma-ray spectra consistent with neutral-pion decay, and an all-sky scan for point sources.

Search for steady-state neutrino emission using IceCube DeepCore — Read itTime-Integrated Searches for Sub-TeV Neutrino Sources with IceCube-DeepCore — arXiv
Tidal disruption event search

IceCube has continued its search for neutrinos from transient astrophysical sources, this time looking at tidal disruption events, in which a star is torn apart by a black hole. The search builds on IceCube's prior identification of two galaxies and the Milky Way as neutrino sources.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
Search for extra dimensions

IceCube has carried out a search for signatures of extra spatial dimensions, motivated by the long-standing hierarchy problem of why the weak force so greatly exceeds gravity in strength.

Searching for extra dimensions with IceCube — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

A study presents projected sensitivities for the planned IceCube Upgrade, in the context of IceCube's existing evidence that active galaxies are among the sources of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
NSF renews IceCube contract

The U.S. National Science Foundation renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers the 2026-2031 period.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
DUNE automated triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI systems to process the experiment's large data volumes, aiming to identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector as part of preparations for data analysis.

US lab to hunt most elusive ‘ghost particles’ deep underground with automated triggers — Read it
Lake Vermilion detector begins

A Fermilab-led underwater neutrino detection experiment, the Lake Vermilion neutrino detector, is set to begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
PandaX-4T quadruple beta decay

PandaX-4T searched for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of xenon-136 using 148.4 kg·yr of exposure collected during commissioning and the first science runs. No significant excess over background was seen, and the collaboration set a lower limit on the decay half-life of 6.01×10^24 years at 90% confidence, the most stringent constraint on this process in xenon to date.

Search for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of ¹³⁶Xe in PandaX-4T detector — arXiv
NOvA Test Beam results

NOvA reports on its Test Beam experiment, which used a 30-ton segmented liquid scintillator detector functionally identical to the NOvA Near and Far Detectors to analyse tagged particles from proton-copper collisions at the Fermilab Test Beam Facility between April 2019 and July 2022. The programme addresses systematic uncertainties in detector response and calibration that underpin NOvA's oscillation measurements.

The NOvA Test Beam Experiment — arXiv
NEXT-100 topological analysis

The NEXT-100 high-pressure xenon time projection chamber completed its first physics run at the Laboratorio Subterráneo de Canfranc, operating at roughly 4 bar. The collaboration reports the first validation of the detector's topological identification performance and the first topological analysis at low pressure, capabilities central to NEXT's goal of detecting neutrinoless double beta decay.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • TAMBO is a proposed mountain-based observatory designed to help map the high-energy neutrino sky in a region that has so far proven difficult to survey with existing instruments. Its proponents argue the observatory could also turn up clues to physics beyond the current picture.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper performs the first combined oscillation fit across atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, something long considered infeasible outside individual collaborations. Fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters, the unified model shows no significant tension between the datasets and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory, this Bayesian analysis derives global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe that mass scale.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event recorded by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, and surveys the astrophysical and cosmological scenarios proposed to explain it, including active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos. Despite extensive follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been identified.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • A full many-body calculation of neutrino helicity (spin-flip) conversion in dense neutrino environments finds that conversion probabilities can exceed, by orders of magnitude, those obtained from standard mean-field treatments. The enhancement stems from many-body momentum exchange, an effect missed by calculations that consider only forward scattering, with relevance to astrophysical settings where neutrino densities are high.

    Neutrino helicity oscillations in astrophysical environments: A many-body approach — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This model generates neutrino mass radiatively while allowing a Dirac dark-matter candidate to be boosted through semi-annihilation into an anti-dark-matter particle plus a neutrino. The authors find this boosted dark matter could be probed by DARWIN if its mass lies between 20 and 50 MeV and the semi-annihilation proceeds through a finely tuned resonance, producing a monochromatic neutrino signal.

    A radiative neutrino mass model with semi-annihilating boosted dark matter — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
