Experiments and results

Nobel Prize in Physics

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison, principal investigator of IceCube. The citation reads "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin". CERN describes the work as the discovery of high-energy neutrinos coming from far beyond our Solar System, and as paving the way for a new generation of observatories.

Super-Kamiokande reactor antineutrinos

Super-Kamiokande reports a first observation of reactor electron antineutrinos, using SK-Gd data with a total exposure of 411.52 live days. The prompt energy spectra and monthly event rates were consistent with the oscillated reactor prediction after accounting for accidental, geoneutrino and spallation backgrounds. The no-reactor hypothesis is disfavoured at approximately 6σ.

First observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors at Super-Kamiokande — arXiv
SBND detector paper

The SBND Collaboration describes the as-built detector, a 112 tonne active-mass liquid argon time projection chamber in the Booster Neutrino Beam at Fermilab and part of the Short-Baseline Neutrino Program. SBND began operation in 2024 and has accumulated what the paper calls the world's largest sample of neutrino-argon interactions. All detector systems performed at or above design requirements. The electron lifetime exceeded 30 ms, ten times the design requirement, for more than 95% of Run 1.

Short-Baseline Near Detector (SBND): Design and Initial Performance — arXiv

PandaX-4T supernova background

PandaX-4T presents the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background using coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering, at the China Jinping Underground Laboratory. With a total exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs, it observes no significant excess over the expected background. It sets an upper limit of (2.27–2.43)×10³ cm⁻²s⁻¹ per flavour at 90% confidence level on the ν_x flux for neutrino energies above 19.3 MeV.

Search for Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background of All Flavors with PandaX-4T — arXiv
Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal, now 70% complete, and has taken data in partial configurations since 2016. A search for point-like astrophysical sources in a five-year track-like event sample from 2019–2024 found no significant source. Upper limits are computed for a selection of candidate objects and compared with published results from IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT.

Search for Point-Like Neutrino Sources with Baikal-GVD — INSPIRE · DOI
T2K near-detector upgrade

The upgrade of the ND280 near detector was completed in 2024 and has enhanced its tracking capabilities. A new event selection targets muon-neutrino charged-current interactions with single positively charged pion production in the new detector modules. It is described as a step towards reducing the dominant systematic uncertainties in T2K's oscillation analysis, which stem from neutrino-nucleus interaction modelling.

  • The authors calculate two-neutrino double-beta decay rates with the full interdependence of nuclear structure and lepton kinematics in one formula, without the decoupling approximations used so far. They compare the resulting rates and electron spectra with closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion treatments for ⁸²Se and ⁷⁶Xe-region isotope ¹³⁶Xe. This matters to double-beta-decay experiments because the two-neutrino spectrum is the background to the neutrinoless search.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper asks whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be seen as nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals, known as paleodetectors. It varies neutron-star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the role of the nuclear equation of state. The sensitivity enhancement is quantified for models with larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors consider magnetic and oscillation effects on neutrinos emitted from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. They find that the electron-antineutrino spectrum hardens significantly through oscillations in the outer layers of the star. The enhancement is particularly pronounced for the inverted mass ordering and is described as favourable for ground-based detection of supernova neutrinos.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies thermal leptogenesis in a model with a scalar quadruplet and a pair of vectorlike fermion triplets, where neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model accommodates sub-eV neutrino masses with new physics at the TeV scale. Leptogenesis here differs from the canonical seesaw case, which is the point of the study.

    Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors model heavy neutrinos as bound states of a near-conformal strongly coupled sector that mixes with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. They find enhanced electromagnetic transition dipole operators, parametrically larger than loop-level predictions of minimal Dirac or Majorana models, and carry out an event-level simulation of the production-and-decay signature. It gives experimentalists a concrete target for dipole-portal searches.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper builds a time-dependent framework for neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae, covering MeV neutrinos from thermonuclear runaway and GeV neutrinos from hadronic interactions in nova-driven shocks. It uses RS Oph as a benchmark and models the shock interacting with both the red giant wind and a dense accretion disk. Neutrino astronomers get a multienergy prediction for a class of transient source.
