Experiments and results

DUNE preparations

Researchers on the Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment are developing AI-based automated triggers to sort through the large volumes of data expected once the detector is running, aiming to identify neutrino interactions, flag signs of a stellar explosion, and catch problems inside the detector itself. Separately, Rice University's DUNE-TECH programme is introducing undergraduates to the science behind the experiment.

US lab to hunt most elusive ‘ghost particles’ deep underground with automated triggers — Read itParticles of Confederation — Read it
IceCube transient search

IceCube continues its search for the sources of the high-energy astrophysical neutrinos first observed in 2013, having so far identified two galaxies as neutrino sources and detected neutrinos from the Milky Way. The collaboration is now searching specifically for neutrino emission associated with tidal disruption events, transient phenomena occurring on timescales of weeks.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
IceCube extra dimensions

IceCube data are being used to search for evidence of extra dimensions, one possible explanation for the hierarchy problem — the enormous discrepancy between the strength of the weak force and that of gravity.

Searching for extra dimensions with IceCube — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivity

The collaboration has released projected sensitivities for the planned IceCube Upgrade, building on the observatory's existing evidence that active galaxies are sources of high-energy neutrinos and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
IceCube funding renewed

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain IceCube, covering 2026 to 2031 with $53 million in funding.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment led by Fermilab will begin testing this fall in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • A combined oscillation analysis brings together atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1,536 bins with 91 parameters. The unified model describes all four datasets without significant tensions between them, and the combination prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance — a combination long thought infeasible outside the individual collaborations.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining results from current neutrinoless double-beta decay searches with ab initio nuclear matrix elements computed via the in-medium similarity renormalization group, this study derives global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to more phenomenological nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that the present generation of double-beta decay experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the targeted mass region.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper lays out the physics case for TAMBO, a proposed mountain-based observatory designed to help map the high-energy neutrino sky, a task that has proven difficult despite a decade of progress in neutrino astronomy. The authors argue the observatory could also yield clues to new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This model embeds radiative neutrino mass generation in a dark sector where a Dirac dark matter particle can semi-annihilate into an anti-dark matter particle plus a neutrino, producing boosted dark matter today. The authors find the resulting monochromatic neutrino and boosted dark matter signal could be probed by DARWIN for dark matter masses between 20 and 50 MeV.

    A radiative neutrino mass model with semi-annihilating boosted dark matter — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using a full many-body treatment rather than the usual mean-field approximation, this study shows that neutrino helicity (spin-flip) oscillations in dense astrophysical environments can be enhanced by orders of magnitude beyond mean-field predictions. The enhancement arises from many-body momentum exchange that forward-only calculations miss, with implications for neutrino transport in such environments.

    Neutrino helicity oscillations in astrophysical environments: A many-body approach — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that forces one neutrino to be exactly massless while remaining consistent with oscillation and cosmological data, via a rank-two Dirac neutrino mass matrix. Anomaly cancellation together with a discrete symmetry fixes the required dark-sector content, giving a specific gauge-origin explanation for the neutrino mass pattern.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
