Gravitational-wave neutrino searches

The ANTARES and KM3NeT collaborations present two searches that combine LIGO-Virgo gravitational-wave data from the third observing run with data from ANTARES and ORCA. In the first, high-energy neutrino candidates serve as triggers to look for gravitational-wave candidates. The second looks for spatial and temporal coincidences between subthreshold candidates in the two data sets.

Fast X-ray transients in IceCube data

Two authors, using the publicly available 14 years of IceCube muon-track data, searched for neutrinos from 27 fast X-ray transients detected by Einstein Probe. They found no statistically significant excess. The largest test statistic, 4.77, was for EP240801a, with a local p-value of 0.04, and they set 90% confidence level upper limits on the muon neutrino flux.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD, a cubic-kilometre-scale detector in Lake Baikal, is now 70% complete and has taken data in partial configurations since 2016. A point-source search on a 5-year track-like dataset from 2019-2024 found no significant source, so upper limits were computed for selected candidate objects. The results are compared with published ones from IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT.

T2K near detector upgrade

A key part of the ND280 upgrade is SuperFGD, a segmented plastic scintillator detector made of about 2 million optically isolated 1 cm³ cubes read out by three orthogonal wavelength-shifting fibres. Chlorine in seawater

A proposal for MeV antineutrino detection in large water-based telescopes such as KM3NeT notes that neutron capture on chlorine, naturally present in seawater, gives a gamma-ray cascade of about 8.6 MeV on a shorter time scale than capture on hydrogen. This offers a distinctive delayed-coincidence signature for inverse beta decay. The study uses measured seawater properties at the TRIDENT site and a detector-level simulation.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper examines how Δ isobars, which may be present in the cores of massive neutron stars, contribute to neutrino emission through Δ-direct-Urca processes. Its authors note that this contribution had not been explicitly determined, although nucleonic and hyperonic Urca processes are established as dominant cooling mechanisms. It matters for neutrino emissivity in neutron-star thermal evolution.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem: every completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is exactly blind to a Majorana phase in open two-flavour oscillations. A rate anisotropy in the environment can act as a phase reference. It delimits when decoherence could make the Majorana phase observable in oscillations.

Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full lepton-energy dependence

The authors compute the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate with the full interdependence of nuclear structure and lepton kinematics, avoiding the usual decoupling approximations. They present deviations of rates and electron spectra from closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion treatments for 82Se and 136Xe. This is relevant to anyone interpreting double-beta decay spectra.

Constraining ultralight vector bosons via solar neutrino oscillations: The gauged L μ - L τ model and prospects for JUNO and XLZD

The paper studies a gauged U(1) Lμ-Lτ model with an ultralight vector boson that, through kinetic mixing with the photon, creates an effective matter potential in the Sun and modifies MSW flavour conversion. Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass

The paper studies neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, where neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model gives sub-eV neutrino masses with a new-physics scale of order TeV. The authors examine whether thermal leptogenesis works in this framework, which differs from canonical seesaw leptogenesis.

Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses

The authors assess whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be seen as nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals (paleodetectors). They vary neutron-star masses, include failed supernovae that form black holes, and consider the nuclear equation of state. Sensitivity is enhanced for models with larger neutrino emission.
