The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Francis Halzen, principal investigator of IceCube, for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. Elsewhere this week KM3NeT reported further construction steps and recognition as an ESFRI Landmark, SBND published its design and first-year performance, and PandaX-4T reported a first coherent-scattering search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background. Experiments and results

Halzen Nobel Prize

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison. IceCube quotes the citation as being for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. CERN describes the work as paving the way for a new generation of observatories that use neutrinos as a messenger of extreme environments. IceCube also says Halzen proposed and proved that ice could be used as a detection medium.

KM3NeT construction

The collaboration reports a period of intense marine operations, with two further significant steps for ARCA and ORCA last week. At the ARCA deep-sea site, Cable Termination Frame 3 (CTF3) was installed during a campaign conducted by Alcatel Submarine Networks. It has also been recognised as a Landmark in the ESFRI Roadmap 2026, the European strategy forum's list of major research infrastructures.

SBND detector paper

The SBND Collaboration has posted a paper describing the as-built detector and its first year of beam operation. SBND is a 112 tonne active-mass liquid argon time projection chamber in the Booster Neutrino Beam at Fermilab and part of the Short-Baseline Neutrino Program. It began operation in 2024, and the authors report all detector systems at or above design requirements. The electron lifetime exceeded 30 ms, ten times the design requirement, for more than 95% of Run 1.

Short-Baseline Near Detector (SBND): Design and Initial Performance — arXiv
PandaX-4T supernova background

PandaX-4T reports the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background through coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. The analysis uses a total exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs. No significant event excess over the expected background was observed. The collaboration sets an upper limit of (2.27 - 2.43) × 10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 per flavour at 90% confidence level on the ν_x flux, for neutrino energies above 19.3 MeV.

Search for Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background of All Flavors with PandaX-4T — arXiv
Combined atmospheric fit

A new preprint combines the latest publicly available atmospheric data from Super-Kamiokande (stages before the gadolinium phase), IceCube-DeepCore and the six-detection-unit configuration of KM3NeT/ORCA. The fit uses all event samples at once, treats shared systematic uncertainties as fully correlated and models detector-response uncertainties separately for each experiment. The authors present the result as evidence for normal neutrino ordering.

Evidence for Normal Neutrino Ordering with Combined Atmospheric Neutrino Experiments — arXiv
JUNO and mass splittings

A single-author preprint states that the JUNO Collaboration has reported the most precise measurements to date of both Δm²_21 and Δm²_31. The author uses them to test an empirical relation between the two mass splittings that was proposed earlier, and reports it to be in excellent agreement with the data. This is the author's own analysis of JUNO's reported values, not a JUNO result.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The authors compute two-neutrino double-beta decay rates without the usual approximations that decouple nuclear structure from the emitted lepton kinematics. They compare the resulting rates and electron spectra with closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion treatments for ⁸²Se and ¹³⁶Xe. This matters for anyone interpreting double-beta spectra, since the two-neutrino mode is the irreducible background to neutrinoless searches.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper investigates whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected as nuclear-recoil tracks in ancient minerals, known as paleodetectors. It varies neutron-star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the role of the nuclear equation of state. It quantifies how sensitivity improves for models with larger neutrino emission, which is relevant to anyone interested in supernova neutrinos on geological timescales.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of the ultra-high-energy event KM3-230213A seen by KM3NeT, whose parent-neutrino energy is inferred to be of order 10^17 eV. It discusses interpretations involving active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos, together with multimessenger constraints. It also notes that, despite extensive follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been found.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper considers magnetic and oscillation effects on neutrinos emitted from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. It finds that oscillations in the outer layers of the star significantly harden the electron-antineutrino spectrum. The enhancement is particularly pronounced for the inverted mass ordering, which links the supernova signal to the ordering question.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. This paper searches for astrophysical point-like sources in a five-year track-like event sample collected in 2019-2024. No significant source was found, so upper limits are computed for selected candidate objects and compared with published IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT results.

    Search for Point-Like Neutrino Sources with Baikal-GVD — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This experimental review traces the development of high-energy neutrino astronomy, from the decades of effort needed to build the large instruments to the detection of a diffuse TeV–PeV flux and the first individual sources. It describes the present status of the field and sketches future developments. It is a useful overview for a reader outside the subfield.

    Neutrino Astronomy at High Energies-An Experimental Review — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
