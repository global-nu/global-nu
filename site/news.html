Experiments and results

JUNO oscillation measurement

An improved fit uses 207.2 live days and 8,294 inverse beta-decay candidates at an average reactor baseline of 52.5 km. The collaboration reports a precision of approximately 1% on the atmospheric mass splitting. It quotes Δm²31 = +2.509 (+0.027, −0.025) ×10⁻³ eV² for normal ordering and −2.482 (+0.025, −0.026) ×10⁻³ eV² for inverted ordering, using the Daya Bay constraint in the fit.

KM3NeT ARCA62

A sea campaign at the ARCA site over the previous two weeks advanced the construction of the detector. It enlarged the submarine infrastructure and the detector, and included an upgrade. The KM3NeT Collaboration describes it as a significant step forward.

KM3NeT record event

KM3NeT reported the highest-energy neutrino candidate ever detected, at 220 PeV. IceCube has now published a search for its source. The excerpt available here does not give the outcome of that search.

DUNE civil construction

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to build the DUNE cryostat. The steel fills the south cavern, which is 500 feet long and 65 feet wide. Separately, the collaboration met at Unicamp to discuss the progress of the project.

IceCube source searches

IceCube has posted summaries of three analyses. One is a steady-state search with DeepCore, which follows the evidence for neutrino emission from NGC 1068 announced four years ago. Another looks for emission from tidal disruption events. A third uses IceCube and HAWC data to study the Galactic centre as a site of PeV cosmic-ray acceleration. The excerpts do not state results.

MicroBooNE cross sections

The first double-differential cross sections for mesonless muon-neutrino charged-current interactions on argon with one or more protons have been presented. They are given as functions of proton multiplicity and kinematics. The analysis uses the full Booster Neutrino Beam dataset, 1.30×10²¹ protons on target.

MINERvA superscaling

The first measurement of the superscaling variable in neutrino–nucleus scattering was reported, using charged-current quasielastic-like muon-neutrino interactions on carbon in the NuMI medium-energy beam. The variable is reconstructed from visible final-state kinematics. Its peak position is sensitive to the invisible nuclear missing energy.

T2K ND280 upgrade

The ND280 upgrade was completed in 2024 and has improved the near detector's tracking capabilities. A new event selection targets muon-neutrino charged-current interactions with single positively charged pion production in the new modules. A separate paper proposes a neural network for particle identification in SuperFGD. Both aim to reduce the systematic uncertainties from neutrino-interaction modelling in T2K's oscillation analysis.

Baikal-GVD point sources

The detector is under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. A point-source search on a five-year track-like dataset from 2019–2024 found no significant source. Upper limits were computed for selected candidate objects and compared with published IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT results.

EXO-200 data release

A subset of calibration data has been made public for AI and machine-learning applications. It comprises approximately one million events from ²²⁸Th calibration campaigns between 26 February and 2 March 2018. For each event it includes raw waveforms from the U-wire, V-wire and APD channels, together with reconstructed quantities.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The paper presents what it calls the first combined oscillation analysis of recent atmospheric datasets from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA, together with Daya Bay reactor data. Such combinations were long considered infeasible outside the collaborations. The author finds no significant parameter tensions and a 3σ preference for normal over inverted mass ordering.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Majorana neutrino mass limits are derived by combining neutrinoless double-beta decay results with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory. In contrast to nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate that the current generation of experiments has likely not yet reached the required sensitivity. This matters for how close present searches are to the relevant mass region.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors calculate two-neutrino double-beta decay rates without decoupling nuclear structure from the lepton kinematics. They compare the resulting rates and electron spectra with closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion approximations for ⁸²Se and ¹³⁶Xe. This is relevant because the electron spectrum of the two-neutrino mode is the background in neutrinoless searches.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper asks whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected through nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals. It varies neutron star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the nuclear equation of state. Sensitivity is quantified as larger for models with greater neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors study composite neutrino models in which heavy neutrinos arise from a near-conformal strongly coupled sector and mix with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. They find transition dipole operators, with couplings d_μN ~ 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁸ GeV⁻¹, parametrically larger than loop-level predictions of minimal Dirac or Majorana models. They also carry out an event-level simulation of the production-and-decay signature.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper considers how magnetic and oscillation effects shape neutrino emission from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. It finds that oscillations in the outer stellar layers harden the electron antineutrino spectrum, most clearly for inverted ordering. That makes the spectrum a possible handle on mass ordering in a future Galactic supernova.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI
