Experiments and results

SBND first cross-section

The Short-Baseline Near Detector reports its first neutrino-argon cross-section measurement. Using a subset of Run 1 data corresponding to 8.8×10^19 protons on target, it selects 12,804 muon-neutrino charged-current candidates with a single proton and no pions, at 91% purity. The cross sections are given as functions of transverse kinematic imbalance variables and discriminate strongly among event-generator predictions. Separately, a companion paper searches for long-lived particles from neutrino up-scattering using the detector's Cosmic Ray Tagger, which ran standalone in 2017-2018; full SBND neutrino data taking began in December 2024.

CONUS+ reactor CEvNS

CONUS+ reports what it calls the most precise measurement to date of coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering from reactor antineutrinos. With 1296 kg·days of reactor-on exposure, a blind likelihood analysis finds 1555 ± 152 events at 10.2σ, against a Standard Model expectation of 1701 ± 198, an agreement within 0.6 standard deviations. The result also gives sin²θ_W = 0.225 ± 0.022 at a momentum transfer of about 10 MeV, consistent with the Standard Model. A companion paper describes the run 2 detector performance and background model.

Diffuse supernova background

Super-Kamiokande reports an indication of electron antineutrinos from the diffuse supernova neutrino background, from a spectral fit to a 5003.0-day dataset. The analysis improves the rejection of atmospheric-neutrino backgrounds, updates the systematic uncertainties and refines the spectral fit. For a representative model the best-fit flux is 3.6 (+1.6, −1.5) cm⁻² s⁻¹ above 13.3 MeV, a 2.6σ tension with the null hypothesis. Models with different spectral shapes give similar significances.

DUNE construction

Fermilab reports that construction of the Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility is entering its next phase, with critical components arriving and partner institutions contributing. UK groups have completed half of the 137 Anode Plane Assemblies, the sensors that will detect the electrical signals from neutrino interactions in liquid argon. A third news item notes that SLAC's SPINE, a machine-learning reconstruction tool, is already deployed in the SBN program and in DUNE.

A new review summarises the properties of the ultra-high-energy neutrino event KM3-230213A, whose inferred parent-neutrino energy is of order 10^17 eV. TAMBO project

The Tau Air-Shower Mountain-Based Observatory is designed to detect tau neutrinos from 1 PeV to 1,000 PeV. It would use up to 5,000 particle detectors on the face of a steep hill. This energy range lies between the reach of optical-Cherenkov and radio-based experiments, and atmospheric backgrounds are largely suppressed there. A new contribution presents the project and its TAMBITO prototype.

PUEO balloon payload

The PUEO collaboration describes the design and flight of a balloon-borne antenna array. It is built to detect the Askaryan emission from ultra-high-energy neutrino interactions. The motivation is that neutrinos at these energies carry information on cosmic-ray acceleration and propagation, but their low predicted flux requires very large detector volumes.

T2K SuperFGD

SuperFGD is a key component of the upgrade of the T2K near detector ND280. It consists of about 2 million optically isolated 1 cm³ scintillator cubes, read out by three orthogonal wavelength-shifting fibres. It provides 3D images of neutrino interactions and can identify Michel electrons and measure neutron energy by time of flight. A recent paper on its performance in the T2K beam covers the design, calibration and detector parameters.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper examines the contribution of Δ-direct-Urca processes to neutrino emissivity in neutron stars, in the presence of Δ isobars. These processes can accompany the nucleonic Urca processes that drive neutron-star cooling. It is relevant to neutrino physicists because neutrino emission sets the thermal evolution of dense stellar matter.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem for observing a Majorana phase through open two-flavour neutrino oscillations: every completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is exactly phase blind. A rate anisotropy in the environment can supply a phase reference. It sets a precise condition for when decoherence could make a Majorana phase visible in oscillations.

The paper studies a gauged U(1) Lμ−Lτ extension with an ultralight vector boson that, through kinetic mixing with the photon, generates an effective matter potential in the Sun. Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass

The paper studies neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, which has a scalar quadruplet and two vectorlike fermion triplets. Neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. Sub-eV masses are accommodated with the new-physics scale at the TeV level, which makes the model a target for experimental searches.

Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses

The paper investigates whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected as nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals (paleodetectors). It includes variations in neutron-star mass, failed supernovae that form black holes, and the role of the nuclear equation of state. Sensitivity is found to be enhanced for models with larger neutrino emission.

Fermionic dark matter and neutrino masses in a minimal U(1)PQ inverse seesaw framework

The paper proposes a minimal extension of the Standard Model in which a single global U(1)PQ symmetry generates the scales of the inverse seesaw and stabilises a fermionic dark matter candidate. Light neutrino masses arise at the TeV scale, and the authors state that the framework predicts a strong correlation between the effective Majorana mass and the CP-violating phase. That correlation is what makes it testable in future experiments.
