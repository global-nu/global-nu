Diffuse supernova neutrinos

Super-Kamiokande reports an indication of electron antineutrinos from the diffuse supernova neutrino background, from a spectral fit of a 5003.0-day dataset. The analysis improves the rejection of atmospheric-neutrino backgrounds, the evaluation of systematic uncertainties and the fitting method. With a representative model the best-fit flux is 3.6 (+1.6, -1.5) cm^-2 s^-1 for E_ν > 13.3 MeV, a 2.6σ tension with the null hypothesis. Models with different spectral shapes give similar significances.

Reactor CEvNS on germanium

CONUS+ reports what it calls the most precise measurement to date of reactor-antineutrino coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. With 1296 kg·days of reactor-on exposure, a blind likelihood analysis gives 1555 ± 152 events at 10.2σ, within 0.6 standard deviations of the Standard Model expectation of 1701 ± 198. The same data give sin²θ_W = 0.225 ± 0.022 at a momentum transfer of about 10 MeV. A companion paper presents the run 2 analysis, covering detector characterisation, the background model and the signal prediction, after three of the original 1 kg detectors were replaced by larger 2.42 kg diodes.

PUEO balloon payload

The PUEO collaboration presents the design and flight of a balloon-borne antenna array. It is built to detect the Askaryan emission from ultrahigh-energy neutrino interactions. The motivation is that neutrinos at these energies carry information on cosmic-ray acceleration and propagation, but need very large detector volumes because the predicted flux is low.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal, now 70% complete. Jinping geoneutrino prediction

A new preprint refines the geoneutrino flux prediction for the 500-tonne Jinping Neutrino Experiment. It builds a local crustal model at 0.2° × 0.2° resolution from a seismic velocity model and combines it with CRUST1.0. It also computes the inverse beta decay spectrum, including the reactor antineutrino background, for an assumed exposure of 500 t × 1500 days.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper computes the neutrino emissivity of Δ-direct-Urca processes in neutron-star matter containing Δ isobars. It notes that this contribution had not been evaluated explicitly, although the nucleonic and hyperonic Urca processes are known as dominant cooling mechanisms. It is relevant to anyone using neutrino emission to model neutron-star thermal evolution.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem: any completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is blind to a Majorana phase in two-flavour oscillations. It also shows that a rate anisotropy can supply an environmental phase reference, which gives the phase a cosine dependence in the exact finite-time result. This sets a clear condition for when open-system effects could in principle reveal a Majorana phase.

Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass

The paper studies neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, with a scalar quadruplet and two vectorlike fermion triplets. Neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop, so sub-eV masses are possible with a TeV-scale new-physics scale. The authors examine whether thermal leptogenesis is viable, noting that it differs from the canonical seesaw case.

Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses

The paper asks whether neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae could be detected as nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals (paleodetectors). It varies neutron-star mass and the nuclear equation of state, and includes failed supernovae that form black holes. It quantifies how sensitivity grows for models with larger neutrino emission.

Neutrino flux from collisions of red giant cores in globular clusters

The paper proposes that the coalescence of degenerate helium cores in red giant collisions is a new class of transient high-energy neutrino source. Using stellar models and hydrodynamic simulations, it builds a semianalytical estimate of the energy release, the magnetic-field amplification and the resulting neutrino flux. The authors find that the TeV-PeV signal may contribute to the diffuse IceCube flux.
