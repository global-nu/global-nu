Who is behind global-nu

A site of the Bari group, built to put our global analyses of neutrino oscillation data — and the material around them — where the community can actually use them.

## The group The global analyses published here are the work of a collaboration centred on Bari. The most recent release, Phys. Rev. D 111, 093006 (2025), is by Francesco Capozzi (L’Aquila and INFN LNGS), William Giarè (Sheffield), Eligio Lisi (INFN Bari), Antonio Marrone (Università di Bari and INFN Bari), Alessandro Melchiorri (Roma “La Sapienza” and INFN Roma I) and Antonio Palazzo (Università di Bari and INFN Bari). The series of analyses it belongs to goes back to the early 2000s; the [parameter history](history.html) page traces it release by release. ## What a global analysis is Every oscillation experiment measures a projection of the same six-parameter space, and each is blind to directions the others constrain. A global analysis combines them into a single χ² — solar, atmospheric, reactor and accelerator data, with cosmological constraints where relevant — and reports the parameters with the correlations intact. That combination is what this site publishes.

How to cite

If you use the numbers or the files from the 2025 release, please cite the paper:

@article{Capozzi:2025wgt,
  author  = {Capozzi, Francesco and Giar\`e, William

  title   = {{Neutrino masses and mixing:
             Entering the era of subpercent precision}},
  journal = {Phys. Rev. D},
  volume  = {111},
  number  = {9},
  pages   = {093006},
  year    = {2025},
  doi     = {10.1103/PhysRevD.111.093006},
  eprint  = {2503.07752},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv},
  primaryClass  = {hep-ph}

The INSPIRE record carries the authoritative key and an always-current BibTeX entry: look it up on INSPIRE.

Automatically generated pages

The arXiv digest, the news and the conference calendar are produced daily with AI assistance and are marked as such at the top of each page. They may contain errors, and they are not a substitute for the sources they link to.

The results and the parameter history are not automated: every number there is transcribed from a published table by hand.
