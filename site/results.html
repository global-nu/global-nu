Best fits and allowed ranges

The oscillation parameters of the three data sets, for either mass ordering. Every value is read from the analysis output, not transcribed.

How the quoted values are obtained. Every number comes from exact profiles of the χ²: at each node all the other parameters and all the nuisances are minimised over. The quoted best fit is the continuous minimum of the profile — the vertex of the parabola through the lowest node and its two neighbours — not the lowest node itself; the 1σ, 2σ and 3σ ranges are where the profile crosses Δχ² = 1, 4, 9, taken linearly between neighbouring nodes. The figures show the profiles as computed.

The bars share one axis: per cent of the best fit, so their length is the precision — a short bar means a well measured parameter. sin²θ₂₃ and δ sit on a wider scale, drawn dashed: they are an order of magnitude less constrained, and one is bimodal, the other cyclic. Arrowheads mean the interval continues past the edge.

How to read the tables

One point deserves attention before any use of these files.

Each ordering is referred to its own minimum. Both Δχ² columns start from zero, so the two orderings are not directly comparable as printed. The ordering preference lives entirely in the offset written in the header of every file: 
offset = J_IO − J_NO          (positive → NO favoured)

Dchi2_joint_NO = Dchi2_NO + max(0, −offset)
Dchi2_joint_IO = Dchi2_IO + max(0, +offset)

Conventions

Nσ = √(Δχ²) with one degree of freedom, as in our papers. The six parameters live on different grids, so the tables are in long format: one row per node, columns parameter value unit Dchi2_NO Dchi2_IO. All three data sets give exact profiles, in 1D and in 2D: at every node all the other parameters and all the nuisances are minimised over — each file states it in its header.

Notation, and how to translate it

Different global analyses use different mass-squared variables. Getting the translation wrong is the single easiest way to misread a comparison, so we state ours explicitly.

What we use

δm² = m₂² − m₁²                                   (positive by definition)

Δm² = m₃² − (m₁² + m₂²)/2  =  (Δm²₃₁ + Δm²₃₂)/2      ( > 0 in NO,  < 0 in IO )

Δm² is the semisum of the two atmospheric splittings, and we quote it with its sign: positive in the normal ordering, negative in the inverted one. Every IO row below therefore carries a negative Δm². A bare magnitude would not say which ordering a number belongs to, and the two best fits are different numbers with different precisions, so wherever a single value appears on this page the ordering is named beside it. Our LaTeX tables carry the same sign. Where a symbol appears between bars it is a magnitude on purpose: that is what the comparison with NuFIT and Valencia needs, since those groups quote |Δm²₃₂| and |Δm²₃₁|.

Translating to Δm²₃₁ and Δm²₃₂

Δm²₃₁ = Δm² + δm²/2        Δm²₃₂ = Δm² − δm²/2        (both orderings)

IO, on magnitudes:   |Δm²₃₂| = |Δm²| + δm²/2       |Δm²₃₁| = |Δm²| − δm²/2

These are exact identities. They follow from Δm²₃₁ = Δm²₃₂ + δm², which is the definition of δm² rearranged: the semisum then gives Δm² = Δm²₃₁ − δm²/2 = Δm²₃₂ + δm²/2. Because Δm² carries its sign, the first line holds in both orderings. In the inverted one Δm² and both Δm²₃ℓ are negative, so taking magnitudes exchanges the two roles and the plus sign passes to the «32».

NuFIT quotes Δm²₃₁ for NO and Δm²₃₂ for IO; the Valencia fit quotes |Δm²₃₁| for both. The comparison table below is labelled accordingly.

Propagating the error

For y = |Δm²| + δm²/2 — that is Δm²₃₁ in NO and |Δm²₃₂| in IO — the full Gaussian formula is var(y) = var(Δm²) + var(δm²)/4 + cov(Δm², δm²). The variances are those of the tables above; the correlation ρ is measured, not assumed zero. It turns out small, and the last column shows how little it matters — but that is a result, not an assumption.

How to read it
  • One axis for six quantities. Each observable is referred to our own best fit: the vertical line is us.
  • Bars are 1σ, for every analysis.
  • δCP is a difference, not a ratio: it is a phase, so its zero is a convention and a percentage would mean nothing.
What not to over-read
  • sin²θ23 in normal ordering: the offset is a choice of octant, not a tension; our 3σ range covers both octants.
  • Different data, different treatments: the three analyses do not use the same data sets, nor treat them in the same way.
  • So differences beyond the bars are expected, and are not by themselves discrepancies.

Their IO entry is therefore shown converted, |Δm²32| = |Δm²31| + δm², with the errors combined assuming independence: their covariance is not published. The NO entry needs no conversion.

• The three analyses do not use identical data sets, nor identical treatments of the same data. Differences exceeding the quoted errors are expected and are not evidence of a discrepancy. Read the table as a comparison of published results, each with its own source stated above, not as a consistency test.

What’s new, and how to cite

New data in release 2026.a, with respect to our previous analysis:

JUNO
  • The second JUNO data release, 207 days, from the collaboration’s paper arXiv:2609.37895: the measured spectrum, with the backgrounds and the statistical treatment of the paper.
SK-Gd reactors
  • Reactor antineutrinos in Super-Kamiokande with gadolinium (phases SK-VI and SK-VII, 411.52 live days), from the collaboration’s paper “First observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors at Super-Kamiokande” (arXiv:2610.03143), reproduced by the group and added to the long-baseline reactors of all three data sets.
