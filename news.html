DUNE construction

Construction of the Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility is entering its next phase, with critical components arriving and partner institutions contributing. UK groups have completed half of the 137 Anode Plane Assemblies, the sensors that detect the electrical signals produced when neutrinos interact in liquid argon. Black Hills State University has also joined the collaboration as an institutional member.

KM3NeT and gravitational waves

The KM3NeT Collaboration presented a paper on searches for coincident gravitational waves and high-energy neutrinos, using data from KM3NeT and ANTARES during the O3 observing run. The motivation is that sources of gravitational waves may produce high-energy neutrinos. The collaboration also held its Autumn 2026 meeting in Siracusa, with status updates on detector operations on the agenda.

Auger and sterile neutrinos

The Pierre Auger Collaboration studied the sensitivity of the observatory to sterile neutrinos with masses from MeV to 100 MeV, in a minimal 3+1 framework with mixing mainly to the tau flavour. Such neutrinos could cross long chords of the Earth and produce tau leptons that decay in the atmosphere, generating detectable air showers. The paper computes the corresponding exposures with a Monte Carlo simulation.

Project 8 atom source

Project 8 aims to measure the neutrino mass with cyclotron radiation emission spectroscopy of tritium beta decay, with a stated goal of 40 meV/c^2 for its final measurement. A new paper describes a prototype electron cyclotron resonance dissociator that ignites a hydrogen plasma, as a step towards dissociating molecular tritium.

Neutrino production at the extreme vicinity of the supermassive black hole in NGC 1068

Using the updated IceCube data for NGC 1068 down to about 0.2 TeV, the authors infer the conditions for neutrino production near the supermassive black hole. They compute hadronic neutrino production and the accompanying cascade gamma rays, sampling the parameter space with Latin hypercube sampling. This bears on what physical conditions are required to explain the first extragalactic neutrino source.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem for observing a Majorana phase through open two-flavour neutrino oscillations: any completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is phase blind. A rate anisotropy in the decoherence can supply a phase reference. It defines when decoherence effects could in principle reveal a Majorana phase.

Exploring anomaly-free dark photon models through solar neutrino-electron scattering in ton-scale dark matter detectors

The authors use solar neutrino-electron scattering data from PandaX-4T and XENONnT to constrain anomaly-free dark photon models. They derive exclusion limits for six benchmark models and find that their stringency correlates with the leptonic charge assignments. This shows how dark matter detectors can also test new physics in the neutrino sector.

Quasisteady flavor configuration of multienergy neutrino ensembles

The paper offers semianalytic approximations for the quasisteady outcome of slow and fast flavour conversion in multienergy neutrino ensembles. It is aimed at supernova simulations that cannot include neutrino quantum kinetics. The authors report excellent agreement with multiangle and multienergy solutions of the kinetic equations, independent of the mass ordering.
