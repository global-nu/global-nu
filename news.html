In summary. This week's news mixed operational milestones at running observatories with a genuine result: XENONnT reported the first solar neutrino detection at the keV scale via electron scattering, and Super-Kamiokande set tighter proton-decay limits using its complete pure-water dataset. On the theory side, several published papers explored links between neutrino mass generation and dark matter, alongside continued work on double-beta decay limits and interaction modelling for accelerator experiments. Experiments and results

IceCube extra dimensions

A dedicated analysis of 10.7 years of upward-going muon neutrino data, covering energies from 0.5 to 100 TeV, was used to constrain low-scale gravity scenarios put forward to address the hierarchy problem. The predicted spectral distortions from matter effects as neutrinos cross the Earth were used to place constraints on large extra dimension models. A companion IceCube blog post explains the motivation behind this line of search.

Searching for Extra Dimensions and Copies of the Standard Model with IceCube — arXivSearching for extra dimensions with IceCube — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

The IceCube Upgrade, installed within the existing DeepCore infill array during the 2025/2026 season, adds densely instrumented multi-PMT optical modules to improve sensitivity in the GeV regime, with commissioning expected to be complete by the end of 2026. The collaboration has published projected sensitivities for neutrino transient searches, steady point-source emission such as from NGC 1068, and diffuse astrophysical emission.

Lake Vermilion detector

Fermilab is leading a new underwater neutrino detection experiment in Minnesota, the Lake Vermilion neutrino detector, continuing the state's history of neutrino research. Testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion is due to begin this autumn.

SuperCDMS SNOLAB start-up

SuperCDMS SNOLAB has begun a preliminary phase of its deep-underground dark matter search. Early scientific results are considered possible even before the experiment reaches its full, optimal sensitivity when the complete search begins next year.

IceCube funding renewal

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The agreement is worth $53 million over five years and covers the period 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
XENONnT solar neutrinos

Using data from its first two science runs, corresponding to an exposure of 2.46 tonne-years, XENONnT reported the first measurement of solar neutrinos through elastic neutrino-electron scattering, with sensitivity down to 17 keV, the lowest energy threshold achieved for any neutrino detection to date. The background-only hypothesis was rejected at 5.0σ, and the collaboration measured a pp solar neutrino flux of (10.2 ± 2.0) × 10^10 cm⁻² s⁻¹.

First Measurement of Solar Neutrinos through Elastic Neutrino-Electron Scattering at the … — arXiv
IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube searched for high-energy neutrino emission coincident with 89 tidal disruption events selected from optical/UV and X-ray observations, using time-dependent likelihood methods to probe both individual sources and a stacked population. Tidal disruption events are theorized to produce neutrinos through photohadronic interactions between accelerated protons and photons in the accretion disc and outflows.

Search for Neutrinos from Tidal Disruption Events with IceCube — arXiv
Forward LHC neutrinos

A review describes the large flux of far-forward neutrinos, reaching TeV energies, produced in proton-proton collisions at the LHC near the beam axis. The new FASER and SND@LHC experiments have reported the first direct observations of these collider neutrinos, opening an experimental window beyond the reach of traditional accelerator-based neutrino experiments.

Neutrino Experiments at the LHC and the HL-LHC — arXiv
Super-Kamiokande proton decay

Using the full 401 kiloton-year exposure of its pure-water phase, Super-Kamiokande searched for proton decay into a charged antilepton plus an invisible, massless particle. No significant signal was found above the expected atmospheric neutrino background, and the collaboration set new lower limits on the proton partial lifetime, improving on previous limits by factors of 2 and 1.5 for the electron and muon channels respectively.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • A paper in Nature Astronomy describes TAMBO, a mountain-based observatory designed to map the high-energy neutrino sky from a deep-valley site. The authors argue the approach could help close gaps in the current coverage of astrophysical neutrino sources and potentially reveal signs of new physics along the way.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining results from multiple neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed from ab initio chiral effective field theory in a Bayesian framework, the authors derive global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass scale. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, the ab initio calculations suggest the current generation of double-beta decay experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity required, a result relevant to planning future searches.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors build a radiative neutrino mass model in which a Dirac fermion dark matter candidate is boosted through semi-annihilation into an anti-dark-matter particle and a neutrino. They find this boosted dark matter component could be probed by DARWIN if its mass lies between 20 and 50 MeV, with the semi-annihilation proceeding through a finely tuned resonance.

    A radiative neutrino mass model with semi-annihilating boosted dark matter — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that enforces a rank-two Dirac neutrino mass matrix, leaving one neutrino exactly massless while remaining consistent with oscillation and cosmological data. Anomaly cancellation requires a second dark fermion doublet, giving a compact, anomaly-free completion of the dark sector.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper presents a secluded dark matter framework built on an extra U(1) gauge symmetry, with a Dirac dark matter particle, three heavy neutrinos, and a singlet scalar that mixes with the Higgs only through a loop involving the same Yukawa couplings that generate active neutrino masses. This ties the dark Higgs mixing angle directly to the heavy neutrino masses, linking two normally separate puzzles.

    Neutrino mass and secluded dark matter puzzles solved together — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using the GENIE event generator, the authors benchmark a range of theoretical and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon scattering against recent MicroBooNE measurements of charged-current interactions. They find strong separation between competing models in some cases, giving upcoming neutrino experiments a clearer basis for choosing interaction models in their simulations.

    Benchmarking state-of-the-art theory and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
