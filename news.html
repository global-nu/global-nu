Experiments and results

DUNE construction

Construction of the Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility is entering its next phase, with critical components arriving. UK groups have completed half of the 137 Anode Plane Assemblies, the sensors that will detect the electrical signals produced by neutrino interactions in liquid argon. Black Hills State University has also become an institutional member, and the collaboration now counts more than 1,500 collaborators from 228 institutions in 38 countries and CERN.

DUNE near-detector sterile search

The DUNE Collaboration presents a sensitivity study for eV-scale sterile-neutrino oscillations in the 3+1 framework, using the simulated near detector. It combines three event samples: highly elastic charged-current muon-neutrino events, highly elastic charged-current electron-neutrino events, and neutrino-electron elastic scattering. These samples are chosen to reduce neutrino-interaction and flux uncertainties. The study is based on simulation, not data.

SBND first results

The Short-Baseline Near Detector collaboration has released its first neutrino results. According to Fermilab, the result improves neutrino interaction modelling and paves the way for DUNE. Separately, SLAC's SPINE, a machine-learning tool that reconstructs neutrino interactions from detector images, is deployed in the SBN programme and in DUNE.

Galactic plane neutrinos

The IceCube Collaboration reports the first measurement of how the high-energy neutrino flux varies along the Galactic plane. It uses 10 years of shower-type events and fits both normalisation and spectral index in several regions, independently of model expectations. The fitted spectral indices are 2.76 (+0.18, -0.15) in the inner region and 3.13 (+0.57, -0.37) in the outer region.

Gravitational-wave coincidences

KM3NeT has published a paper describing a search for joint observations of high-energy neutrinos and gravitational waves, using data from the KM3NeT and ANTARES telescopes during the O3 observing run. The motivation is that sources of gravitational waves may also produce high-energy neutrinos. The collaboration also held its autumn meeting in Siracusa, with status updates on detector operations.

Auger sterile search

The Pierre Auger Collaboration studies sensitivity to sterile neutrinos with masses between MeV and 100 MeV, mixing mainly with the tau flavour. Such particles could cross long chords of the Earth, interact through their active component and produce tau leptons that decay in the atmosphere and generate detectable air showers. The exposures are computed with a Monte Carlo simulation of neutrino propagation.

NINJA scintillator tracker

A meter-scale scintillator tracker named FROST was built and operated in the NINJA experiment to match muon tracks between the nuclear emulsion detectors and the downstream muon detector. During J-PARC neutrino-beam operation it reached a data acquisition efficiency of 99.59% and a detection efficiency of 99.946±0.006% for selected neutrino-induced muons well within the active area.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper studies the contribution of Δ-direct-Urca processes to neutrino emissivity in neutron stars, which may contain Δ isobars and hyperons in their cores. Urca processes are dominant cooling mechanisms, so the emissivity feeds directly into thermal-evolution models of neutron stars.

Toward measuring the low-energy cosmic-ray spectrum with next-generation neutrino detectors

The authors examine whether next-generation kiloton-scale detectors such as Hyper-Kamiokande can measure the primary cosmic-ray spectrum through the atmospheric neutrino flux. They report that such detectors can distinguish between cosmic-ray models with high statistical significance, even allowing for cross-section and hadronic-interaction uncertainties. This is relevant to anyone using atmospheric neutrinos, since the cosmic-ray input is a key source of flux uncertainty.

Neutrino production at the extreme vicinity of the supermassive black hole in NGC 1068

Motivated by updated IceCube data for NGC 1068 down to about 0.2 TeV, the paper infers the conditions for neutrino production close to the supermassive black hole. When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem: in open two-flavour oscillations, any completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane cannot reveal a Majorana phase. It also shows that a rate anisotropy between the two transverse decoherence rates can supply a phase reference. This delimits the circumstances in which decoherence could be used to probe the Majorana nature of neutrinos.

Quasisteady flavor configuration of multienergy neutrino ensembles

The authors give semianalytic approximations for the outcome of slow and fast flavour conversion in quasihomogeneous neutrino ensembles. They report excellent agreement with multiangle and multienergy solutions of the kinetic equations, independent of mass ordering. Such ansätze could serve as subgrid schemes in supernova simulations that cannot include neutrino quantum kinetics.

Exploring anomaly-free dark photon models through solar neutrino-electron scattering in ton-scale dark matter detectors

The paper uses low-energy solar neutrino-electron scattering data from PandaX-4T and XENONnT to constrain anomaly-free U(1)_X dark photon models with kinetic and mass mixing. It derives exclusion limits for six benchmark models and finds that their stringency correlates with the leptonic charge assignments. It also gives model-independent limits for the pure kinetic and pure mass mixing cases.
