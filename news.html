Experiments and results

KM3NeT ARCA62 campaign

A two-week sea campaign at the KM3NeT ARCA site enlarged the submarine infrastructure and the detector itself, including an upgrade to the apparatus. The Collaboration described the campaign as a significant step forward in construction of the telescope.

IceCube checks KM3NeT event

IceCube and KM3NeT's ARCA telescope both search for the sources of astrophysical neutrinos. Following KM3NeT's report of the highest-energy neutrino candidate ever detected, at 220 PeV, IceCube carried out its own search for a possible source of that event.

IceCube search for the source of KM3NeT’s record-breaking event — Read it
DUNE cryostat steel

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to build the cryostat for the Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment, nearly 2 million pounds that now fill the 500-foot-long south cavern. Construction continues ahead of detector installation.

SURF crews move 39% of steel for DUNE — Read it
IceCube Upgrade progress

During the 2025/26 austral summer, five new strings equipped with new photosensor designs were deployed as a dense infill in the middle of the existing IceCube detector. Studies of the projected sensitivities of the completed Upgrade build on IceCube's role in identifying active galaxies as sources of high-energy neutrinos.

IceCube steady-state AGN search

Four years after reporting evidence for high-energy neutrinos from the active galaxy NGC 1068, IceCube used its DeepCore sub-array to search for steady-state neutrino emission from active galactic nuclei, where escaping X-rays trace the environment around the central supermassive black hole.

Search for steady-state neutrino emission using IceCube DeepCore — Read it
IceCube tidal-disruption search

IceCube searched for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena occurring on timescales of weeks. The search extends IceCube's programme of identifying sources of the astrophysical neutrino flux, which so far includes two galaxies and the Milky Way.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
IceCube extra-dimensions search

IceCube used its data to search for signatures of extra dimensions, motivated by the unresolved hierarchy problem between the weak force and gravity.

Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment led by Fermilab, the Lake Vermilion neutrino detector, will begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
T2K ND280 Time-of-Flight

The T2K ND280 Upgrade's new Time-of-Flight detector, comprising six panels of plastic-scintillator bars read out by silicon photomultipliers, has been commissioned around the SuperFGD and the High-Angle TPCs. It is intended to reduce systematic uncertainties in oscillation measurements and improve sensitivity to the CP-violating phase.

MicroBooNE cross sections

MicroBooNE reported the first measurement of flux-integrated differential cross sections in kinematic imbalance variables for electron-neutrino interactions on argon, using its full Booster Neutrino Beam dataset. These variables are sensitive to nuclear effects such as Fermi motion and final-state interactions.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • This paper presents a combined oscillation analysis using atmospheric and reactor data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore, KM3NeT/ORCA and Daya Bay within a single unified model, fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters. It finds no significant tensions between the datasets and reports a preference for the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance, a result of direct relevance to the global determination of the mass ordering.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper derives global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass by combining likelihoods from several neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory. It concludes that, in contrast to phenomenological nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate the current generation of experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass ordering.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal extension of the Standard Model in which a single U(1)PQ symmetry sets both the inverse-seesaw scales for neutrino mass and the stability of a fermionic dark matter candidate, mediated by an axion-like pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson. The model generates light neutrino masses without invoking very high energy scales and predicts a correlation between the effective Majorana mass and the CP-violating phase, making it testable in future neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments.

    Fermionic dark matter and neutrino masses in a minimal U(1)PQ inverse seesaw framework — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper studies the prospects for detecting neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae through nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals (paleodetectors), accounting for the diversity of neutron star masses, failed supernovae leading to black hole formation, and the nuclear equation of state. It quantifies how detection sensitivity is enhanced for more compact remnants that release more gravitational energy.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper constructs a radiative neutrino mass model in which a Dirac fermion dark matter candidate produces boosted dark matter today through semi-annihilation into an anti-dark-matter particle and a neutrino. Within the allowed parameter space, the boosted dark matter could be probed by the DARWIN experiment for dark matter masses between 20 and 50 MeV, with the semi-annihilation proceeding through a resonance.

    A radiative neutrino mass model with semi-annihilating boosted dark matter — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, and surveys its interpretation within active-galactic-nucleus, transient-source and cosmogenic scenarios. It notes that despite extensive follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been identified.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI
