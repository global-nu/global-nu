Experiments and results

DUNE

DUNE, led by Fermilab, has been included in a line-up of the most ambitious international science projects. The collaboration is preparing for data analysis by developing AI-based automated triggers that sort through detector data, identify neutrino interactions, flag possible signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector. Rice University's DUNE-TECH programme is separately introducing undergraduates to the science behind the experiment.

Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment led by Fermilab, the Lake Vermilion neutrino detector, is set to begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

IceCube funding renewal

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a five-year, $53 million contract covering 2026-2031.

IceCube DeepCore search

IceCube has searched for steady-state neutrino emission using the DeepCore sub-array, following up on earlier evidence for high-energy neutrinos from NGC 1068, an active galactic nucleus whose escaping X-rays trace the environment around its central supermassive black hole.

Search for steady-state neutrino emission using IceCube DeepCore — Read it
IceCube transient search

IceCube continues to search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena occurring on timescales of weeks, as part of its ongoing effort to pin down the sources of the high-energy astrophysical neutrinos first observed in 2013.

IceCube extra dimensions

IceCube has carried out a search for signatures of extra dimensions, motivated by the long-standing hierarchy problem between the weak force and gravity.

IceCube Upgrade

The collaboration has projected the sensitivities of the planned IceCube Upgrade, aimed at sharpening the study of high-energy astrophysical neutrino sources, including the active galaxies already identified as likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration.

T2K ND280 upgrade

The collaboration has commissioned and characterised the Time-of-Flight detector for the T2K ND280 Upgrade, comprising six panels of plastic-scintillator bars surrounding the Super Fine-Grained Detector and the High-Angle Time Projection Chambers. The detector is designed to reduce systematic uncertainties on oscillation parameters and improve sensitivity to the CP-violating phase.

MicroBooNE cross sections

MicroBooNE has reported the first measurement of flux-integrated differential cross sections in kinematic imbalance variables for electron neutrino interactions on argon, using its full dataset of 1.3×10^21 protons on target from the Booster Neutrino Beam. The variables are sensitive to Fermi motion, multinucleon processes and final-state interactions in neutrino-nucleus scattering.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay fits over 839,000 events with a unified physics model and finds no significant tensions between datasets. The combination yields competitive mixing-parameter measurements and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • A global Bayesian analysis combines results from current neutrinoless double-beta decay searches with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory. In contrast to phenomenological nuclear models, the ab initio treatment indicates that the current generation of 0νββ experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the target mass regime.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • A paper on the TAMBO concept, a mountain-based observatory designed to map the high-energy neutrino sky, argues that the field has struggled to fully chart this sky despite a decade of progress and presents the observatory as a route to closing that gap while searching for new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • A review of the ultra-high-energy neutrino event KM3-230213A, detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, summarises the event's properties and surveys its possible astrophysical and cosmogenic interpretations. Despite extensive follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been identified.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • A multimessenger model of white dwarf tidal disruptions by intermediate-mass black holes develops an accretion-disk description in which the bound debris accretes at extremely super-Eddington rates. The model finds that the inner-flow temperature can exceed 10^9 K, enabling prolific pair production and MeV neutrino emission alongside gravitational-wave and photon signals.
