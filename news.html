Experiments and results

DUNE data triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI-based systems to prepare for the experiment's data analysis, aiming to sort through very large data volumes, identify neutrino interactions, flag possible signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems within the detector itself. This work is part of preparations for automated triggering of the deep-underground detector.

US lab to hunt most elusive ‘ghost particles’ deep underground with automated triggers — Read it
IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube continues its search for the sources of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, first observed in 2013, having so far identified two galaxies as sources in addition to neutrino emission from the Milky Way. The collaboration is now specifically searching for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena that unfold over timescales of weeks.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
IceCube and extra dimensions

Motivated by the unresolved hierarchy problem, the puzzle of why the weak force is so much stronger than gravity, IceCube is searching its data for signatures of extra spatial dimensions.

Searching for extra dimensions with IceCube — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivity

Building on prior evidence that active galaxies are sources of high-energy neutrinos and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration, the collaboration has released projected sensitivities for the planned IceCube Upgrade.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
IceCube operations renewed

The U.S. National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a five-year, $53 million agreement covering the period 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment led by Fermilab, sited in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, is due to begin testing this autumn, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • A combined oscillation fit brings together atmospheric data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA with reactor data from Daya Bay, showing that a single unified model can describe all four datasets without significant tension between them, a combination long considered infeasible outside the individual collaborations. Fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters, the analysis prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This work derives global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass by combining recent neutrinoless double-beta decay results with nuclear matrix elements computed from first-principles chiral effective field theory within a Bayesian framework. In contrast to results based on more conventional nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that the current generation of 0νββ experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass scale of interest.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This study asks whether ancient minerals could preserve nuclear recoil tracks left by neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae, extending the paleodetector concept across a range of collapse outcomes, including varying neutron star masses and failed supernovae that form black holes. The achievable sensitivity depends on the nuclear equation of state, since it sets the neutron star radius and maximum mass, and is largest for the more compact remnants and larger neutrino emission associated with them.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors build a time-dependent, multienergy model of neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae, tracking both the MeV neutrinos produced during the initial thermonuclear runaway and the GeV neutrinos generated by nova-driven shocks. Using RS Oph as a benchmark, they model the shock as it interacts with both the red giant wind and a dense accretion disk around the white dwarf, both of which shape the resulting neutrino signal.

    Time-dependent multienergy neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae: The role of … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that forces one neutrino to be exactly massless while remaining consistent with oscillation data and cosmological constraints on a Dirac neutrino mass matrix. Anomaly cancellation and a discrete symmetry forbidding Majorana masses fix the minimal dark-sector content needed, tying the existence of a massless neutrino state to a specific new gauge sector.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using energy density functional nuclear models, the authors calculate charged-current quasielastic neutrino scattering on carbon-12 for both electron- and muon-neutrino beams and compare the results with MINERvA data. They find that the in-medium effective mass of the nucleon affects the single- and double-differential cross sections, a detail relevant to how well such nuclear models can reproduce data from current neutrino beams.
