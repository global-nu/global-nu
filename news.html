Experiments and results

DUNE automated triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI systems to sort through the large volumes of data the experiment will produce, aiming to identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector. The effort draws on decades of machine-learning work at Fermilab and is part of preparations for data analysis ahead of operation.

DUNE detector stress test

A prototype DUNE detector component was subjected to an extreme 300,000-volt stress trial to test its limits before installation deep underground in the US.

300,000 volts: US team puts world’s largest neutrino detector through extreme stress test — Read it
IceCube funding renewed

The US National Science Foundation renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to manage and operate IceCube, covering continued operation and maintenance for 2026-2031 under a five-year, $53 million agreement.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
Probing Earth with neutrinos

IceCube researchers are using atmospheric neutrinos, produced in cosmic-ray-induced air showers, to probe the interior structure of the Earth, complementing indirect methods such as seismic and gravitational measurements.

Probing the Earth’s interior using atmospheric neutrinos — Read it
IceCube's WavePID tool

IceCube has described WavePID, a tool that identifies neutrinos from the shape of the light pattern produced when a neutrino interacts with ice, working at the nanosecond level.

WavePID: A tool that identifies neutrinos at the nanosecond level — Read it
JUNO calibration background

A new study characterises the radioactive background contributed by JUNO's calibration system, whose components sit close to the detector's target volume. The reactor-neutrino analysis requires the total singles background rate from radioactivity to stay below 10 Hz in the 0.7-12 MeV range within the fiducial volume, motivating extensive material screening and selection for the calibration hardware.

The Radioactive Background of the JUNO Calibration System — arXiv
ESSnuSB detector design study

A study for the proposed ESSnuSB experiment used graph neural networks to test flavour identification in candidate designs for its near water Cherenkov detector, examining whether a smaller or less instrumented detector can still meet the classification performance the CP-violation measurement requires.

Reactor neutrino-electron detector

A study evaluated the expected performance of a proposed 170-ton water Cherenkov detector for reactor antineutrino-electron elastic scattering at the RENO near-detector site, projecting the achievable precision on the reactor-spectrum normalisation for two different event-selection scenarios over a one-year exposure.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • This paper builds a minimal flavoured axion model that treats the Peccei-Quinn symmetry as a flavour symmetry, combining the KSVZ axion framework with the type-I seesaw mechanism. The resulting texture in the inverse Majorana neutrino mass matrix, together with the observed smallness of θ13, predicts an inverted neutrino mass ordering with the ratio m1/m3 constrained to lie between 17 and 45, linking axion model-building directly to a testable prediction for neutrino mass ordering.

    Neutrino mass hierarchy and axion–lepton couplings in a minimal flavored νKSVZ model — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors study how well combined NOvA and DUNE data could constrain an invisible decay of the third neutrino mass state into a sterile state, assuming the normal mass ordering. They find that the achievable constraint on the decay parameter depends strongly on the true value of θ23, with a value in the higher octant improving the sensitivity.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This work models three-flavour neutrino oscillations as a qutrit coupled to a classical random-telegraph-noise environment acting as pure dephasing in the mass basis. The authors show that the ratio between the noise switching rate and coupling strength determines whether flavour coherence decays monotonically or shows memory-induced revivals, offering a framework for non-Markovian environmental effects on oscillations.

    Non-Markovian Dephasing and Flavor Coherence in a Three-Flavor Neutrino Qutrit — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper develops an open-quantum-systems formalism for the response of superfluid helium-4 to neutrino scattering, aimed at the coherent elastic neutrino-atom scattering process that has not yet been observed. It is motivated by a planned experiment at the National Center for Physics and Mathematics in Sarov that would combine a high-intensity tritium neutrino source with a superfluid helium detector to probe neutrino physics at very low energies.

    The Formalism of Open Quantum Systems for Superfluid Helium Response to Neutrino … — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using effective Lagrangians, chiral perturbation theory and a hadronic molecular model, this paper calculates cross sections for antineutrino-proton scattering into hyperons, charmed baryons and candidate hadronic molecular states. The authors argue that future high-statistics neutrino facilities could serve as a complementary probe of these exotic baryonic states.

    Neutrino-proton scattering as a probe of exotic baryonic states: Hyperons, charmed … — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper introduces TAMBO, a mountain-based observatory designed to measure the high-energy neutrino sky, addressing physicists' difficulty in fully mapping this sky over the past decade of neutrino astronomy. The authors present it as a route to both a clearer map of high-energy neutrino sources and potential clues to new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI
