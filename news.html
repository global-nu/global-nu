Experiments and results

XENONnT solar neutrinos

XENONnT reported the first detection of solar pp neutrinos via elastic neutrino-electron scattering in a multi-tonne liquid xenon dark matter detector, using an exposure of 2.46 tonne-years and reaching sensitivity down to 17 keV recoil energy, the lowest energy threshold achieved for any neutrino measurement. The collaboration rejected the background-only hypothesis at 5.0 sigma and measured a pp neutrino flux of (10.2 ± 2.0) × 10^10 cm^-2 s^-1. A subsequent comment from outside authors flagged what they describe as an important omission in the collaboration's summary plot.

Super-Kamiokande proton decay

Using the full pure-water phase of Super-Kamiokande, a 401 kiloton-year exposure, a search for proton decay via p to l+ plus an invisible massless particle found no significant signal above the expected atmospheric-neutrino background. The analysis set 90% confidence-level lower limits on the partial proton lifetime of 1.72×10^33 years for the positron channel and 0.61×10^33 years for the muon channel, improving on previous limits by factors of 2 and 1.5 respectively.

IceCube extra dimensions

IceCube placed new constraints on low-scale-gravity scenarios that address the hierarchy problem, using 10.7 years of upward-going muon-neutrino data spanning 0.5 to 100 TeV. Such scenarios predict characteristic spectral distortions from matter effects as neutrinos cross the Earth; none were observed, letting the collaboration constrain the compactification scale of large extra dimension models.

IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

A new study projects the sensitivity of the planned IceCube Upgrade to the sources of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, building on IceCube's existing evidence that active galaxies are among the sites of hadronic particle acceleration producing these neutrinos.

IceCube operations renewed

The US National Science Foundation renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a five-year, $53 million agreement covering 2026-2031.

KM3NeT source stacking

KM3NeT/ARCA carried out a stacking search for high-energy neutrino emission from three candidate source populations: ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and two blazar subclasses (high-synchrotron-peaked BL Lacs and extreme blazars). Using 336 days of data from a 19-to-21-detection-unit array configuration, the collaboration selected 1544 track-like neutrino candidates, including a sample of 75 ultra-luminous infrared galaxies modelled with power-law spectra.

DUNE automated triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI-based automated triggers to process the large data volumes expected from the deep-underground experiment, aiming to identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector.

Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment, led by Fermilab, will begin testing this autumn in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
ARA diffuse UHE search

The Askaryan Radio Array completed its first array-wide diffuse search for ultra-high-energy neutrinos, combining data from all five autonomous South Pole stations collected between January 2013 and December 2023. The analysis used improved detector characterisation, data-driven noise and electronics models, and updated interaction and lepton-propagation modelling, and represents the largest livetime yet accumulated by an in-ice radio array.

The First Array-Wide Diffuse Flux Search for UHE Neutrinos with the Askaryan Radio Array — arXiv
NEON reconstruction methods

The proposed Neutrino Observatory in the Nanhai (NEON), a deep-sea neutrino telescope for the South China Sea, now has a first comprehensive reconstruction framework for shower-like events, combining a physics-driven maximum-likelihood method with a graph neural network. The work targets challenges specific to the site, including seawater optical attenuation, irregular detector geometry, and background from potassium-40.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • Combining results from current neutrinoless double-beta-decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory, this work derives global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass scale. Unlike phenomenological nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that current-generation 0νββ experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the target mass range.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that fixes the structure of the Dirac neutrino mass matrix so that one neutrino is exactly massless, while remaining consistent with oscillation and cosmological data. Anomaly cancellation forces a specific dark-sector field content, giving a predictive, testable completion rather than an ad hoc assumption of masslessness.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Working in modified teleparallel gravity, this paper shows that spacetime torsion alters both vacuum neutrino oscillations and the matter-enhanced MSW resonance, and uses solar neutrino data to place new bounds on the torsion-neutrino coupling. The authors argue the resulting modification to the survival probability could account for observational discrepancies without invoking new particle physics.

    Testing modified teleparallel gravity by neutrino oscillations — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using the GENIE event generator, this study benchmarks several theoretical and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon charged-current scattering against recent MicroBooNE data, testing components such as nucleon form factors and treatments of the nuclear ground state. The comparison shows strong separation between competing models in some kinematic regions, giving upcoming argon-based experiments clearer guidance on which interaction models to trust.

    Benchmarking state-of-the-art theory and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper studies how fast flavour instabilities and collisional flavour instabilities compete with collisional damping in spatially inhomogeneous neutrino gases, relevant to the region behind a core-collapse supernova shock. Quantum kinetic transport simulations including both spatial advection and collisions show the two instability types can coexist during the post-bounce phase, refining the picture of neutrino flavour conversion used in supernova models.

    Dynamic competition of fast and collisional neutrino flavor instabilities with … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • nuSCOPE is a proposed short-baseline experiment at CERN, building on the ENUBET and NuTAG concepts, that monitors and tags the parent particles producing a neutrino beam so the muon and electron neutrino flux and each event's energy can be reconstructed with high precision. Because poorly known neutrino cross sections are projected to be a leading uncertainty for next-generation oscillation experiments, this event-by-event control would enable measurements normally reserved for electron-scattering facilities.
