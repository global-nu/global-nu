Experiments and results

JUNO oscillation update

JUNO reported an improved oscillation measurement and an indication of the neutrino mass ordering, using 207.2 live days and 8,294 inverse beta-decay candidates at an average reactor baseline of 52.5 km. The atmospheric splitting Δm²31 was measured to about 1% precision. With the Daya Bay constraint in the fit, it is +2.509 (+0.027, −0.025) ×10^-3 eV² for normal ordering and −2.482 (+0.025, −0.026) ×10^-3 eV² for inverted ordering. The abstract excerpt does not give the strength of the ordering indication.

Super-Kamiokande reactor signal

Super-Kamiokande reported the first observation of reactor electron antineutrinos in the detector, using 411.52 live days from the SK-Gd phase, which began in 2020. The prompt energy spectra and monthly event rates are consistent with the oscillated reactor prediction after accounting for accidental, geoneutrino and spallation backgrounds. The no-reactor hypothesis is disfavoured at approximately 6σ.

First observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors at Super-Kamiokande — arXiv

DUNE cryostat steel

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have moved about 39% of the I-beam steel needed to build the DUNE cryostat, nearly 2 million pounds. That steel completely fills the south cavern. The DUNE Collaboration also held a meeting at Unicamp, where progress of the project was discussed.

MicroBooNE proton kinematics

MicroBooNE presented the first double-differential cross-section measurements for muon-neutrino charged-current interactions on argon with no mesons and one or more protons in the final state. The analysis uses the full Booster Neutrino Beam dataset, 1.30 × 10^21 protons on target. Cross sections are given as functions of proton multiplicity, proton momenta and muon-proton opening angles, up to the fourth-leading proton.

MINERvA superscaling

MINERvA reported the first measurement of the superscaling variable in neutrino-nucleus scattering. It used charged-current quasielastic-like muon-neutrino interactions on carbon in the medium-energy NuMI beam. The variable is reconstructed from visible final-state kinematics, and its peak position is sensitive to the invisible nuclear missing energy.

PandaX-4T supernova background

PandaX-4T presented the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background through coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering. With an exposure of 1.54 tonne·year, it observed no significant excess over the expected background. It set a 90% confidence level upper limit of (2.27–2.43) ×10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 per flavour on the ν_x flux for neutrino energies above 19.3 MeV.

Search for Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background of All Flavors with PandaX-4T — arXiv
Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal and is now 70% complete. A search for point-like sources in its 2019-2024 track-like dataset found no significant source, and upper limits were computed for selected candidate objects. The limits are compared with published IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT results.

Search for Point-Like Neutrino Sources with Baikal-GVD — INSPIRE · DOI
T2K ND280 upgrade

The ND280 upgrade was completed in 2024 and has improved the tracking capability of the near detector. Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The paper calculates two-neutrino double-beta decay rates with the full dependence between nuclear structure and lepton kinematics, without the closure, non-closure or Taylor-expansion approximations. It presents the resulting deviations in rates and electron spectra for 82Se and 136Xe. This matters to anyone interpreting double-beta decay spectra, since the approximations it removes are standard in current calculations.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies whether nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals (paleodetectors) could register neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae. It varies neutron-star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the nuclear equation of state. It quantifies how much the sensitivity improves for models with larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper examines thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, in which neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model gives sub-eV neutrino masses with new physics at the TeV scale. The authors test whether leptogenesis works in it, which differs from canonical seesaw leptogenesis.

    Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper considers how magnetic and oscillation effects shape neutrino emission from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. It finds that oscillations in the outer layers of the star harden the electron antineutrino spectrum. The enhancement is particularly pronounced for the inverted mass ordering and is favourable for ground-based detection of supernova neutrinos.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper builds composite neutrino models in which heavy neutrinos are bound states of a near-conformal strongly coupled sector, mixing with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. This yields transition dipole couplings of order 10^-6 to 10^-8 GeV^-1, parametrically larger than loop-level predictions of minimal Dirac or Majorana models. The authors carry out an event-level simulation of the resulting signatures.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper develops a time-dependent, multienergy model of neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae. It includes MeV neutrinos from the thermonuclear runaway and GeV neutrinos from hadronic interactions in nova-driven shocks. It uses RS Oph as the benchmark and models the shock interacting with both the red giant wind and a dense accretion disk around the white dwarf.
