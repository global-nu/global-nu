DUNE cryostat construction

Crews from Fermilab, CERN and SURF have started building the first of two cryostats for DUNE. These are large insulated containers designed to hold liquid argon at very low temperature. Fermilab describes the cryostats as housing approximately 17,000 tons of liquid argon, and the work is taking place a mile underground.

ProtoDUNE-SP muon seasonality

The DUNE Collaboration measured seasonal variation of the cosmic-ray muon rate with the ProtoDUNE single-phase detector at the CERN Neutrino Platform, using data from December 2018 to July 2020. The rate shows a seasonal modulation with a significance of 4σ relative to no variation, peaking in winter. This is consistent with other surface experiments that have a low muon-energy threshold.

Super-Kamiokande solar core

The Super-Kamiokande Collaboration used 5805 days of phase I-IV 8B solar neutrino data to study the solar core. The analysis presents an outer bound on the core size and measurements of the density and temperature in the core. It relies on the dependence of 8B production on core temperature and of adiabatic flavour conversion on the local electron density.

IceCube neutral-current cross section

A phenomenology study examines the sensitivity of IceCube to the neutral-current neutrino-nucleon cross section using 12 years of data. Neutral-current interactions remain largely unexplored experimentally, unlike the charged-current cross section. The authors consider two strategies: neutrino absorption and regeneration in the Earth, and the track-to-cascade event ratio.

SHiP sterile-neutrino sensitivity

The SHiP Collaboration shows that the baseline detector forms a two-baseline setup for oscillations induced by a sterile neutrino. Neutrino interactions are recorded in the scattering and neutrino detector and, about 70 m further downstream, in the calorimeter system. The study uses a Geant4 simulation of Genie-generated interactions to derive the energy response and the separation of electron-neutrino charged-current events from the neutral-current pi-zero background.

ESSnuSB solar neutrinos

The ESSnuSB Collaboration studies the sensitivity of its large underground water-Cherenkov detector to the MSW spectral upturn in the 8B survival probability and to the first direct observation of the hep neutrino flux. The analysis includes detector systematics and models backgrounds from cosmic-ray muon spallation and radon-222. It uses a shape-driven strategy with floating normalisation.

Warwick gas TPC platform

The Warwick TPC (WarTPC) is an R&D platform for active-target gas time projection chambers, which could provide measurements of neutrino interactions for future oscillation studies. It operates at pressures up to approximately 10 bar and combines thick gas electron multipliers with a Timepix3 camera for optical readout. The paper describes its construction and commissioning.

CYGNO electron directionality

The CYGNO collaboration studied how well the initial direction of low-energy electron recoils can be reconstructed in its optical time projection chamber, using the 50 L LIME prototype with an sCMOS readout. Directionality could help separate solar-neutrino interactions from backgrounds. A dedicated algorithm finds the interaction point from the asymmetry of the track light and estimates the direction from the early part of the ionisation trail.

Geo-neutrino global fit

A global fit to geo-neutrino measurements at Kamioka, Gran Sasso, Sudbury and Jiangmen constrains the quantity and distribution of radioactivity in the Earth's mantle. For a uniform mantle the fit gives a rate of 14.8 ± 4.5 NIU, corresponding to radiogenic heating of 18.5 ± 5.6 TW. The uniform-mantle signal is resolved at about 3.2σ, with noticeable tension noted in the abstract.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper computes the neutrino emissivity of Δ-direct-Urca processes in neutron-star matter, where Δ isobars may accompany nucleons and hyperons. Urca processes are leading neutrino-cooling channels, so this contribution had not been explicitly evaluated before and bears on neutron-star thermal evolution.

Toward measuring the low-energy cosmic-ray spectrum with next-generation neutrino detectors

The authors investigate whether kiloton-scale neutrino detectors such as Hyper-Kamiokande could constrain the primary cosmic-ray spectrum through the atmospheric neutrino flux. They report that future detectors can differentiate between cosmic-ray models with high statistical significance, even with cross-section and hadronic-interaction uncertainties. This links atmospheric-neutrino flux inputs directly to cosmic-ray modelling.

Neutrino production at the extreme vicinity of the supermassive black hole in NGC 1068

The paper uses multimessenger data, including updated IceCube data for NGC 1068 down to about 0.2 TeV, to infer conditions for neutrino production near the supermassive black hole. It computes hadronic neutrino production and accompanying cascade gamma rays and explores the parameter space by Latin hypercube sampling. It is relevant for understanding the first identified extragalactic steady neutrino source.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The authors prove a no-go theorem: open two-flavour neutrino evolutions covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane are exactly blind to a Majorana phase. A rate anisotropy in the environment can supply a phase reference. This clarifies when decoherence could in principle reveal the Dirac or Majorana nature of neutrinos.

Quasisteady flavor configuration of multienergy neutrino ensembles

The paper presents semianalytic approximations for the quasisteady outcome of slow and fast flavour conversion in neutrino-dense environments with periodic boundary conditions. These agree with multiangle and multienergy solutions of the kinetic equations, independent of mass ordering. Such schemes are needed because supernova hydrodynamic simulations cannot include neutrino quantum kinetics.

Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass

The authors study thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, in which neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model accommodates sub-eV neutrino masses with new physics at the TeV scale. Leptogenesis here differs from the canonical seesaw case.
