Experiments and results

DUNE construction and outreach

The Fermilab-led Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment was named among the twelve most ambitious international science projects, in a feature on how large-scale modern science crosses borders. DUNE researchers are also developing AI-based automated triggers to sort through the experiment's large data volumes, identify neutrino interactions, flag possible stellar explosions, and detect detector problems. Separately, Rice University's DUNE-TECH programme is introducing undergraduates to the science behind the experiment.

Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment led by Fermilab, sited in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, is due to begin testing this autumn. The project continues Minnesota's history of neutrino research through a novel underwater detection approach.

IceCube operations renewed

The U.S. National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to manage and operate the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers continued operation and maintenance of the detector for 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
IceCube AGN search

IceCube has carried out a search for steady-state neutrino emission using the DeepCore sub-array, following up on the collaboration's earlier evidence for high-energy neutrinos from NGC 1068, an active galactic nucleus with a central supermassive black hole. The analysis is motivated in part by the fact that X-rays escaping the dense environment around such black holes can provide independent information about the source.

IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube has searched for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena occurring on timescales of weeks. The search extends the collaboration's ongoing effort to identify sources of the high-energy astrophysical neutrinos first observed in 2013, an effort that has so far identified two galaxies as sources and detected neutrinos from the Milky Way.

IceCube extra dimensions

The hierarchy problem, the vast disparity between the strength of the weak force and gravity, remains one of the biggest unresolved questions in particle physics. IceCube has searched its data for signs of extra dimensions as a possible explanation for this discrepancy.

IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

IceCube has projected the sensitivities expected from the planned IceCube Upgrade. The projections build on the collaboration's existing evidence that active galaxies are likely sources of high-energy neutrinos and sites of hadronic particle acceleration.

T2K ToF detector

The T2K collaboration has reported the commissioning and performance of the new Time-of-Flight detector built as part of the ND280 upgrade. Comprising plastic-scintillator bars read out at both ends by silicon photomultipliers and digitised with SAMPIC waveform electronics, the detector is intended to reduce systematic uncertainties in oscillation measurements and improve sensitivity to the CP-violating phase δCP by providing timing and directional information that rejects backgrounds entering the tracker.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • This work presents the first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, a combination previously thought infeasible outside the individual collaborations. Fitting 839,048 events across 1,536 bins with 91 parameters, the unified model shows no significant tension between datasets and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by the KM3NeT telescope with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, and surveys its possible interpretations, including active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos. Despite extensive follow-up observations, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been found, so the event's origin remains an open question.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper introduces TAMBO, the Tau Air-shower Mountain-Based Observatory, a deep-valley design intended to help map the high-energy neutrino sky more completely than existing facilities have managed. The authors note that, beyond mapping neutrino sources, the observatory could also yield clues to new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • By combining likelihoods from current neutrinoless double-beta decay searches with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory within the in-medium similarity renormalization group approach, this study derives global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to results from more conventional nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that the current generation of 0νββ experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the neutrino mass scale suggested by oscillation data.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a gauge-symmetry mechanism that produces a rank-two Dirac neutrino mass matrix with one neutrino left exactly massless, while remaining consistent with oscillation and cosmological data. The construction requires a minimal dark SU(2) gauge sector, with anomaly cancellation and a discrete symmetry fixing the minimal dark-sector field content needed to forbid Majorana masses.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper revisits accretion-disk winds from active galactic nuclei as a possible common origin for the cosmic neutrino and gamma-ray backgrounds, building a lepto-hadronic wind model calibrated against radio and GeV gamma-ray data from nearby Seyfert galaxies. Because the predicted background levels depend sensitively on poorly constrained wind energetics and ambient densities, the work aims to sharpen how strongly this scenario can be tested against future observations.
