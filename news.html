Experiments and results

IceCube tidal disruption search

Building on earlier evidence that galaxies can be sources of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, and that neutrinos arrive from the Milky Way, the collaboration is now searching for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — transient phenomena unfolding over weeks. No detection has yet been reported.

Search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

A new study projects the sensitivity of the planned IceCube Upgrade, building on the collaboration's evidence that active galaxies are likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration and sources of high-energy neutrinos. The work examines how the Upgrade's additional instrumentation could improve searches for these sources.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
JUNO information analysis

A new analysis quantifies how information about oscillation parameters, encoded in reactor antineutrino states, is degraded as it flows through to the events observed in a detector such as JUNO. Using quantum and classical Fisher information, the authors construct an 'information ladder' for JUNO and find that the loss of precision across different stages is strongly dependent on which oscillation parameter is being measured.

Quantifying Information Hierarchy for Neutrino Oscillation Parameters at JUNO — arXiv
IceCube funding renewed

The U.S. National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers the period 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
Lake Vermilion detector testing

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment led by Fermilab, the Lake Vermilion neutrino detector, will begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn. The project continues Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
DUNE readies AI triggers

Researchers are developing automated, AI-based trigger systems to sort through the experiment's data, identify neutrino interactions, flag possible signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector. The effort draws on decades of machine-learning work at Fermilab as the collaboration prepares for full data analysis.

NOvA sterile neutrino search

A search for active-to-sterile neutrino oscillations has combined data from both near and far detectors using neutrino-mode and antineutrino-mode beams for the first time. No evidence for sterile neutrinos was found, and the collaboration excluded parameter space previously allowed by other experiments, including most of the region reported by IceCube.

NOvA neutron modelling

A study of how well detector simulation reproduces neutron response used a high-statistics antineutrino sample from the near detector. It finds an excess of simulated low-energy neutron candidates relative to data, feeding into the experiment's neutron-related systematic uncertainties.

Neutron detector response modeling in NOvA — arXiv
KM3NeT source stacking search

A stacking search for high-energy neutrino emission from ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and two classes of blazars used 336 days of data from a 19-21 detection-unit configuration of the ARCA detector. The analysis selected 1544 track-like neutrino candidates and modelled the infrared-galaxy sample of 75 sources with power-law spectra.

KM3NeT/ARCA stacking search for high-energy neutrino point sources: the case of … — arXiv
PandaX-4T quadruple beta limit

A search for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of xenon-136 used an exposure of 148.4 kg·yr collected during commissioning and the first science runs. No significant excess over background was observed, and the collaboration set a lower limit on the decay half-life of 6.01x10^24 years at 90% confidence — the most stringent constraint on this process in xenon.

Search for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of ¹³⁶Xe in PandaX-4T detector — arXiv

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • TAMBO, the Tau Air-shower Mountain-Based Observatory, is a proposed mountain-based neutrino observatory aimed at mapping the high-energy neutrino sky more completely than has been possible to date. A neutrino physicist would care because the concept is presented as a route both to filling gaps in that sky map and to searching for new physics.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This work presents the first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1,536 bins with 91 parameters. The combination, previously thought infeasible outside individual collaborations, finds no significant tension between datasets and prefers the normal mass ordering over the inverted at 3 sigma.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors derive global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass from neutrinoless double-beta decay searches using nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio from chiral effective field theory, within a Bayesian framework. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, the ab initio results suggest current-generation experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the target mass region.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a minimal dark SU(2) gauge symmetry that forbids the Standard Model Yukawa coupling of one right-handed neutrino-like fermion, producing a rank-two Dirac neutrino mass matrix with one exactly massless neutrino while remaining consistent with oscillation and cosmological data. Anomaly cancellation forces a specific, minimal dark-sector completion, making the scenario predictive.

    Minimal dark S U ( 2 ) origin of a massless Dirac neutrino — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors construct a radiative neutrino mass model in which a Dirac fermion dark matter candidate semi-annihilates into an anti-dark-matter particle and a neutrino, producing boosted dark matter in the present universe. They find this boosted component could be probed by DARWIN if the dark matter mass lies between 20 and 50 MeV and the semi-annihilation proceeds through a resonance with fine tuning, alongside a monochromatic neutrino signal.

    A radiative neutrino mass model with semi-annihilating boosted dark matter — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using a full many-body calculation rather than the usual mean-field treatment, the authors show that spin-flip probabilities between left- and right-handed neutrino states in dense neutrino environments can exceed mean-field estimates by orders of magnitude. This matters for astrophysical settings such as supernovae, where helicity conversion is normally modelled only at the mean-field level.

    Neutrino helicity oscillations in astrophysical environments: A many-body approach — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI
