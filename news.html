DUNE dominates the news this week: Fermilab, CERN and SURF have begun building the steel structure and cryostats for the far-detector modules a mile underground, and a new university has joined the collaboration. On the data side, IceCube reported a first measurement of how the neutrino flux varies along the Galactic plane, and the Askaryan Radio Array published its first array-wide search for ultrahigh-energy neutrinos, with no candidate events. Experiments and results

DUNE far detector

Construction of the detector modules has started nearly a mile underground in South Dakota. Crews from Fermilab, CERN and SURF are assembling large steel beams for the outer structure, and teams are beginning the cryostats that will house approximately 17,000 tons of liquid argon. A separate Fermilab report describes the Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility construction as entering its next phase, with critical components arriving.

DUNE collaboration and near detector

Black Hills State University became an institutional member of DUNE, which now counts more than 1,500 collaborators from 228 institutions in 38 countries and CERN. The collaboration also released a simulation-based sensitivity study of the near detector to eV-scale sterile neutrinos in the 3+1 framework. It uses three samples: highly elastic muon-neutrino and electron-neutrino charged-current events, and neutrino-electron elastic scattering, chosen to reduce interaction and flux uncertainties.

IceCube Galactic plane

IceCube presented the first measurement of the variation of the high-energy neutrino flux along the Galactic plane, using 10 years of shower data. The fit leaves both normalisation and spectral index free in several regions, independent of model expectations. The spectral indices are 2.76 (+0.18, −0.15) in the inner region and 3.13 (+0.57, −0.37) in the outer region. The collaboration also held its fall meeting in Madison with more than 230 participants.

Askaryan Radio Array

The ARA Collaboration published its first array-wide search for diffuse ultrahigh-energy neutrinos, using all five stations and 10.6 years of livetime, three times more than previous ARA searches. It found no events on an expected background of 0.13 ± 0.01 (stat.) (+0.05, −0.06) (syst.). The resulting upper limit is the most stringent from 50 EeV to 1000 EeV and gives the first constraints on the flux reported by KM3NeT. A companion paper details the analysis methodology.

Neutrino telescope network

A projection study considers X-ray-bright Seyfert galaxies as neutrino sources, using a network of IceCube, Baikal-GVD, KM3NeT/ARCA and P-ONE. In an AGN-corona emission model, it finds the Circinus Galaxy to be the most promising point-source target in the Southern sky. It would be detected at the 5σ level by 2038 at the latest, under assumptions about the Seyfert population's contribution to the diffuse flux.

NINJA FROST tracker

A meter-scale scintillator tracker with embedded scatterers, FROST, was built and operated in the NINJA experiment to match muon tracks between the nuclear emulsion detectors and the downstream muon detector. During J-PARC neutrino-beam operation it reached a data acquisition efficiency of 99.59% and a detection efficiency of 99.946 ± 0.006% for selected neutrino-induced muons well within the active area.

Quasisteady flavor configuration of multienergy neutrino ensembles

The authors present semianalytic approximations for the quasisteady outcome of slow and fast flavour conversion in quasihomogeneous neutrino ensembles with periodic boundary conditions. The ansatz agrees well with multiangle and multienergy solutions of the kinetic equations and does not depend on the mass ordering. It offers a possible subgrid scheme for supernova simulations that cannot include neutrino quantum kinetics.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem: any completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is exactly blind to a Majorana phase in two-flavour oscillations. A rate anisotropy can act as an environmental phase reference and restore sensitivity. This shows when decoherence could, and could not, reveal a Majorana phase.

Exploring anomaly-free dark photon models through solar neutrino-electron scattering in ton-scale dark matter detectors

The authors derive exclusion limits on six anomaly-free U(1)_X dark photon models, with kinetic and mass mixing, from solar neutrino-electron scattering in PandaX-4T and XENONnT.
