Experiments and results

Nobel Prize for IceCube

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced on 6 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen, principal investigator of IceCube. The citation reads "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin". IceCube's announcement says he proposed and proved that ice could be used as a detection medium, and KM3NeT congratulated him. Physics World also published a podcast on why neutrinos keep attracting Nobel prizes, with Kirsty Duffy as guest.

KM3NeT construction and ESFRI

KM3NeT reports two further marine-operation steps for ARCA and ORCA in the past week. At the ARCA deep-sea site, Alcatel Submarine Networks installed Cable Termination Frame 3 (CTF3). Separately, the collaboration announced that it has been recognised as a Landmark in the ESFRI Roadmap 2026.

SBND detector paper

The SBND Collaboration has released a paper describing the as-built detector and the performance of the first year of beam operation. SBND is a 112 tonne active-mass liquid argon time projection chamber in the Booster Neutrino Beam at Fermilab, and began operation in 2024. The collaboration reports that all detector systems performed at or above design requirements. The electron lifetime exceeded 30 ms, ten times the design requirement, for more than 95% of Run 1.

Short-Baseline Near Detector (SBND): Design and Initial Performance — arXiv
IceCube decoherence search

IceCube searched for quantum-gravity-induced decoherence of neutrino flavour oscillations using 368,071 atmospheric neutrinos between 0.5 and 100 TeV. The collaboration states this energy range is an order of magnitude higher than in any previous decoherence search. It finds no evidence for decoherence and sets what it describes as the strongest constraints to date on Planck-scale effects.

Planck-Scale Constraints on Quantum Gravity-Induced Neutrino Decoherence with IceCube — arXiv
Joint atmospheric fit

A combined analysis uses public atmospheric-neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande (all stages before the gadolinium phase), IceCube-DeepCore and the six-detection-unit configuration of KM3NeT/ORCA. All event samples are fitted simultaneously, with shared systematic uncertainties treated as fully correlated and detector-response uncertainties modelled independently per experiment. The title of the paper states that the combination gives evidence for normal neutrino ordering.

Evidence for Normal Neutrino Ordering with Combined Atmospheric Neutrino Experiments — arXiv
IceCube and HAWC data

IceCube published an article on using IceCube and HAWC data to investigate the centre of the Galaxy. The region contains dense gas and potential accelerators, including a supermassive black hole. Of particular interest are particles accelerated to petaelectronvolt energies, which the article describes as roughly 100 times higher than a reference scale given in the record.

Baikal-GVD point sources

Baikal-GVD is a cubic-kilometre-scale detector under construction in Lake Baikal. It has taken data in partial configurations since 2016 and is now 70% complete. A search for point-like astrophysical sources in a five-year track-like dataset from 2019-2024 found no significant source, so upper limits are given for selected candidate objects. These are compared with published results from IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT.

Search for Point-Like Neutrino Sources with Baikal-GVD — INSPIRE · DOI
T2K ND280 upgrade

The upgrade of the T2K near detector ND280 was completed in 2024, which the authors say has significantly enhanced its tracking capabilities. One paper proposes a neural network to distinguish electrons, photons, muons, pions and protons in the SuperFGD scintillator target. A second presents an event selection for muon-neutrino charged-current interactions with a single positively charged pion in the new modules. Both aim at constraining neutrino-interaction modelling, which the authors identify as the leading systematic uncertainty of T2K's oscillation analysis.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The authors compute two-neutrino double-beta decay rates with a unified formula that keeps the full interdependence between nuclear structure and lepton kinematics, instead of decoupling them. They compare rates and electron spectra with the closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion approximations for 82Se and 136Xe. Anyone interpreting double-beta spectra will want to know how far these standard approximations deviate.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, with a scalar quadruplet and two vectorlike fermion triplets. Neutrino masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop, allowing sub-eV masses with new physics at the TeV scale. It examines how leptogenesis in this setup differs from the canonical seesaw case.

    Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This work examines how scalar-mediated neutrino self-interactions would alter the diffuse supernova neutrino background as it propagates through the cosmic neutrino background. It uses a full three-flavour treatment that retains the complete PMNS structure. It matters for the interpretation of any future detection of this flux, since the interactions would leave spectral imprints.

    Diffuse Supernova Neutrinos with Secret Neutrino Interactions — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors study composite neutrino models in which heavy neutrinos are bound states of a near-conformal strongly coupled sector, mixing with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. Matching onto the low-energy theory gives transition dipole couplings of order 10^-6 to 10^-8 GeV^-1, which they state are parametrically larger than loop-level predictions of minimal Dirac or Majorana models. They also carry out an event-level simulation of the production and decay signature.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper investigates whether nuclear-recoil tracks in ancient minerals could record neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae. It varies neutron-star masses, includes failed supernovae that form black holes, and considers the role of the nuclear equation of state. The sensitivity enhancement is quantified for models with larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors consider magnetic and oscillation effects on neutrinos emitted by a protoneutron star in a supernova. They find that oscillations in the outer layers of the star harden the electron antineutrino spectrum significantly, most clearly for the inverted mass ordering. This is of interest because the spectral shape could carry mass-ordering information in a ground-based detection.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI
