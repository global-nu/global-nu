This week's neutrino news is dominated by null results that sharpen the picture: NOvA finds no evidence for sterile neutrinos in a new dual-baseline search, IceCube sees no counterpart to KM3NeT's ultra-high-energy event, and PandaX-4T tightens the limit on neutrinoless quadruple beta decay in xenon. On the infrastructure side, the NSF has renewed IceCube's operating funding through 2031, Fermilab is preparing new underwater detector tests in Minnesota, and DUNE continues preparations for automated data analysis and undergraduate outreach. Experiments and results

DUNE

DUNE researchers are developing AI-based automated triggers to sort through large volumes of detector data, identify neutrino interactions, flag possible stellar explosions and spot detector problems ahead of data-taking. Separately, Rice University's DUNE-TECH programme is introducing undergraduates to the science behind the experiment.

NOvA sterile search

NOvA has reported the first search for sterile-neutrino oscillations to combine neutrino- and antineutrino-mode beam data across both its near and far detectors, using exposures of 26.61×10^20 and 12.50×10^20 protons on target respectively. The collaboration finds no evidence for sterile neutrinos and excludes regions of parameter space allowed by previous experiments, including most of the region favoured by IceCube.

IceCube–KM3NeT follow-up

IceCube searched 15 years of its data for neutrino emission from the direction of KM3NeT's roughly 200 PeV event, the highest-energy neutrino yet reported, testing steady, flaring and arbitrary-time hypotheses. No evidence of emission was found, and IceCube set upper limits on neutrino output from that location.

PandaX-4T

PandaX-4T has searched for neutrinoless quadruple beta decay of xenon-136, a process whose observation without accompanying neutrinoless double beta decay would point to neutrinos being Dirac particles. Using an exposure of 148.4 kg·yr from its commissioning and first science runs, the collaboration saw no significant excess and set a lower limit on the decay half-life of 6.01×10^24 years at 90% confidence, the most stringent constraint on this process in xenon to date.

IceCube operations

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to manage and operate the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The five-year, $53 million agreement covers continued operation and maintenance of the detector for 2026-2031.

IceCube Upgrade

IceCube has reported projected sensitivities for the IceCube Upgrade, the enhanced in-ice instrumentation now being prepared. The study builds on the collaboration's evidence that active galaxies are among the sources of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos and likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration.

IceCube transient searches

IceCube has published a search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events, transient phenomena occurring on timescales of weeks, continuing the collaboration's hunt for sources of the high-energy astrophysical neutrinos first observed in 2013. A separate IceCube study looked for signatures of extra dimensions, motivated by the long-standing hierarchy problem between the weak force and gravity.

NOvA detector systematics

NOvA has published two technical studies aimed at reducing systematic uncertainty in its oscillation measurements: a report on the Test Beam experiment, which used a scaled-down detector at the Fermilab Test Beam Facility to calibrate detector response with data collected between 2019 and 2022, and a study of neutron detector response that finds an excess of simulated low-energy neutron candidates relative to data in its antineutrino near-detector sample.

Lake Vermilion detector

Fermilab is leading a new underwater neutrino detection experiment, with testing set to begin this autumn in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

JUNO information limits

A new study quantifies, using quantum and classical Fisher information, how precisely JUNO can determine oscillation parameters from the information encoded in reactor antineutrino states as it flows through to detected events. The analysis builds an information ladder for JUNO and finds that the loss of precision at each stage is strongly dependent on which oscillation parameter is being measured.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • This paper presents the first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1536 bins with 91 parameters. The unified model describes all four datasets without significant parameter tension and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance, a combination long considered infeasible outside the individual experimental collaborations.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors combine likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio via the in-medium similarity renormalization group, deriving global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate that the current generation of 0νββ experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the relevant mass range.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using a Geant4 Monte Carlo simulation of cosmic-ray protons, alpha particles and heavier nuclei interacting throughout a spherical molecular cloud, the authors compute radial energy deposition profiles and secondary particle fluxes for the first time with a full six-species nuclear composition. The result gives a more complete template for interpreting the gamma-ray and neutrino emission expected from cosmic-ray interactions with interstellar gas.

    Hadronic cosmic-ray interactions in molecular clouds: Gamma-ray, neutrino, and secondary … — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors study anomalous neutrino-two-photon interactions at a future muon-antimuon collider using a model-independent effective-field-theory approach, simulating the process via FeynRules, MadGraph5_aMC@NLO and Pythia8. The work explores how a future high-energy lepton collider could probe non-standard neutrino electromagnetic couplings that are hard to access with current facilities.

    Investigation of neutrino electromagnetic properties at muon antimuon collider — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors develop a time-dependent, multi-energy model of neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae, combining MeV neutrinos from the thermonuclear runaway with GeV neutrinos from hadronic interactions in nova-driven shocks. Using RS Ophiuchi as a benchmark, they show that the shock's interaction with both the red giant wind and a dense accretion disc around the white dwarf shapes the resulting multi-energy neutrino signal.

    Time-dependent multienergy neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae: The role of … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors assess prospects for detecting neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae via nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals, so-called paleodetectors, accounting for the diversity of neutron star masses, failed supernovae that form black holes, and the nuclear equation of state. Sensitivity is enhanced for models with more compact remnants, which release more of their gravitational energy as neutrinos.
