Experiments and results

Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment, the Lake Vermilion neutrino detector, is being led by Fermilab and will begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn. It continues Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
DUNE data triggers

DUNE researchers are developing AI systems to sort through the large volumes of data the experiment expects to collect, aiming to identify neutrino interactions, spot signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector automatically. This work is part of preparations for the experiment's deep-underground search for proton decay and neutrino oscillations.

US lab to hunt most elusive ‘ghost particles’ deep underground with automated triggers — Read it
IceCube Upgrade sensitivities

Since IceCube's first observation of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos in 2013, the collaboration has continued to hunt for their sources, finding evidence that active galaxies are likely sites of hadronic particle acceleration. A new report projects the sensitivities of the upcoming IceCube Upgrade for continuing this source-hunting programme.

Projected sensitivities of the IceCube Upgrade — Read it
IceCube operations renewed

The U.S. National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to manage and operate IceCube, covering a five-year, $53 million contract for 2026-2031.

The U.S. National Science Foundation renews NSF IceCube operation and maintenance contract — Read it
IceCube tidal disruption search

IceCube searched for high-energy neutrinos coincident with 89 tidal disruption events, selected using optical/UV and X-ray observations, applying time-dependent likelihood methods to look for emission from individual sources and from the population as a whole.

Search for Neutrinos from Tidal Disruption Events with IceCube — arXiv
KM3NeT/ARCA stacking search

The KM3NeT Collaboration carried out a stacking search for high-energy astrophysical neutrino emission from ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and two blazar categories, using 336 days of ARCA data taken with 19-21 detection units and 1544 selected track-like neutrino candidates. The infrared-galaxy sample comprised 75 sources modelled with conventional power-law spectra.

Super-Kamiokande proton decay limit

Using the full 401 kiloton-year exposure of its pure-water phase, Super-Kamiokande searched for proton decay into a charged antilepton plus an invisible massless particle and found no significant signal above the expected atmospheric-neutrino background. The resulting 90% confidence lower limits on the proton partial lifetime, 1.72×10^33 years for the positron channel and 0.61×10^33 years for the muon channel, improve on previous limits by factors of 2 and 1.5 respectively.

ARA diffuse UHE search

The Askaryan Radio Array reported its first array-wide diffuse search for ultra-high-energy neutrinos, combining data from all five autonomous South Pole stations taken between January 2013 and December 2023, the largest livetime accumulated by any in-ice radio array. The analysis used improved detector characterisation and updated neutrino interaction and propagation modelling.

The First Array-Wide Diffuse Flux Search for UHE Neutrinos with the Askaryan Radio Array — arXiv

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • Building on the ENUBET and NuTAG R&D programmes, this paper describes nuSCOPE, a proposed monitored and tagged neutrino beam for a short-baseline experiment at CERN. By tagging neutrino production and reconstructing neutrino energy event by event, it targets the GeV-scale cross-section uncertainties expected to be a leading systematic in next-generation oscillation experiments.

    nuSCOPE: A monitored and tagged neutrino beam — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Working in modified teleparallel gravity, the authors show that spacetime torsion alters both vacuum neutrino oscillations and the matter-enhanced MSW resonance. Fitting the model to solar neutrino data, they find the resulting changes to the survival probability could account for observational discrepancies without invoking new neutrino physics or non-standard interactions.

    Testing modified teleparallel gravity by neutrino oscillations — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using a Geant4 simulation of six cosmic-ray species interacting in a shell-structured molecular cloud, the authors compute, for the first time in this framework, the radial energy deposition and differential fluxes of all secondary particles produced, including neutrinos. This gives a self-consistent baseline for interpreting gamma-ray and neutrino emission from cosmic-ray-irradiated molecular clouds.

    Hadronic cosmic-ray interactions in molecular clouds: Gamma-ray, neutrino, and secondary … — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper computes, in the second Born approximation, the emission of a neutrino pair by a variable classical electromagnetic field and derives the effective photon-neutrino interaction Lagrangian for the first time. It also identifies a class of fields for which the effect vanishes, extending nonlinear QED-type processes to particles of vanishing mass.

    Emission of pairs of neutrinos by a variable field in the second Born approximation … — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using an effective-field-theory description of anomalous neutrino-two-photon couplings, the authors study the process μ⁻γ→μ⁻νν̄ at a future muon-antimuon collider, simulating signal and background with FeynRules, MadGraph5_aMC@NLO and Pythia 8. The result gives a concrete projection for how well such a collider could probe neutrino electromagnetic properties.

    Investigation of neutrino electromagnetic properties at muon antimuon collider — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Based on simulations of fallback accretion after core-collapse supernovae, the authors compute how progenitor mass, metallicity and explosion energy shape neutrino emission from neutrino-dominated accretion flows, then show that flavour conversion during propagation significantly modifies the resulting diffuse neutrino background spectrum. This changes predictions for how detectable this background component would be.
