In summary. This week's neutrino news is dominated by running experiments: IceCube reported new results on extra dimensions and tidal disruption events alongside projected sensitivities for its Upgrade and a renewed five-year NSF operations contract, while XENONnT announced the first solar-neutrino measurement at keV-scale recoil energies and Super-Kamiokande and KM3NeT published new searches for proton decay and astrophysical point sources respectively. Experiments and results

IceCube extra dimensions

A new analysis of 10.7 years of upward-going muon-neutrino data sets constraints on low-scale-gravity scenarios invoked to address the hierarchy problem, including large extra dimensions, by searching for matter-induced spectral distortions between 0.5 and 100 TeV. No such distortion was found, tightening bounds on the compactification scale. The result extends earlier collaboration work motivated by the long-standing puzzle of why gravity is so much weaker than the other forces.

IceCube Upgrade sensitivity

A densely instrumented multi-PMT extension to IceCube's DeepCore infill array, installed in 2025/2026 and due for commissioning by the end of 2026, is projected to sharpen sensitivity in the GeV regime for three analyses: neutrino transient searches, steady emission from point sources such as NGC 1068, and diffuse emission.

IceCube contract renewed

The US National Science Foundation has renewed its cooperative agreement with the University of Wisconsin–Madison to operate and maintain IceCube, covering 2026 to 2031 with $53 million in funding. The five-year agreement secures continued running of the South Pole observatory.

Lake Vermilion detector

Underwater neutrino detection testing is set to begin this autumn in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion, Minnesota, as part of the Fermilab-led Lake Vermilion neutrino detector experiment. The project continues Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Underwater neutrino testing in Lake Superior, Lake Vermilion to begin this fall — Read it
DUNE automated triggers

Ahead of data taking, DUNE researchers are developing AI systems intended to sort through large volumes of detector data, identify neutrino interactions, flag signs of a stellar explosion, and detect problems inside the detector itself. The work draws on machine-learning expertise built up at Fermilab as part of a broader push to integrate AI into the experiment's analysis chain.

XENONnT solar neutrinos

Using 2.46 tonne-years of exposure from its first two science runs, XENONnT reported the first measurement of solar neutrinos via elastic neutrino-electron scattering at keV-scale recoil energies, achieving the lowest energy threshold reached by any neutrino detection to date, and rejected the background-only hypothesis at 5.0 sigma. A subsequent comment on the result noted an omission in the collaboration's summary plot.

Super-K proton decay

Analysing the full 401 kiloton-year pure-water-phase exposure of Super-Kamiokande, a search for proton decay into a charged antilepton plus an invisible massless particle found no significant excess over the expected atmospheric-neutrino background. The null result sets new lower limits on the proton partial lifetime, improving on previous limits by factors of 2 for the positron channel and 1.5 for the muon channel.

KM3NeT source stacking

A stacking search for high-energy astrophysical neutrino emission from ultra-luminous infrared galaxies and two blazar populations was carried out with 336 days of KM3NeT/ARCA data from 19-21 detection units, yielding 1544 selected track-like neutrino candidates. The sample included 75 ultra-luminous infrared galaxies modelled with power-law spectra.

IceCube tidal disruption

A dedicated search combined optical, UV and X-ray selected samples of 89 tidal disruption events with time-dependent likelihood analysis, looking for coincident high-energy neutrino emission from individual sources and in a stacked sample. Tidal disruption events are theorised to produce neutrinos through photohadronic interactions between accelerated protons and multi-wavelength photons in the accretion disc and outflows.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • A Geant4 Monte Carlo study models cosmic-ray interactions in a molecular cloud using six primary nuclear species, computing for the first time within this framework the radial energy deposition, per-layer secondary production, and differential fluxes of all emerging secondaries, including neutrinos. This gives modellers of Galactic diffuse neutrino and gamma-ray backgrounds a more complete treatment of the underlying hadronic cascade than single-species estimates.

    Hadronic cosmic-ray interactions in molecular clouds: Gamma-ray, neutrino, and secondary … — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Working within a model-independent effective field theory, this study examines anomalous neutrino-two-photon interactions via the process mu-gamma to mu-nu-nubar at a future muon collider, parametrising the coupling with dimension-7 operators and simulating signal and background with FeynRules, MadGraph5_aMC@NLO and PYTHIA8. It offers a concrete projection for how well a muon collider could probe non-standard neutrino electromagnetic couplings.

    Investigation of neutrino electromagnetic properties at muon antimuon collider — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper computes, for the first time, the effective Lagrangian for photon-neutrino interaction and treats neutrino pair emission by a variable classical electromagnetic field in the second Born approximation, identifying a class of fields for which the effect vanishes. It extends nonlinear QED-type processes, previously studied for photons, to the neutrino sector.

    Emission of pairs of neutrinos by a variable field in the second Born approximation … — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Starting from fallback core-collapse-supernova simulations, this work models how progenitor mass, metallicity and explosion energy shape neutrino emission from neutrino-dominated accretion flows, and shows that flavour conversion during propagation significantly modifies the resulting diffuse NDAF neutrino background. The result matters for interpreting any diffuse supernova-related neutrino background at current or future detectors.

    Impact of neutrino flavour conversion on the diffuse neutrino background from … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Assuming the third neutrino mass state decays invisibly to a sterile state under the normal mass hierarchy, this study evaluates how well combined NOvA and DUNE data could constrain the decay parameter, finding that the true value of theta_23 significantly affects the achievable constraint, with a higher-octant value improving it. It gives current and near-term long-baseline experiments a concrete benchmark for invisible-decay sensitivity.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Combining results from multiple neutrinoless double-beta-decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed from ab initio chiral-effective-field-theory methods, this Bayesian analysis derives global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. In contrast to conventional nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that current-generation double-beta-decay experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the relevant mass range.
