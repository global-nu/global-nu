Experiments and results

Halzen Nobel Prize

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences has awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison. The citation reads "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin". CERN describes IceCube as having discovered high-energy neutrinos coming from far beyond our Solar System, and IceCube says Halzen proposed and proved that ice could be used as a detection medium.

Super-Kamiokande reactor antineutrinos

Super-Kamiokande has reported the first observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors in the detector. The analysis uses SK-Gd data, in which neutron capture on dissolved gadolinium improves sensitivity to low-energy electron antineutrinos, with a total exposure of 411.52 live days. The observed spectra and monthly rates are consistent with the oscillated reactor prediction after accounting for accidental, geoneutrino and spallation backgrounds. The no-reactor hypothesis is disfavoured at approximately 6σ.

PandaX-4T supernova background

This is the first search for the diffuse supernova neutrino background that uses coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering, here in the PandaX-4T liquid-xenon detector at the China Jinping Underground Laboratory. With a total exposure of 1.54 tonne·year from the commissioning and first science runs, no significant event excess over the expected background was seen. The upper limit on the flux per flavour of ν_x, for neutrino energies above 19.3 MeV, is (2.27–2.43)×10^3 cm^-2 s^-1 at 90% confidence level.

Baikal-GVD point sources

The cubic-kilometre-scale detector in Lake Baikal is under construction and is now 70% complete, having taken data in partial configurations since 2016. A search for point-like astrophysical sources in a five-year track-like dataset from 2019–2024 found no significant source. Upper limits are given for a selection of candidate objects and compared with published IceCube, ANTARES and KM3NeT results.

Search for Point-Like Neutrino Sources with Baikal-GVD — INSPIRE · DOI
T2K ND280 upgrade

The ND280 near-detector upgrade was completed in 2024 and has significantly enhanced its tracking capabilities. A new selection of muon-neutrino charged-current interactions with single positively charged pion production in the new modules is designed to maximise kinematic acceptance. The authors describe it as a step towards reducing the dominant systematic uncertainties from neutrino-nucleus interaction modelling in T2K's oscillation analysis.

JUNO and mass splittings

A single-author preprint takes the JUNO Collaboration's reported measurements of Δm²21 and Δm²31, described as the most precise to date, and tests an empirical relation between the two splittings that was proposed earlier. The author reports that the relation is in excellent agreement with the data and gives a value of 1.4143 with a relative precision of 0.27%. This is the author's analysis, not a JUNO result.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • The paper computes two-neutrino double-beta decay rates with the full interdependence of nuclear structure and lepton kinematics in a single formula, avoiding the usual decoupling approximations. It compares the resulting rates and electron spectra with the closure, non-closure and Taylor-expansion approaches for 82Se and 136Xe. Anyone interpreting double-beta-decay spectra should note that the traditional approximations deviate from this treatment.

    Direct calculation of the two-neutrino double-beta decay rate including the full … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors ask whether nuclear recoil tracks in ancient minerals could record neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae. They vary neutron-star masses and include failed supernovae that form black holes, and they consider the nuclear equation of state. The sensitivity of paleodetectors is found to be enhanced for models with larger neutrino emission, which are the more compact remnants.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper considers magnetic and oscillation effects on neutrinos emitted from a protoneutron star in a magnetorotational supernova. It finds that oscillations in the outer layers of the star harden the electron antineutrino spectrum significantly, most strongly for the inverted mass ordering. This matters for ground-based supernova detection and for any attempt to read the mass ordering from a future burst.

    Magnetorotational Supernova Neutrino Spectra Versus Mass Ordering — INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors build a time-dependent framework for neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae, covering MeV neutrinos from thermonuclear runaway and GeV neutrinos from hadronic interactions in nova-driven shocks. Using RS Oph as a benchmark, they model the shock interacting with both the red-giant wind and a dense accretion disk. It gives a concrete multi-energy signal model for a class of sources not usually treated in neutrino searches.

    Time-dependent multienergy neutrino emission from symbiotic recurrent novae: The role of … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The paper studies composite neutrino models in which heavy neutrinos are bound states of a near-conformal strongly coupled sector, mixing with Standard Model neutrinos through an inverse seesaw. Matching onto low-energy theory gives transition dipole operators with couplings of order 10^-6 to 10^-8 GeV^-1, parametrically larger than loop-level predictions of minimal Dirac or Majorana models. The authors carry out an event-level simulation of production and decay, which links the model to experimental searches.

    Neutrino dipole moments and radiative signatures from partial compositeness — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV. It discusses interpretations involving active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos, and the role of multimessenger constraints. It notes that, despite extensive follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been found.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI
