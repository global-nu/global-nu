Experiments and results

KM3NeT ARCA construction

A two-week sea campaign at the ARCA site produced a significant step forward in construction, enlarging both the submarine infrastructure and the detector itself. The collaboration marked the milestone by welcoming the newly added detection unit, ARCA-62.

DUNE cryostat steel

Crews at the Sanford Underground Research Facility have installed about 39% of the I-beam steel needed for the DUNE cryostat, close to two million pounds of steel now filling the 500-foot-long south cavern. DUNE was also included in a recent roundup of the most ambitious international science projects, cited as an example of how modern science transcends borders.

IceCube Upgrade infill

During the austral summer of 2025/26, five new strings equipped with new photosensor designs were deployed as a dense infill in the middle of the existing IceCube detector. The upgrade builds on IceCube's role in discovering the diffuse astrophysical neutrino flux and, more recently, neutrino emission from the Galactic plane.

IceCube source searches

Recent IceCube write-ups cover ongoing searches for the origin of high-energy neutrinos: a steady-state emission search using the DeepCore sub-array, building on the evidence reported four years ago for the active galaxy NGC 1068; a search for neutrino emission from tidal disruption events; and a study using IceCube data to probe the hierarchy problem through extra-dimensional physics.

T2K ND280 upgrade

The collaboration reports on the commissioning and performance of the new Time-of-Flight detector for the ND280 upgrade, comprising six panels of 118 plastic-scintillator bars read out by silicon photomultipliers. The detector is designed to reduce systematic uncertainties in oscillation measurements and improve sensitivity to the CP-violating phase.

MicroBooNE cross sections

The collaboration reported the first measurement of flux-integrated cross sections for electron-neutrino interactions on argon as a function of kinematic imbalance variables, using the experiment's full dataset of 1.3×10^21 protons on target from the Booster Neutrino Beam. These variables are sensitive to Fermi motion, multinucleon processes and final-state interactions in neutrino-nucleus scattering.

SHiP/NA67 detector

The SHiP/NA67 experiment at CERN's SPS is developing a detector system to search for feebly interacting GeV-scale particles and to perform all-flavour neutrino-physics measurements at the HI-ECN3 beam facility. The collaboration is currently optimising the initial configuration ahead of commissioning and first physics runs planned for 2032-2033.

The detector system of the SHiP/NA67 experiment at CERN — arXiv
Lake Vermilion detector

A new underwater neutrino detection experiment, led by Fermilab, is set to begin testing in Lake Superior and Lake Vermilion this autumn, continuing Minnesota's history of neutrino research.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • This paper presents the first combined oscillation analysis of atmospheric-neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore and KM3NeT/ORCA together with reactor data from Daya Bay, fitting 839,048 events across 1,536 bins with 91 parameters. The unified fit finds no significant tension between the datasets and prefers the normal over the inverted mass ordering at 3σ significance.

    Atmospheric neutrino oscillations: The full picture — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • The authors derive global limits on the Majorana neutrino mass by combining likelihoods from a suite of neutrinoless double-beta-decay experiments with nuclear matrix elements computed ab initio via the in-medium similarity renormalization group. In contrast to results based on nuclear models, the ab initio calculation indicates that current-generation experiments have likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the target mass regime.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This review summarises the properties of KM3-230213A, the ultra-high-energy neutrino event detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV, and discusses its possible astrophysical and cosmological origins. Despite extensive follow-up observations, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been identified.

    KM3-230213A: The Highest-Energy Cosmic Neutrino — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper introduces TAMBO, a proposed mountain-based, deep-valley neutrino observatory designed to help complete the map of the high-energy neutrino sky, a goal that has remained elusive a decade into the field of neutrino astronomy. The authors suggest the detector could also yield clues to physics beyond the Standard Model.

    Measuring the high-energy neutrino sky using the deep-valley neutrino observatory TAMBO — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This study examines the prospects for detecting neutrinos from past Galactic core-collapse supernovae via nuclear recoil tracks preserved in ancient minerals, accounting for the diversity of collapse outcomes including variations in neutron-star mass and failed supernovae that form black holes. Sensitivity is shown to depend on the nuclear equation of state and to improve for more compact remnants with larger neutrino emission.

    Paleodetectors for neutrino signals from diverse Galactic stellar collapses — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper studies constraints on invisible decay of the third neutrino mass state into a sterile state, combining the DUNE and NOvA long-baseline experiments under the normal mass hierarchy. The authors find that the true value of θ23 significantly affects the achievable constraint on the decay parameter, with the constraint improving when θ23 lies in the higher octant.

    Constraining invisible neutrino decay at NOνA and DUNE — INSPIRE · DOI
