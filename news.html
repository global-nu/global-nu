IceCube and the Nobel Prize

The Royal Swedish Academy of Science announced on 6 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen, IceCube principal investigator, for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. At the South Pole the winterover team marked the news with a group photo at the IceCube Lab. Detector operations continue: the week 42 report mentions a hard drive replacement, software updates and calibration operations on the Upgrade.

KM3-230213A

A new review summarises the properties of the ultra-high-energy event KM3-230213A, detected by KM3NeT with an inferred parent-neutrino energy of order 10^17 eV. It discusses interpretations involving active galactic nuclei, transient sources and cosmogenic neutrinos. Despite extensive follow-up, no unambiguous electromagnetic counterpart has been found.

CONUS+ reactor CEvNS

The CONUS+ collaboration reports the most precise measurement to date of reactor coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering on germanium, at the Leibstadt power plant. With an exposure of 1296 kg·days reactor-on, it finds 1555 ± 152 events at 10.2σ, against a Standard Model expectation of 1701 ± 198. A companion paper describes the run 2 analysis, after three of the original 1 kg detectors were replaced with 2.42 kg diodes.

SBND first results

SBND presents its first neutrino-argon cross-section measurement, for muon-neutrino charged-current interactions with a single proton and no pions, as a function of transverse kinematic imbalance variables. Using a Run 1 subset of 8.8×10^19 protons on target, it selects 12,804 signal candidates with 91% purity. The measurement discriminates strongly among event-generator predictions. Separately, a search for neutrino-produced long-lived particles used the Cosmic Ray Tagger Beam Telescope.

TAMBO

TAMBO aims to detect tau neutrinos from 1 PeV to 1000 PeV using up to 5,000 particle detectors on the face of a steep hill in the Colca valley. This range lies between the reach of optical-Cherenkov and radio-based experiments. A proceedings contribution presents the project and the TAMBITO prototype.

nEXO backgrounds

A simulation study estimates the cosmogenic background rate for nEXO, a five-tonne liquid-xenon time projection chamber for neutrinoless double beta decay in 136Xe, at its preferred host site, SNOLAB. Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper computes the neutrino emissivity of Δ-direct-Urca processes in neutron-star matter containing Δ isobars. Urca processes with nucleons and hyperons are established dominant cooling mechanisms, so this contribution had not been explicitly evaluated before. It matters for thermal evolution models of massive neutron stars.

Toward measuring the low-energy cosmic-ray spectrum with next-generation neutrino detectors

The authors study how upcoming kiloton-scale detectors such as Hyper-Kamiokande could constrain the primary cosmic-ray spectrum through atmospheric neutrinos. They find that future detectors can distinguish between cosmic-ray models with high statistical significance, even allowing for cross-section and hadronic-interaction uncertainties. This links atmospheric-neutrino measurements to cosmic-ray physics.

The paper sets limits on anomaly-free U(1)_X dark photon models with kinetic and mass mixing, using solar neutrino-electron scattering data from PandaX-4T and XENONnT. When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves that any completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is exactly blind to a Majorana phase in two-flavour oscillations. A rate anisotropy in the environment can supply a phase reference. It sets a clear condition for when decoherence could reveal a Majorana phase.

Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass

The authors study neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, where a scalar quadruplet and vectorlike fermion triplets generate masses through a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. Sub-eV masses arise even for a new-physics scale of order TeV. The paper examines whether thermal leptogenesis is viable in this setting.

Comparative periodogram analysis of 22 years of Super-Kamiokande solar ⁸B neutrino data: classical, phase-based, and information theoretic methods

The authors analyse 22 years of Super-Kamiokande solar 8B neutrino data with nine periodogram methods, looking for periodic signals. They find the Generalized Lomb-Scargle method the most statistically robust because it handles heteroscedastic uncertainties correctly. The comparison is useful to anyone searching for time modulation in solar neutrino rates.
