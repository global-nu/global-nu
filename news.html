Experiments and results

SBND first results

The Short-Baseline Near Detector released its first neutrino-argon cross-section measurement, described by Fermilab as improving neutrino interaction modelling in preparation for DUNE. The analysis uses muon-neutrino charged-current events with a single proton and no pions, measured as functions of transverse kinematic imbalance variables. A subset of Run 1 data corresponding to 8.8×10^19 protons on target yields 12,804 signal candidates at 91% purity. The collaboration reports that the cross sections discriminate strongly among event-generator predictions.

Super-Kamiokande DSNB

Super-Kamiokande reports an indication of electron antineutrinos from the diffuse supernova neutrino background, from a spectral fit to a 5003.0-day dataset. The analysis improves atmospheric-neutrino background rejection, the systematic uncertainty evaluation and the spectral fitting method compared with earlier searches. With a representative model the best-fit flux is 3.6 (+1.6, -1.5) cm^-2 s^-1 for E>13.3 MeV, a 2.6σ tension with the null hypothesis. Signal models with different spectral shapes give similar significances.

CONUS+ reactor CEvNS

CONUS+ reports what it calls the most precise measurement to date of reactor-antineutrino coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering, using germanium detectors at the Leibstadt nuclear power plant. With 1296 kg·days of reactor-on exposure, a blind likelihood analysis gives 1555 ± 152 events at 10.2σ. This agrees with the Standard Model expectation of 1701 ± 198 events within 0.6 standard deviations. The result gives sin²θ_W = 0.225 ± 0.022 at a momentum transfer of about 10 MeV. A companion paper presents the run 2 detector performance, background model and signal extraction.

DUNE construction

Fermilab reports that construction of the Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility is entering its next phase, with critical components arriving and partner institutions contributing. TAMBO project

The Tau Air-Shower Mountain-Based Observatory is designed to detect tau neutrinos from 1 PeV to 1,000 PeV, an energy range where backgrounds are largely suppressed. This range lies between the reach of optical-Cherenkov and radio-based experiments. The design uses up to 5,000 particle detectors installed on the face of a steep hill. A contribution on the TAMBITO prototype describes the project.

Neutrino Emissivity in Δ -Direct-Urca Processes Inside Neutron Stars

The paper computes the neutrino emissivity of Δ-direct-Urca processes in neutron-star matter, a contribution the authors say had not been explicitly evaluated before. It matters for neutrino cooling of massive neutron stars if Δ isobars are present in the dense core, alongside the nucleonic and hyperonic Urca processes.

Toward measuring the low-energy cosmic-ray spectrum with next-generation neutrino detectors

The authors assess whether kiloton-scale detectors such as Hyper-Kamiokande could determine the primary cosmic-ray spectrum from the atmospheric neutrino flux. They find that future detectors can distinguish between cosmic-ray models with high statistical significance, even allowing for cross-section and hadronic-interaction uncertainties. They also introduce a technique for reconstructing the primary spectrum.

When can decoherence reveal a Majorana phase? A phase-covariance no-go theorem for two-flavor neutrino oscillations

The paper proves a no-go theorem for observing a Majorana phase in open two-flavour neutrino oscillations. Every completely positive trace-preserving evolution covariant under rotations of the mass-coherence plane is exactly phase blind. A rate anisotropy between the two transverse decoherence rates can provide an environmental phase reference.

Thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model of neutrino mass

The authors study neutrino mass and thermal leptogenesis in the Babu-Nandi-Tavartkiladze model, in which masses arise from a dimension-7 operator at tree level and a dimension-5 operator at one loop. The model gives sub-eV neutrino masses with new physics at the TeV scale. The paper tests whether thermal leptogenesis works, noting that it differs from canonical seesaw leptogenesis.

Comparative periodogram analysis of 22 years of Super-Kamiokande solar ⁸B neutrino data: classical, phase-based, and information theoretic methods

The authors apply nine periodogram methods to 22 years of Super-Kamiokande solar 8B neutrino data, using temporal segmentation to separate astrophysical signals from detector systematics. They conclude that the Generalized Lomb-Scargle method gives the most statistically robust detections because it handles heteroscedastic uncertainties correctly. This is relevant to anyone searching for periodic modulations in solar neutrino rates.
