Experiments and results

DUNE detector stress test

DUNE scientists tested the limits of a prototype detector by subjecting it to an extreme 300,000-volt stress trial. The test took place before the detector is installed deep underground in the United States.

300,000 volts: US team puts world’s largest neutrino detector through extreme stress test — Read it
DUNE data-analysis AI

DUNE researchers are preparing for data analysis by developing AI systems to sort through large volumes of data, identify neutrino interactions, and spot signs of a stellar explosion. These automated-trigger systems, which can also detect problems inside the detector, build on decades of machine-learning work at Fermilab.

DUNE Neutrino Day

SURF and partner organisations hosted the 18th annual Neutrino Day festival in Lead, South Dakota, with Fermilab as a sponsor. The event included an interactive underground livestream broadcast, in which South Dakota Public Broadcasting spoke with SURF laboratory director Mike Headley and DUNE senior scientist Sowjanya Gollapinni.

IceCube probes Earth's interior

Atmospheric neutrinos, produced in cosmic-ray-induced air showers in the Earth's atmosphere and detected by IceCube, are being used to probe the properties of the Earth's interior. This complements indirect methods such as seismic and gravitational measurements, which have left many questions about the Earth's internal structure unanswered.

Probing the Earth’s interior using atmospheric neutrinos — Read it
IceCube's WavePID tool

When a neutrino interacts with a molecule of ice, it produces secondary particles that emit blue light, which IceCube detects. From the shape and nanosecond-level timing of that light pattern, a new tool called WavePID identifies the neutrino interaction.

WavePID: A tool that identifies neutrinos at the nanosecond level — Read it
IceCube Upgrade calibrations

IceCube's winterover team at the South Pole carried out routine detector maintenance and continued calibrations for the IceCube Upgrade. The week was also unusually cold, with temperatures reaching levels not seen since 2018.

Week 29 at the Pole — Read it
NOvA hydrogen scattering

The NOvA collaboration measured the total cross section for muon antineutrino charged-current quasielastic scattering on hydrogen using its near detector, selecting 35,509 signal events from a 1.2×10^21 proton-on-target exposure in the NuMI beam. Backgrounds from heavier nuclei were constrained with dedicated control samples, yielding the highest-statistics sample of antineutrino-hydrogen interactions measured to date.

TAMBO observatory

TAMBO, the Tau Air-shower Mountain-Based Observatory, is a new mountain-based neutrino observatory intended to help map the high-energy neutrino sky. The field of neutrino astronomy has advanced over the past decade, but physicists have struggled to fully map that sky, which TAMBO aims to address.

Theory highlights

recently published, with the links each record carries

  • Combining results from multiple neutrinoless double-beta decay experiments with ab initio nuclear matrix elements derived from chiral effective field theory, this analysis sets global Bayesian limits on the Majorana neutrino mass. Unlike calculations based on other nuclear models, the ab initio results indicate that the current generation of 0νββ experiments has likely not yet reached the sensitivity needed to probe the mass region of interest.

    Global ab initio neutrino mass limits from neutrinoless double-beta decay — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Working within R-parity-violating supersymmetry with comparatively light third-generation sleptons, this paper derives the neutrino non-standard interactions that arise from LLE-type couplings and checks the resulting parameter space against a broad set of experimental constraints. It finds that percent-level NSI can be realised in some channels, with the largest magnitudes reaching about 8% for ϵee and 3.8% for ϵμμ, while other entries stay near or below the sub-percent level.

    Neutrino nonstandard interactions from \(LLE\)-type \(R\)-parity violation — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper proposes a secluded dark-matter model built around an extra U(1) gauge symmetry, in which a Dirac dark-matter particle, heavy neutrinos and a singlet scalar are linked so that the same Yukawa structures generating light neutrino masses also fix the scalar-Higgs mixing angle at one loop. The construction ties an open dark-matter puzzle directly to the mechanism of neutrino mass generation.

    Neutrino mass and secluded dark matter puzzles solved together — INSPIRE · DOI

  • Using the GENIE event generator, this study benchmarks state-of-the-art theoretical and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon scattering against recent MicroBooNE charged-current measurements. The comparison finds strong separation between competing models in some cases, giving upcoming argon-based experiments a clearer basis for choosing interaction models in their simulations.

    Benchmarking state-of-the-art theory and empirical models of pionless neutrino-argon … — arXiv · INSPIRE · DOI

  • Revisiting the common assumption that Galactic cosmic-ray spectra have the same shape everywhere in the Milky Way, this paper examines how a more realistic, non-factorised spatial distribution of cosmic rays changes predictions for the diffuse Galactic gamma-ray and neutrino fluxes. The result bears on how IceCube's Galactic-plane neutrino signal is interpreted against models of cosmic-ray propagation.

    Cosmic-ray spatial distribution and diffuse gamma-ray and neutrino fluxes — INSPIRE · DOI

  • This paper compares two different formalisms for implementing quantum decoherence in neutrino oscillation probabilities, defining the decoherence matrix in either the matter or the vacuum mass-eigenstate basis, and evaluates how each affects sensitivity projections for the future DUNE and P2SO long-baseline experiments. The two formalisms are shown to give different results at the probability level, which matters for how future long-baseline data are used to constrain decoherence.
